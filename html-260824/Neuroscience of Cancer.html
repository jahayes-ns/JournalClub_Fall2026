The neuroscience of cancer

Mancusi1, Monje1,2 | 2023

Final Summary

This scientific paper explores the intricate interactions between neurons and gliomas in the central nervous system, focusing on brain tumors. The research question revolves around understanding how neuronal activity promotes glioma growth, initiation, and progression, as well as the reverse impact of gliomas on neural circuits.

Methods include studying electrochemical communication between neurons and gliomas, paracrine growth factor secretion (such as NLGN3, IGF-1, and BDNF), and synaptogenic protein secretion by gliomas that affect neuronal activity. The study also considers the influence of sensory experiences like vision and olfaction on glioma initiation and growth.

Key findings suggest that visual experience can contribute to low-grade gliomas in the optic pathway, while odorants stimulating olfactory receptor neurons influence high-grade glioma initiation and growth in the olfactory bulb. Gliomas can also induce neuronal hyperexcitability and functional remodeling of neural circuits within the tumor microenvironment, further amplifying glioma growth.

Conclusions emphasize the complexity of these interactions, which could lead to novel strategies for treating brain tumors. The study also discusses the role of neurotransmission and neuron-glial interactions in various types of cancer, including gastric and intestinal cancers, oral squamous cell carcinomas, and pancreatic cancers. The literature cited provides evidence for the complex interplay between nerves and cancer development, with implications for disease progression, treatment strategies, and prognosis.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The combined preamble of the scientific paper discusses the intricate interactions between neurons and gliomas in the central nervous system, particularly within the brain. Neuronal activity promotes the development, growth, and progression of gliomas by stimulating the secretion of certain paracrine growth factors like NLGN3, IGF-1, and BDNF, which encourage glioma cell proliferation. Electrochemical communication between neurons and gliomas, facilitated by synapses and potassium-evoked currents, can amplify and synchronize depolarizing currents in tumor cell networks, fostering glioma growth.

Sensory experiences such as vision and olfaction can influence glioma initiation and growth by regulating neuronal activity. Visual experience-induced stimulation of retinal ganglion cells promotes shedding of NLGN3, contributing to low-grade gliomas in the optic pathway associated with NF1 tumor predisposition syndrome. Odorants that stimulate olfactory receptor neurons induce IGF-1 secretion from mitral/tufted cells, influencing high-grade glioma initiation and growth in the olfactory bulb.

Gliomas can also impact neural circuits by secreting factors like glutamate and synaptogenic proteins, which promote neuronal hyperexcitability and functional remodeling of neural circuits in the tumor microenvironment. This enhanced neuronal activity can further amplify glioma growth through activity-regulated mechanisms.

Understanding these complex interactions between neurons and gliomas could lead to novel strategies for treating brain tumors. The text also discusses the role of neurotransmission and neuron-glial interactions in various types of cancer, such as gastric and intestinal cancers, oral squamous cell carcinomas, and pancreatic cancers.

The literature cited includes papers on synapses between neurons and oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs), myelination, glioblastoma, developmental and oncogenic programs in H3K27M gliomas, and the impact of neurotransmitters like adrenaline, serotonin, GABA, and glutamate on cancer progression. The studies highlight the complex interplay between nerves and cancer development, with implications for understanding disease progression, treatment strategies, and prognosis.
