VCO2: The Essential Underpinning to Homeostatic Control of Breathing

Dempsey, McCrimmon

Final Summary

Title: CO2, VCO2, and Breathing: Regulation and Adaptation

This study, authored by Jerome A. Dempsey and Donald R. McCrimmon, investigates the role of carbon dioxide (CO2) in regulating alveolar ventilation and maintaining arterial partial pressure of CO2 (PaCO2) homeostasis during exercise. The research was conducted at the John Rankin Laboratory of Pulmonary Medicine (University of Wisconsin School of Medicine and Public Health) and the Department of Neuroscience (Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine).

The study suggests that pulmonary sites, muscle afferents sensing blood flow as a metabolic surrogate, and the reticular formation (RTN) of the brainstem play crucial roles in controlling alveolar ventilation during exercise. Adaptive control or associative learning may encode CO2 proportionality in ventilation regulation. However, further investigation is needed to better understand these mechanisms and their roles in regulating ventilation.

The research also reveals a critical dependence of central CO2 chemosensitivity on carotid chemoreceptors, suggesting that hyperoxic CO2 response tests in intact animals or humans may not solely measure central chemoreceptor sensitivity but also include a substantial contribution from carotid chemoreceptors.

Additionally, the paper discusses cardiorespiratory changes during steady-state treadmill exercise, age-matched healthy vs. patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and adaptation of the V̇A:V̇CO2 response to altered resting PaCO2 and plant gain. The results demonstrate the substantial adaptability of the V̇A:V̇CO2 linkage in the face of changing resting PaCO2 and plant gain, independently of any change in ventilatory responsiveness to inspired PCO2 or in PaCO2/H+.

The authors acknowledge valuable contributions from various professors and researchers, as well as gratitude towards Moreira, two anonymous reviewers, and the late Professor Brian Whipp for championing the significance of respiratory CO2 exchange in ventilatory control.

ABSTRACT

This scientific article explores the role of carbon dioxide (CO2) in controlling alveolar ventilation and maintaining arterial partial pressure of CO2 (PaCO2) homeostasis, even without direct chemoreceptor CO2 sensing or changes in sensitivity to inspired CO2.

The article suggests that during exercise, respiratory CO2 exchange plays a crucial role in controlling alveolar ventilation, primarily through mechanisms such as pulmonary sites like blood vessel and airway receptors, muscle afferents sensing blood flow as a metabolic surrogate, and the reticular formation (RTN) of the brainstem integrating various sensory inputs.

Adaptive control or associative learning is proposed as a means for encoding CO2 proportionality in ventilation regulation. However, the authors emphasize the need for further investigation to better understand these mechanisms and their roles in regulating ventilation.
