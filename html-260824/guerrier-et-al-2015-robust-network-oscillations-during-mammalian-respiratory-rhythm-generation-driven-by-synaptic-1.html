Robust network oscillations during mammalian respiratory rhythm generation driven by synaptic dynamics

Guerriera, Hayes, Fortin, Holcman | 2015

Keywords: central pattern generator, synaptic depression, mathematical modeling, numerical simulations, breathing

Final Summary

This scientific paper explores the generation of synchronous oscillations in the preBötzinger complex (preBötC), a crucial region responsible for the eupneic respiratory rhythm in mammals. The study employs a computational approach to model a randomly connected network of neurons that rely on short-term synaptic facilitation (SF) and depression (SD).

Through reduced inhibitory activities and repetitive stimulation, the researchers found that SF/SD occurs at preBötC synapses on timescales influencing rhythmic population activity. Key findings include the importance of synaptic connections in burst induction, the ability of a few active neurons to trigger population bursts supporting the group-pacemaker hypothesis, and the simulation of burstlets reminiscent of patterns observed experimentally.

The study's computational model was validated against experimental conditions and demonstrated that nondeterministic neuronal spiking and dynamic synaptic strengths in a randomly connected network are sufficient to generate regular respiratory-like rhythmic network activity, potentially playing an important role in generating the rhythm for breathing and other coordinated motor activities in mammals. The research was supported by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and provides insights into neural networks, network dynamics, and central pattern generators for various motor behaviors.

PREAMBLE

The preamble of the scientific paper discusses the generation of synchronous oscillations in neuronal networks, specifically focusing on the preBötzinger complex (preBötC) which is responsible for generating the eupneic respiratory rhythm in mammals. The researchers use a computational approach to model a randomly connected network of neurons that rely on short-term synaptic facilitation (SF) and depression (SD). They demonstrate that synaptic fluctuations can initiate population activities through recurrent excitation, allowing the network activity to synchronize (bursts) and generate a population refractory period (silence). The model was validated against experimental conditions, revealing that SF/SD occurs at preBötC synapses on timescales that influence rhythmic population activity. The study suggests that nondeterministic neuronal spiking and dynamic synaptic strengths in a randomly connected network are sufficient to give rise to regular respiratory-like rhythmic network activity, potentially playing an important role in generating the rhythm for breathing and other coordinated motor activities in mammals. RESULTS

The study by Guerrier et al. investigates the synapse dynamics in the preBötC, a crucial region for respiratory rhythm generation. The research was conducted under reduced inhibitory activities to highlight glutamatergic transmission. Stimulation involved repetitive (10–30 Hz) trains of varying intensity.

Results showed that at 20 and 30 Hz, stimulation often led to population recruitment in seven neurons. The amplitude of evoked EPSCs varied during stimulus trains, showing a waxing phase followed by a waning phase, with transmission failures reoccurring at the end of subsequent trains.

Normalizing the eEPSC amplitudes revealed an exponential trend, suggesting the presence of short-term synaptic facilitation (SF) in the rising component and short-term synaptic depression (SD) in the falling component. These findings support the applicability of the modeling approach for understanding the dynamics of the respiratory rhythm-generating network in vitro.

The study's key findings include the importance of synaptic connections in burst induction, the ability of a few active neurons to trigger population bursts (supporting the group-pacemaker hypothesis), and the simulation of burstlets reminiscent of patterns of activity observed experimentally. The results were validated against experimental data from previous studies.

DISCUSSION

This scientific paper focuses on a study that simulates neuronal network behavior in the preBötzinger complex (preBötC), responsible for respiratory rhythm generation in the brainstem. The researchers used a simplified Hodgkin-Huxley model and a two-pool synaptic model to create a 400-neuron network, demonstrating that bursting oscillations can be generated without pacemaker neurons.

Key findings from the research include:

  1. Identification of respiratory pacemaker neurons within the preBötC.
  2. Characterization of various inspiratory pacemaker neurons with distinct biophysical properties and roles.
  3. Evidence for a group-pacemaker mechanism involving synaptically activated burst-generating conductances in mice.
  4. Investigation into the role of inhibition in respiratory pattern generation, including glycinergic interneurons and other inhibitory mechanisms.
  5. Analysis of dendritic calcium activity as a precursor to inspiratory bursts in PBC neurons.
  6. Examination of outward currents contributing to inspiratory burst termination in PBC neurons.
  7. Mathematical modeling and simulations for better understanding of neural dynamics underlying respiratory rhythm generation and network behavior.
  8. Emphasis on the importance of understanding complex interactions between ionic currents, neurotransmitter receptors, and synaptic connections in the preBötC for fully elucidating respiratory rhythmogenesis.

The research was supported by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and other institutions. The findings may provide insights into neural networks, network dynamics, and central pattern generators for various motor behaviors.
