Hindbrain neurons that underlie water discrimination and consumption

Yamada, Nomura, Suematsu, Taruno | 2026

Final Summary

Title: Identification of a central neuronal substrate underlying water perception and consumption

Research question: This study aimed to identify the specific neuronal population in the hindbrain (wPBN Oxtr) that conveys sensory signals associated with the perception of ingested water and promotes drinking behavior in mammals.

Methods: The authors used a combination of recombinant viral vectors, stereotaxic surgery, optogenetic manipulation, behavioral tests, calcium imaging, histology, and RNAscope to explore the role of oxytocin receptor (OXTR) expressing neurons in the posterior part of the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (PBN).

Key findings: Activation of wPBN Oxtr neurons during water ingestion plays a crucial role in water consumption and discrimination. Inhibition of these neurons during drinking leads to immediate cessation of water intake, suggesting their importance in this behavior. Silencing the same population also impairs the ability to discriminate water from other liquids but does not affect water-seeking behavior.

Conclusions: This study highlights a central neuronal substrate underlying the perception of water and contributes to our understanding of the neural basis for hydration. The findings reveal the opposing roles of wPBN and dlPBN Oxtr neurons in controlling water intake, with wPBN Oxtr neurons facilitating drinking behavior while dlPBN inhibits it. Further research is needed to identify the specific subpopulation responsible for water perception and determine the specific water-related properties that activate these neurons.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The paper by Yu Yamada et al. identifies a specific neuronal population in the hindbrain, called wPBN Oxtr, that is activated during water ingestion and plays a crucial role in water consumption and discrimination. These neurons respond selectively to aqueous liquids, not non-aqueous ones. Inhibition of these neurons during drinking leads to immediate cessation of water intake, suggesting their importance in this behavior. Silencing the same population also impairs the ability to discriminate water from other liquids but does not affect water-seeking behavior. This study highlights a central neuronal substrate underlying the perception of water and contributes to our understanding of the neural basis for hydration.

INTRODUCTION

This scientific paper introduces a study aimed at identifying neuronal populations that convey sensory signals associated with the perception of ingested water and promote drinking behavior in mammals. The introduction highlights the importance of water regulation for survival and discusses how external and internal sensory signals control homeostatic water drinking behavior. It mentions the increasing understanding of neural mechanisms governing other homeostatic behaviors like eating, particularly taste and hunger, but notes that the neural substrates supporting drinking behavior in response to water sensation are still unclear.

The paper then discusses the selective attraction of animals to aqueous liquids, suggesting they rely on sensory cues generated during water ingestion. Responses to water ingestion have been observed in specific brain areas like the nucleus of the solitary tract (NST) and the parabrachial nucleus (PBN), which are extensively interconnected. The PBN, a central hub for coordinating homeostatic behaviors, integrates various sensory inputs and projects to several brain regions.

Within the PBN, four genetically distinct neuronal populations have been reported as responsive to water ingestion, but none promotes water intake. Three of these populations (oxy-tocin receiver-expressing, prodynorphin-expressing, and cholecystokinin-expressing neurons in the dorsolateral subdivision, and SATB homeobox 2-expressing neurons in the ventrolateral and medial subdivisions) are involved in satiety signaling or feedback-driven termination of fluid intake. As a result, the neuronal population conveying sensory signals underlying the perception of ingested water and promoting drinking behavior remains unidentified, limiting our understanding of the neural basis of water consumption. In this study, researchers aim to identify such neurons.

RESULTS

The study examines the role of Oxtr neurons in the posterior bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (PBN) in regulating water intake. Key findings include:

  1. Dehydrated mice with ablated BN showed significantly higher total lick counts compared to controls.
  2. Food intake was not affected by BN ablation.
  3. Optogenetic experiments were conducted on Vglut2-Cre mice, targeting the wPBN specifically. Light stimulation did not evoke aversiveness nor affect general appetitive behaviors, suggesting that wPBN Oxtr neurons play a role in sustaining water drinking behavior.
  4. Two groups of mice were created with AAV vectors to inhibit wPBN and dlPBN Oxtr neurons. The results indicate opposing roles for these neurons in controlling water intake: facilitative (wPBN) and inhibitory (dlPBN). This study suggests that activity of wPBN Oxtr underlies the ability of animals to discriminate water.

The study employed in vivo calcium imaging, optogenetic techniques, and various statistical tests such as Mann-Whitney U test, unpaired t-test, and figures are presented but not fully cited due to the text being incomplete.

DISCUSSION

The discussed scientific paper explores the role of central neurons expressing the oxytocin receptor (OXTR) in the posterior part of the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (PBN) in water perception and consumption for homeostatic regulation. Activation of these neurons occurs quickly after lick onset, suggesting they are involved in tasting or gulping rather than later processes like gastric loading and absorption.

Bitter taste suppresses their responses, but the underlying mechanism is unknown. The physiological roles of centrally released oxytocin and vasopressin in regulating water intake remain unclear. Further investigation, including circuit-selective manipulation strategies, is needed to identify the specific subpopulation responsible for water perception, as well as to determine the specific water-related properties that activate these neurons.

The study employs various methods such as recombinant viral vectors, stereotaxic surgery, optogenetic manipulation, behavioral tests, calcium imaging, histology, and RNAscope. Data analysis for calcium imaging was performed, with statistical analysis conducted to evaluate results.
