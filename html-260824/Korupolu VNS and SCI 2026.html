Safety and feasibility of paired vagus nerve stimulation with rehabilitation for improving upper extremity function in people with cervical spinal cord injury: a pilot randomized controlled trial

Korupolu, Yozbatiran, Miller, Shields, Tandon | 2026

Final Summary

This scientific paper presents a pilot randomized controlled trial evaluating the safety, feasibility, and preliminary efficacy of pairing vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) with rehabilitation for improving upper extremity function in individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury (SCI). The study enrolled six participants who underwent implantation of a VNS device and were randomly assigned to receive active VNS paired with rehabilitation or sham VNS paired with rehabilitation.

The trial demonstrated that the intervention was safe, feasible, and well-tolerated. At immediate post-intervention, all participants receiving active VNS showed mild-to-moderate improvements in upper extremity motor function, while no improvements were observed in the sham group. At 90 days, two active VNS participants sustained these gains. Participants receiving active VNS also reported subjective improvements in coordination, movement quality, and daily task performance.

These findings suggest that further investigation in larger trials is warranted to rigorously assess the efficacy, durability, and optimal dosing parameters of pairing invasive VNS with upper extremity rehabilitation for individuals with chronic cervical SCI. The trial extends the established safety profile of invasive VNS to the SCI population and provides initial data for future research on the effectiveness and optimal implementation of VNS paired with rehabilitation in spinal cord injury.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This is a pilot randomized controlled trial evaluating the safety and feasibility of pairing vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) with rehabilitation for improving upper extremity function in individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury. The study, conducted at the University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston, enrolled six participants who underwent implantation of a VNS device and were randomly assigned to receive active VNS paired with rehabilitation or sham VNS paired with rehabilitation. All participants completed 18 in-clinic rehabilitation sessions over 6–8 weeks, followed by a 90-day home-based exercise program.

The trial demonstrated that the intervention was safe, feasible, and well-tolerated. At immediate post-intervention, all participants receiving active VNS showed mild-to-moderate improvements in upper extremity motor function, while no improvements were observed in the sham group. At 90 days, two active VNS participants sustained these gains. Participants receiving active VNS also reported subjective improvements in coordination, movement quality, and daily task performance.

These findings suggest that further investigation in larger trials is warranted to rigorously assess the efficacy, durability, and optimal dosing parameters of pairing invasive VNS with upper extremity rehabilitation for individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, identifier NCT05601661.

INTRODUCTION

The paper presents an introduction to a study focused on vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) paired with rehabilitation for individuals with traumatic spinal cord injuries, particularly those affecting the cervical region leading to tetraplegia. The global annual incidence of such injuries is estimated at 768,473 new cases, with over 50% resulting in cervical spinal cord injury (SCI).

The study aims to improve upper extremity function after SCI as it significantly impacts independence and quality of life. Various rehabilitation methods are currently used, but recovery is challenging. Neuroplasticity within spared neural pathways above and below the level of injury plays a critical role in neurological recovery following SCI.

The study investigates VNS, an emerging approach that fosters a neurochemical environment facilitating the release of neuromodulators promoting cortical plasticity. Previous studies show enhanced cortical plasticity and improved upper extremity recovery with paired VNS rehabilitation compared to identical rehabilitation without VNS. The optimal dosing and timing for maximal cortical plasticity and motor recovery have been identified in pre-clinical animal studies.

The study further discusses the safety, feasibility, and efficacy of these VNS parameters paired with rehabilitation in clinical trials conducted on human subjects with stroke, leading to FDA approval of paired VNS using the Vivistim® system for upper extremity motor recovery after stroke. The current study aims to evaluate the safety, feasibility, and therapeutic potential of fully implanted invasive VNS (Vivistim®) paired with rehabilitation in individuals with cervical SCI, addressing the critical unmet need in this population. Although VNS is already FDA-approved for stroke rehabilitation, evidence in the SCI population remains limited.

METHODS

The study was a single-site, triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot trial evaluating the feasibility, safety, and preliminary efficacy of invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) paired with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI). Six adults with chronic traumatic incomplete cervical SCI were enrolled and implanted with the Vivistim® System. Participants were randomized to receive either active VNS paired with rehabilitation or control (sham) VNS paired with rehabilitation. The study included recruitment, screening, baseline assessments, device implantation, randomization, blinding, intervention, a control group crossover, outcome measures, and statistical analysis.

The primary objectives were to assess feasibility and safety. Feasibility outcomes included recruitment, retention, session attendance, and adherence to both in-clinic and home-based rehabilitation. Safety outcomes included surgery and therapy-related adverse events. Preliminary efficacy was assessed using validated measures of upper extremity motor function, activities of daily living, and quality of life. The primary clinical outcome was the Graded Redefined Assessment of Strength, Sensibility, and Prehension (GRASSP). Secondary outcomes included the Capabilities of Upper Extremity Questionnaire (CUE-Q), patient satisfaction, and treatment tolerability.

Given the pilot nature of the study, analyses were primarily descriptive. Outcomes were summarized by treatment group following the intention-to-treat principle. Analyses were not intended for formal inference, and estimation of variability, confidence intervals, or effect sizes was not emphasized. A detailed statistical analysis plan is provided in the published protocol.

RESULTS

In this study, six participants with chronic traumatic incomplete cervical SCI were implanted with the Vivistim® System for a maximum of 90-day follow-up. No serious adverse events or device complications were observed during the study period. Four out of six participants completed all 18 in-clinic rehabilitation sessions, and five participants completed over 94% of scheduled sessions.

Clinically, all three participants in the active VNS group demonstrated improvement in total GRASSP scores immediately after intervention compared to baseline, while none in the control group showed improvements. At the 90-day follow-up, two out of three participants in the active group sustained improvements, whereas one participant showed attenuation of initial gains. In the control group, one participant demonstrated improvement, while the remaining two did not.

Participants reported subjective improvements in upper extremity function following the intervention, including improved coordination and movement smoothness, increased pinch strength, and greater ease in performing activities of daily living. Overall treatment satisfaction was high, with 4 participants reporting being very satisfied and 2 reporting being satisﬁed; no neutral or negative responses were reported.

DISCUSSION

The study by Korupolu et al. is a triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot investigation evaluating the safety and feasibility of pairing invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI). The primary objectives were to establish procedural safety and feasibility, with exploratory clinical outcomes included to inform future trial designs.

The study demonstrated that the implantation of the FDA-approved Vivistim® VNS system and its integration with intensive rehabilitation is safe, well-tolerated, and feasible in the SCI population. The study participants showed mild-to-moderate improvements in Graded Redefined Assessment of Strength, Sensibility and Prehension (GRASSP) scores immediately after completing in-clinic therapy, while no participants in the sham group showed post-intervention improvement.

The feasibility benchmarks were met for in-clinic rehabilitation, with high adherence to scheduled therapy sessions and no post-implantation attrition. However, transportation and access barriers emerged as meaningful challenges affecting recruitment and limiting completion of in-clinic sessions. These findings extend the established safety profile of invasive VNS previously demonstrated in epilepsy and stroke to individuals with chronic cervical SCI, a population with unique physiological vulnerabilities.

The study has several important limitations, including small sample size, pilot design, clinical heterogeneity, variable adherence to home exercise programs, and the need for further studies to explore automated or technology-assisted stimulation approaches to improve scalability and precision. The primary contribution of this study remains the demonstration of safety, tolerability, and feasibility.

CONCLUSION

In the pilot study, invasive vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) combined with upper extremity rehabilitation for individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury was found to be safe, feasible, and well-tolerated. The high adherence to in-clinic therapy and lack of severe or unanticipated adverse events support the practicality of this intervention in a clinical setting. Although clinical improvements were mild to moderate for a small number of participants, consistent post-intervention gains in the active VNS group suggest potential benefits worth further investigation. The study extends the safety profile of invasive VNS to the SCI population and provides initial data for future research. Larger trials with refined inclusion criteria, enhanced support for home-based therapy, extended or intensive in-clinic rehabilitation, quantitative movement assessments, and hybrid in-clinic/tele-rehabilitation models are needed to determine effectiveness, durability, and optimal implementation of VNS paired with rehabilitation in spinal cord injury.

Key findings: 1. Invasive VNS combined with upper extremity rehabilitation is safe, feasible, and well-tolerated for individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury. 2. High adherence to in-clinic therapy and the absence of serious or unanticipated adverse events support the practicality of this intervention in a clinical setting. 3. Although clinical improvements were mild to moderate, consistent post-intervention gains in the active VNS group suggest potential benefits worth further investigation. 4. The study provides initial data for future research on the effectiveness and optimal implementation of VNS paired with rehabilitation in spinal cord injury.
