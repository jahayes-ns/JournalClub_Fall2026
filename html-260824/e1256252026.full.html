Behavioral/Cognitive: Kv4.2V404M Mutation Induces Epileptiform Activity and Multiple Behavioral Abnormalities in Heterozygous Knock-in Mice

Jerng, Silva-Pérez, David, Chin, Krishnan, Pfaffinger | 2026

Keywords: A-current, autism, channelopathy, epilepsy, mutation, somatodendritic

Final Summary

This study investigates the effects of a Kv4.2V404M mutation in mice, associated with early-onset epilepsy, autism, and global developmental delay in humans. The researchers generated heterozygous knock-in C57BL/6J mice using CRISPR technology and compared their development, physiology, and behavior with wild-type (Kv4.2(+/+)) littermates.

Key findings include high mortality during early development in Kv4.2(V404M/+) mice (>50%), reduced body weight in male Kv4.2(V404M/+) mice by 25-30%, altered ISA functional properties, occurrence of 4–5 Hz spike-wave epileptiform discharges in Kv4.2(V404M/+) mice during non-rapid eye movement sleep, elevated levels of ΔFosB in the cortex and hippocampus, and significant alterations in exploratory behavior, social interaction, fear conditioning, and spatial memory in Kv4.2(V404M/+) mice.

The study suggests that the Kv4.2V404M mutation produces a syndrome of epilepsy and aberrant behavioral phenotypes in mice. The findings suggest potential implications for human neurological disorders related to these factors, making this mouse model valuable for understanding the pathology and validating targeted therapeutic approaches to treating this disorder.

RESULTS

The study investigates the effects of a V404M mutation in the Kv4.2 potassium channel on neurons, behavior, and potential implications for neurodevelopmental disorders like autism spectrum disorder (ASD).

  1. Biophysical properties of neuronal ionic currents (ISA) in cerebellar granule neurons were altered, with V404M ISA being larger, slower to decay, and slower to inactivate over a wide range of voltages.

  2. V404M mice displayed epileptiform discharges during sleep characterized by high-amplitude spike–wave activity in the frontal and parietal cortices.

  3. Elevated ΔFosB expression was observed in the cortical retrosplenial/parietal region and hippocampus, with a twofold increase in the former and a dramatic elevation in the latter compared to wild-type mice.

  4. In behavioral studies, V404M mice exhibited reduced activity levels, increased shelter engagement, impaired memory, and an ASD-like phenotype in terms of interactions with other mice and inanimate objects.

  5. No significant impact of sex was found on the behavioral results, except for male mice's tendency to be more open in the elevated plus maze.

In response to provocative home cage maneuvers like bright light or loud sound, V404M mice showed increased immobility and shelter engagement compared to wild-type mice, but responded similarly to a single 60-second beep. During a 2-hour shelter removal trial, some but not all V404M mice resided in the area previously occupied by their shelter ("sheltering"). In fear conditioning assays, V404M mice had lower initial fear responses and impaired memory of the learned context. Spatial memory was impaired in the Morris water maze test, as V404M mice did not exhibit a preference for a specific quadrant or spend less time near the platform's prior location compared to wild-type mice. Lastly, V404M mice spent significantly less time interacting with a stranger mouse during the three-chamber social interaction assay.
