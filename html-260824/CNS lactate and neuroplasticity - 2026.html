Final Summary

This study published in The Journal of Physiology explores how lactate, produced by astrocytes, enhances neuronal NMDA receptor (NMDAR) function and synaptic plasticity through a redox mechanism. Lactate enters neurons via monocarboxylate transporters (MCTs), is oxidized by lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) to pyruvate, generating NADH and increasing the intracellular NADH/NAD+ ratio. This redox shift promotes Ca2+ release from ryanodine-sensitive stores (RyR) and recruits Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) to GluN2B-containing NMDARs, leading to enhanced NMDAR currents, prolonged decay kinetics, increased NMDAR presence at postsynaptic sites, and facilitating long-term synaptic plasticity and memory formation.

The study found that lactate potentiates NMDAR responses through an intracellular redox mechanism, with the C-terminal domain (CTD) of GluN2B being crucial for this effect. Two potential redox-sensitive cysteine-rich sequences within the disordered CTD were identified, and redox-insensitive mutants showed reduced responses to lactate compared to wild-type subunits.

In neurons, lactate enhances the association between CaMKII and GluN2B, leading to increased synaptic localization of this complex and enhanced synaptic responses. These findings suggest a specific role for lactate in promoting the association between CaMKII and GluN2B at synapses, contributing to synaptic plasticity changes by lactate in neurons.

The study provides evidence that intracellular redox modifications of the CTD of the GluN2B subunit are crucial for lactate-induced potentiation of NMDAR and suggests a specific role for lactate in promoting the association between CaMKII and GluN2B at synapses, contributing to synaptic plasticity changes that may play a critical role in learning and memory processes. Further investigation is needed to confirm these hypotheses and to understand the specific proteins and molecular pathways involved in lactate-mediated modulation of NMDARs.

ABSTRACT

The study investigates how lactate, a metabolite produced by astrocytes, influences neuronal activity and synaptic function in the brain. Lactate was found to enhance NMDA receptor (NMDAR) currents in cortical neurons, increasing their amplitude and decay time constant. This modulation is dependent on monocarboxylate transporters and lactate dehydrogenase, requiring lactate entry, metabolic conversion to pyruvate, and NADH formation within neurons.

The potentiation of NMDAR by lactate was shown to depend on calcium signaling involving Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), which interacts directly with the GluN2B subunit of the receptor. Lactate strengthens the interaction between CaMKII and GluN2B through redox-sensitive cysteine residues in GluN2B, facilitating synaptic localization of this complex and enhancing synaptic responses.

These findings suggest a molecular pathway by which lactate, produced by astrocytes, can significantly influence neuronal activity and synaptic function, linking brain metabolism with learning and memory processes.

INTRODUCTION

The research paper describes a study investigating the molecular mechanism of NMDA receptor potentiation by lactate. The experiment was conducted using HEK cells transfected with NMDA receptor subunits and cortical neurons isolated from mouse embryos. Whole-cell patch clamp recordings were performed to measure NMDAR currents, while non-NMDA ionotropic glutamate receptors and voltage-dependent sodium channels were blocked. Co-immunoprecipitation and proximity ligation assays (PLAs) were used to investigate protein interactions in cortical neurons at later stages of culture.

The study found that lactate enhances the interaction between NMDA receptors and CaMKIIα, an enzyme involved in synaptic plasticity. This leads to increased phosphorylation of the NMDA receptors and their subsequent potentiation. The mechanism is critical for synaptic plasticity in cultured neurons and may play a role in learning and memory processes, with potential implications for understanding lactate's role in neurodegenerative diseases and developing new therapeutic strategies to enhance cognitive function.

RESULTS

The combined results of the scientific paper discuss the effects of lactate on NMDA receptors (NMDAR) in neurons. Lactate is found to potentiate NMDAR responses through an intracellular redox mechanism, distinct from the classical extracellular cysteine-dependent modulation.

The C-terminal domain (CTD) of GluN2B, a subunit of the NMDAR, is enriched in cysteine residues and includes large portions of intrinsic disorder, which may play a role in redox chemistry and protein functionality. Two potential redox-sensitive cysteine-rich sequences were identified within the disordered CTD of the GluN2B subunit through computational modeling.

Co-transfection experiments showed that two redox-insensitive mutants (redox 1-CTD and redox 2-CTD) displayed markedly attenuated responses to lactate compared to wild-type subunits, suggesting that redox modifications of the CTD are crucial for lactate-induced potentiation of NMDAR.

In neurons, lactate enhances the association between CaMKII and GluN2B, leading to an increase in the number of PLA clusters where GluN2B is expressed in close proximity to PSD-95. These findings suggest a specific role for lactate in promoting the association between CaMKII and GluN2B at synapses, which may play a critical role in synaptic plasticity changes by lactate in neurons.

Overall, the study provides evidence that intracellular redox modifications of the CTD of the GluN2B subunit are crucial for lactate-induced potentiation of NMDAR and suggests a specific role for lactate in promoting the association between CaMKII and GluN2B at synapses, contributing to synaptic plasticity changes. Further investigation is needed to confirm these hypotheses and to understand the specific proteins and molecular pathways involved in lactate-mediated modulation of NMDARs.

DISCUSSION

This text appears to be a scientific article discussing the role of lactate, a byproduct of cellular metabolism, in modulating synaptic transmission and facilitating synaptic plasticity, which is crucial for learning and memory. The article explains that lactate is transported into neurons through monocarboxylate transporters 2 (MCT-2) and converted to pyruvate by the enzyme lactate dehydrogenase (LDH). This process increases the NADH/NAD+ ratio, leading to a more reduced intracellular milieu.

The altered redox environment affects cysteine-rich sites on the C-terminal domain of the NMDA receptor GluN2B subunits, promoting binding of calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII). This interaction stimulates the accumulation of NMDA receptors at synapses and enhances synaptic NMDA receptor function and synaptic plasticity.

The article includes a figure that shows a schematic representation of these cellular mechanisms, as well as appendices with supporting data such as concentration-response relationships for lactate-induced intracellular acidification in HEK cells and Western blots demonstrating the specificity of co-immunoprecipitation assays used to detect CaMKII association with GluN2B.

Overall, this article provides molecular resolution on the interaction between lactate, generated by astrocytes, and neurons, linking energy metabolism with neurotransmission and higher brain functions.
