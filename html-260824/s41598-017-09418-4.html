Transcriptome of neonatal preBötzinger complex neurones in Dbx1 reporter mice

Hayes, Kottick, Picardo, Halleran, Ronald D. Smith, Gregory D. Smith, Saha, Del Negro | 2017

Final Summary

This scientific study investigates the roles of Dbx1 (glutamatergic) and non-Dbx1 preBotanic nucleus (preBötC) neurons in breathing rhythm regulation through a transcriptome analysis. The research employed tamoxifen-inducible Dbx1 Cre-driver mice to identify and isolate Dbx1 preBötC neurons, which were then subjected to RNA sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2500.

Key findings indicate increased expression of glutamate-related genes in Dbx1 preBötC neurons, potentially linked to synaptic depression. Non-Dbx1 preBötC neurons showed differential expression of Gata2 and Htr1a, suggesting inhibitory functions. Evx1 expression was observed, indicating potential differentiation of Dbx1-expressing progenitor cells into ventral interneurons within the preBötC core. Additional respiratory-related genes, like Tshz3, Mecp2, and Egr2, were also identified.

The study's findings offer valuable insights into the regulation of breathing rhythm and set the foundation for future investigations aimed at developing and manipulating Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 preBötC neuron development and physiology. The original data is publicly available in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus database (accession number GSE100356).

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The combined preamble from the scientific paper outlines a transcriptome analysis focused on perinatal Dbx1 (glutamatergic) and non-Dbx1 preBotanic nucleus (preBötC) neurons, key players in respiratory rhythm generation.

Key findings include: 1. Increased expression of glutamate-related genes such as Syt10 in Dbx1 preBötC neurons, potentially linked to synaptic depression in these neurons. 2. In non-Dbx1 preBötC neurons, Gata2 and Htr1a were found to be differentially expressed, indicating potential inhibitory functions. 3. Evx1 expression was observed in some samples, suggesting that Dbx1-expressing progenitor cells might differentiate into Evx1-expressing ventral interneurons within the preBötC core. 4. Additional genes associated with respiratory function and dysfunction, like Tshz3, Mecp2, and Egr2, were also identified.

This research offers valuable insights into Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 preBötC neuron roles in breathing rhythm regulation and sets the foundation for future investigations aimed at developing and manipulating their development and physiology.

METHODS

The study identified Dbx1-derived preBötC neurons through a series of methods:

  1. Crossing female tamoxifen-inducible Dbx1 Cre-driver mice with male reporter mice to express tdTomato in cells derived from Dbx1-expressing progenitors.
  2. Administering tamoxifen during gestation at embryonic day 9.5 to pregnant dams.
  3. Anesthetizing neonatal Dbx1CreERT2; Rosa26tdTomato mice, preparing transverse brainstem sections, and perfusing them with ice-cold aCSF.
  4. Identifying and isolating Dbx1 preBötC neurons using epifluorescence under a fixed-stage microscope, followed by cDNA synthesis, amplification, and sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2500.
  5. Sequence analysis involved aligning the reads to the mm10 murine genomic database, mapping them to genes using HTSeq-count, and calculating reads per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (RPKM) for each gene in each sample.
  6. Differential gene expression was evaluated between Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 neurons using the DESeq. 2 algorithms, with a significance level set at FDR=0.1.
  7. The original data is publicly available in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus database (accession number GSE100356).
