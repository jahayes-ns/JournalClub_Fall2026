The hypoxic ventilatory response and arousal burden predict the magnitude of ventilatory long-term facilitation in humans with obstructive sleep apnoea

Mateika, Hammo, Kissane, Azarbarzin | 2025

Keywords: arousal, intermittent hypoxia, obstructive sleep apnoea, respiratory plasticity

Final Summary

This research investigates the relationship between the hypoxic ventilatory response (HVR), arousal burden, and the magnitude of ventilatory long-term facilitation (VLTF) in patients with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). The study, conducted at four US institutions, involved OSA patients who underwent polysomnography and hypoxic ventilatory response tests during wakefulness and non-rapid eye movement sleep (NREM).

Regression analysis revealed that both the HVR alone and in combination with the arousal index significantly predicted the magnitude of Progressive Augmentation (PA) and vLTF during wakefulness. Conversely, markers of apnea severity like hypoxic burden did not significantly contribute to predicting PA or vLTF. The findings suggest that HVR and arousal burden are significant factors in understanding muscle fatigue development in sleep apnea, with the arousal burden potentially being a more accurate predictor than hypoxic burden.

The study's conclusions may contribute to better understanding and management strategies for OSA. It was funded by grants from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs, National Institute of Heart, Lung and Blood, and American Academy of Sleep Medicine Foundation.

ABSTRACT

This meta-analysis investigates the relationship between progressive augmentation (PA) and ventilatory long-term facilitation (vLTF), forms of respiratory plasticity enhanced in individuals with Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA). The study used data from 91 OSA participants who underwent two mild intermittent hypoxia protocols during wakefulness.

Regression analysis identified that the Hypoxic Ventilatory Response (HVR) alone or in combination with the arousal index significantly predicted the magnitude of PA (R = 0.589, P < 0.001 and R = 0.625, P < 0.015 respectively). Similarly, for vLTF, HVR in combination with the arousal burden or arousal index were strong predictors (R = 0.602, P < 0.001 and R = 0.593, P < 0.002 respectively). In contrast, markers of apnoea severity like hypoxic burden did not significantly contribute to predicting the magnitude of PA or vLTF.

Keywords: arousal, intermittent hypoxia, obstructive sleep apnoea, respiratory plasticity.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper summarizes research on respiratory plasticity in humans during wakefulness, specifically focusing on Progressive Augmentation (PA) and ventilatory Long-Term Facilitation (vLTF) elicited by mild intermittent hypoxia. Both forms of plasticity involve a gradual increase in the ventilatory response to hypoxia from the initial to final episode, as well as a sustained increase in minute ventilation following exposure to brief intermittent periods of hypoxia.

These forms of plasticity have been observed frequently during wakefulness in healthy humans, individuals with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), and those with spinal cord injury. The initiation of these forms of plasticity and their interaction may play a role in modulating breathing instability associated with spinal cord injury or OSA.

Previous findings have shown that the hypoxic ventilatory response, measured during wakefulness, is the sole predictor of PA during exposure to intermittent hypoxia. Additionally, the hypoxic ventilatory response combined with the administered hypoxic burden predicted the magnitude of vLTF. The magnitude of PA and vLTF has been found to be greater in some OSA individuals compared to healthy controls.

The paper aims to explore whether modifications in mechanisms that cause OSA, coupled with parameters characterizing the severity of OSA, predict the magnitude of PA and vLTF in OSA patients during wakefulness. The investigation will consider both traditional indicators and recently ascertained novel indicators of sleep apnea severity.

METHODS

The study explores the effects of two mild intermittent hypoxia protocols (P1 and P2) during sleep and wakefulness on ventilatory control parameters. Methods used include calculating hypoxic burden, ventilatory response to arousal, progressive augmentation, and ventilatory long-term facilitation. A best subset regression analysis was employed to identify predictive models for these measures.

The study found that P1 had a higher hypoxic burden compared to P2, but does not provide specific conclusions or findings.

A secondary part of the study compares two questionnaire versions to assess sleep apnea severity, using best subset regression analysis to identify optimal models with significant variables set at P < 0.05 and data presented as means ± standard deviation. The dependent variable was either wake after sleep onset (PA) or ventilatory response to a low flow-pause test (vLTF), with independent variables determined through best subset regression analysis.

RESULTS

The study investigated the effects of mild intermittent hypoxia on participants with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). Anthropometric variables, endotypic markers, and indicators of OSA severity were measured before and after the protocol. The protocol consisted of two parts, P1 and P2, during which participants were exposed to controlled periods of hypoxia interspersed with normoxia (normal oxygen levels).

The key findings are as follows:

  1. Best subset regression analysis using novel indicators of apnea severity revealed that the hypoxic ventilatory response (HVR), hypoxic burden (during sleep), arousal threshold, and body mass index (BMI) predicted the magnitude of progressive augmentation (PA). HVR was the only significant predictor in the multi-linear regression analysis.

  2. The best subset regression analysis using novel indicators also revealed that HVR, hypoxic burden (during wakefulness), arousal burden, hypoxic burden (during sleep), and loop gain best predicted the magnitude of long-term facilitation (LTF). In the multi-linear regression analysis, the magnitude of LTF was predicted by a linear combination of HVR and arousal burden.

  3. Regression analyses using established measures of apnea severity showed that the hypoxic ventilatory response and arousal index were the significant predictors for both PA and LTF. Other factors like ventilatory response to arousal, apnea duration, body mass index, age did not significantly contribute to the prediction of PA or LTF.

The study employed a controlled protocol with a specific focus on hypoxic ventilatory response and its role in predicting progressive augmentation and long-term facilitation in individuals with OSA. The findings suggest that HVR is a crucial factor in understanding these phenomena.

DISCUSSION

In a 2001 study, researchers investigated the factors contributing to muscle fatigue (Phrenic Amplitude - PA and Ventilatory Long-Term Facilitation - vLTF) in individuals with Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA). The study found that both hypoxic ventilatory response and markers of arousal significantly predicted the magnitude of PA and vLTF.

The researchers suggest that peripheral chemoreflex sensitivity to hypoxia may enhance PA and vLTF. Additionally, they propose that repeated nocturnal exposure to stimuli activating neural pathways involved in both arousal response and respiratory plasticity beyond intermittent hypoxia may also contribute to enhancing PA or vLTF.

The study suggests that the arousal burden, which reflects multiple potential stimuli involved in activating neuronal pathways relevant to both arousal and LTF initiation, might be a more accurate predictor of muscle fatigue than hypoxic burden, focusing on a single stimulus. Further studies are needed to confirm these findings and better understand the mechanisms underlying muscle fatigue development in sleep apnea.
