PREAMBLE

Title: Obstructive Sleep Apnea Endotype Prediction During Wakefulness Using Voice Biomarkers

Authors: Shiva Akbari, Behrad Taghibeyglou, Atousa Assadi, Dominick Madulid, Devin Brown, Daniel Vena, Scott Sands, Azadeh Yadollahi

This study investigates the potential of using voice biomarkers to predict Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) endotypes during wakefulness. The research was conducted on a diverse group of participants. A novel method involving Automated Speech Recognition (ASR) and deep learning algorithms was employed to analyze voice recordings for various OSA-related parameters.

The study identified specific voice biomarkers associated with OSA endotypes, which showed promising results in distinguishing between different OSA severities even during wakefulness. The findings suggest that these voice biomarkers could potentially serve as a non-invasive and cost-effective diagnostic tool for OSA. Further research is recommended to validate these findings and explore potential clinical applications of this novel approach.

ABSTRACT

This paper introduces a non-invasive machine learning framework for predicting the underlying endotypes of Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), specifically focusing on anatomical impairment and poor muscle compensation using speech acoustic features. The framework employs a supervised autoencoder optimized for feature reconstruction and endotype prediction, with mutual information-optimized dimensionality selection to refine the latent space prior to modeling. An enhanced multi-layer perceptron (MLP) architecture incorporating Swish activation, batch normalization, dropout, and RMSprop optimization is then utilized for robust evaluation.

The paper highlights that traditional methods of evaluating OSA using the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI) lack an explanation of why OSA occurs or why patients respond differently to therapy. Instead, OSA is understood as a syndrome of distinct physiological endotypes, with four primary endotypes identified: Anatomical Compromise (High Collapsibility), Impaired Pharyngeal Dilator Muscle Function (Poor Muscle Compensation), Upper Airway Resistance Syndrome (UARS), and Mixed Endotype. The proposed framework aims to help tailor OSA therapy for individual patients, addressing the ongoing clinical challenge.
