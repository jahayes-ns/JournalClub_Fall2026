Astrocytes connect specific brain regions through plastic networks

Cooper, Selles, Cammer, Redd, Gildea, Sall, Chiurri, Cheung, Wheeler, Saab, Liddelow, Chao | 2026

Final Summary

The scientific paper presents two interconnected studies exploring the development of a new tool, the Astrocyte Network Tracer, and its application to understand the plasticity of astrocyte networks in response to sensory deprivation (whisker trimming). The tracer is a genetically encoded biotinylation enzyme that allows visualization of astrocyte networks by identifying connected cells and demonstrating distinct network morphology across various brain regions.

The whisker trimming study reveals that long-range astrocyte networks are structurally plastic, differing from neuronal connectomes, and are more affected in the barrel cortex following whisker trimming. Key findings include a significant reduction in the volume of astrocyte networks without gap junctional proteins Cx43 and Cx30 and evidence that astrocyte networks play a crucial role in maintaining synaptic transmission and neurodegenerative stress resistance by facilitating the exchange of metabolites between neurons and astrocytes.

The study by Mori et al. (2006) focuses on the effects of deleting the connexin43 gene in astroglia and radial glia cells in mice, employing induction methods to selectively remove Cx43 and analyzing its impact on brain functions such as hearing and locomotion. Astrocyte networks were detected using viral vector delivery and biotin, with motor cortex, hypothalamic, and prefrontal cortex network reproducibility demonstrated using light sheet microscopy. Whisker trimming reduced the size of cortical astrocyte networks in the barrel cortex, while chains of fibrous astrocytes were found to interconnect bilateral astrocyte networks in the corpus callosum.

The findings suggest important implications for our understanding of brain function, neurological diseases, and potential therapeutic approaches. The studies also reference other works discussing roles of gap junctions in various physiological processes, diseases, and therapeutic approaches.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The combined preamble of the scientific papers discusses two main topics: the development and validation of a new tool called the "astrocyte network tracer" and a study on the impact of whisker trimming on astrocyte and neuron connectivity in mouse brains.

  1. Astrocyte Network Tracer: The tracer is a genetically encoded biotinylation enzyme (TurboID) fused with Connexin 43, a protein found in gap junctions of astrocytes. It allows for the visualization of astrocyte networks by identifying connected cells and demonstrates distinct network morphology across various brain regions.

  2. Whisker Trimming Study: The study investigated the plasticity of astrocyte networks in response to sensory deprivation (whisker trimming) compared to neuronal networks. Results suggest that long-range astrocyte networks are structurally plastic, differing from the neuronal connectome, and are more affected in the barrel cortex, where connections to the prefrontal cortex and contralateral signals were largely absent post-trimming.

Key findings include: - The volume of astrocyte networks is significantly reduced in mice without gap junctional proteins Cx43 and Cx30. - Astrocyte networks are plastic, able to change in response to sensory deprivation. - Long-range astrocyte networks differ from neuronal connectomes.

CONCLUSION

The combined conclusion of the scientific paper discusses the discovery and investigation of astrocyte metabolic networks in the brain. Using proximity labeling with TurboID, researchers found that astrocytes form extensive, interconnected networks that facilitate the exchange of metabolites between neurons and astrocytes, playing a crucial role in maintaining synaptic transmission and neurodegenerative stress resistance. The findings suggest important implications for our understanding of brain function, neurological diseases, and potential therapeutic approaches.

The paper also references several studies on gap junctions, intercellular channels that allow direct communication between cells. These studies highlight their roles in various physiological processes, such as metabolite clearance from the adult brain (Xie et al., 2013), short-term motor learning (Wang et al., 2019), and astroglial glutamine requirement for physiological synaptic activity and recognition memory (Cheung et al., 2022). Additionally, the studies discuss gap junctions' implications in diseases such as epilepsy (Onodera et al., 2021; Munoz-Ballester et al., 2025) and neurodegeneration (Le et al., 2014; Shandra et al., 2019).

Overall, these studies provide valuable insights into the roles of astrocyte metabolic networks and gap junctions in brain function, disease, and potential therapeutic approaches.

METHODS

The scientific paper summarized describes a study by Mori et al. published in Glia in 2006, focusing on the effects of deleting the connexin43 (Cx43) gene in astroglia and radial glia cells in mice. The researchers used an induction method to selectively remove Cx43 from these cell types, studying its impact on brain functions such as hearing and locomotion.

Key methods employed include:

  1. Inducible gene deletion for removing Cx43
  2. Tissue clearing techniques for visualizing mouse brains
  3. qPCR, Western blotting, and immunofluorescence microscopy for analyzing gene expression and protein levels in astrocytes
  4. Viral vector delivery (AAV5-GfaABC1D-Cx43:TID:HA) to detect astrocyte networks

In the study, primary immunopanned astrocytes from rats and mice with genetically induced Cx43 knockout (cKO) were analyzed. The viability of cKO astrocytes decreased significantly at 4-Hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT) concentrations above 1:2000, and there was a significant reduction in Cx43/Actin ratio in primary immunopanned cKO astrocytes at all 4-OHT concentrations.

Astrocyte networks were detected using AAV5-GfaABC1D-Cx43:TID:HA and biotin, with the viral load necessary for ~10% of astrocytes to express Cx43-TID found to be approximately 80%. Genetically-expressed Cx43 was essential for biotinylated molecules to distribute beyond infected cells. The study demonstrated motor cortex, hypothalamic, and prefrontal cortex network reproducibility using light sheet microscopy and revealed that astrocyte networks link specific brain regions.

Whisker trimming reduced the size of cortical astrocyte networks in barrel cortex, while chains of fibrous astrocytes were found to interconnect bilateral astrocyte networks in the corpus callosum. The study further showed that cortical astrocyte networks can contain both parenchymal and glia limitans superficialis astrocytes on the brain’s surface.
