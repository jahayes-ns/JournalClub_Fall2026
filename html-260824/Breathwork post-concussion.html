Respiratory modulation of neurophysiology and symptoms in athletes with sports-related concussion: a randomized crossover trial

Wang, Zhu, Gao, Yu, Yan, Wang, Zhang | 2026

Keywords: autonomic nervous system, electroencephalography, pelvic floor resonance breathing, resonance breathing, skin sympathetic nerve activity, sports-related concussion

Final Summary

This study investigates the impact of Respiratory Biofeedback (RB) and Proprioceptive Respiratory Biofeedback (PRB) on symptoms associated with Sport-Related Concussion (SRC). A randomized crossover design with 26 male athletes was employed. Both interventions demonstrated a reduction in SRC symptoms.

In terms of Electroencephalography (EEG) changes, both RB and PRB influenced the prefrontal cortex and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, showing distinct spatial distributions and spectral characteristics. Theta, beta, and delta band power changes implied an impact on attentional control, cognitive engagement, and cortical state regulation.

Prefrontal theta activity increased during both interventions, associated with focused attention, cognitive control, and self-regulation. PRB, due to its complex nature involving pelvic floor muscle coordination alongside diaphragmatic breathing, exhibited more extensive EEG changes.

Correlations between brain activity and the Autonomic Nervous System (ANS) revealed that following the PRB intervention, changes in prefrontal EEG activity were linked to changes in SKNA, suggesting enhanced inhibitory regulatory processes in the prefrontal cortex and potential reduced sympathetic overactivation.

However, the study has limitations such as a small sample size, limited generalizability, potential Type I errors, uncorrected correlation analyses, and lack of a control group. Despite these limitations, the findings suggest that respiratory exercises could be a valuable adjunctive approach for concussion rehabilitation.
