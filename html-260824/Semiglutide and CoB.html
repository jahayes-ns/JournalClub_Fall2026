Final Summary

The research question addressed by the paper "Acta Physiologica, 2026; 242:e70171" is the potential expanded clinical impact of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists (GLP1RAs) in respiratory physiology and the treatment of respiratory disorders, specifically Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA).

The study utilizes a combination of experimental research on mice with obstructive lung disease and clinical trials involving obese individuals treated with tirzepatide, a dual receiver long-acting agonist of glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP1R.

The key findings reveal that GLP1RAs reduce apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) in patients with obesity and moderate/severe OSA, enhance the hypercapnic ventilatory response, increase breathing during sleep, and decrease AHI and oxyhemoglobin desaturations. Additionally, the study proposes targeting hypoglossal motoneurons as a potential drug target for improving upper airway patency in OSA.

Indirect benefits of GLP1RAs were also observed in reducing mortality and improving lung function in mouse studies with obstructive lung disease. The research suggests that GLP1RAs offer additional benefits beyond Type 2 diabetes mellitus (TD2M) and weight loss management, including improvements in respiratory diseases. However, no datasets were generated or analyzed during the current study.

In conclusion, this paper highlights the expanding clinical impact of GLP1RAs beyond their initial use for TD2M and obesity, emphasizing their potential role in treating OSA and other respiratory disorders, and indirectly improving lung function.
