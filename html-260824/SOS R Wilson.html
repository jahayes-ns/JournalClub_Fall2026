Novel oxygen sensing mechanism in the spinal cord involved in cardiorespiratory responses to hypoxia

Barioni, Derakhshan, Tenorio Lopes, Onimaru, Roy, McDonald, Scheibli, Baghdadwala, Heidari, Bharadia, Ikeda, Yazawa, Okada, Harris, Dutschmann, Wilson | 2022

Final Summary

In the study by Barioni et al. (2022), a novel oxygen-sensing mechanism in the spinal cord, termed Spinal Oxygen Sensors (SOS), was discovered. This mechanism is present in sympathetic preganglionic neurons and plays a critical role in enhancing sympatho-respiratory activity during hypoxia, potentially conferring a life-or-death advantage. The researchers propose that this mechanism involves neuronal nitric oxide synthase 1 (NOS1) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase (NOX). During hypoxia, NOS1 acts as an oxygen-dependent sink for NADPH, while in hyperoxia, NOS1 decreases its availability to NOX, thereby silencing SOS. The findings suggest that SOS could play a significant role in physiological regulation in chronic diseases, spinal cord injury, and cardiorespiratory crises, with potential implications for treating hypoxic-induced pathologies such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) and ischemic heart disease. The study was conducted using artificial perfused in situ and en bloc preparations to investigate the oxygen sensitivity of sympathetic preganglionic neurons (SSPNs) in the thoracic spinal cord and their role in sympathetic responses to hypoxia, identifying TRPA1 and TRPM4 as potential effector molecules involved in transducing ROS to SSPN excitability.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Barioni et al. (2022) reveals a novel oxygen sensing mechanism in the spinal cord that contributes to cardiorespiratory responses during hypoxia. This mechanism, termed Spinal Oxygen Sensors (SOS), is found in sympathetic preganglionic neurons and functions independently of astrocytes. Hypoxia excites SOS, while hyperoxia silences them. This mechanism enhances sympatho-respiratory activity during asphyxia-like stimuli, potentially conferring a life-or-death advantage. The researchers propose that this mechanism involves neuronal nitric oxide synthase 1 (NOS1) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase (NOX). In hyperoxia, NOS1 acts as an oxygen-dependent sink for NADPH, while in hypoxia, NADPH catabolism by NOS1 decreases, increasing its availability to NOX and triggering reactive oxygen species–dependent processes. The findings suggest that SOS could play a significant role in physiological regulation in chronic diseases, spinal cord injury, and cardiorespiratory crises.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the role of carotid bodies in mammals as primary oxygen sensors, driving breathing, sympathetic activity, and blood pressure responses during acute hypoxia. Although denervation of carotid bodies abolishes most but not all hypoxic ventilatory responses, certain sympathetic responses persist, indicating the existence of central sympathetic oxygen sensors.

Studies have identified potential hypoxia-excited cells in various regions such as the hypothalamus and brainstem's nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) and rostral ventrolateral medulla (RVLM). These cells are believed to receive carotid body inputs, activate brainstem respiratory and/or spinal thoracic preganglionic sympathetic neurons, and potentially contribute to proexcitatory effects of brainstem hypoxia under certain conditions.

Mechanisms for oxygen sensing are not fully understood, but they may involve astrocytes, heme oxygenase (HO), Nap channels, adenosine 5′-monophosphate–activated protein kinase, and more. However, the existence of brainstem and hypothalamic oxygen sensors does not exclude the possibility of oxygen-sensitive spinal cord "vasomotor centers."

Previous studies dating back to 1909 suggest the presence of oxygen-sensitive spinal centers. However, the ability of the spinal cord to mediate carotid body–independent pressor effects of hypoxia remains controversial due to potential involvement of brainstem and systemic factors like lactic acid release or hormones in activating spinal circuits.

Recent interest has focused on brainstem oxygen sensing, with astrocyte activation proposed as a mechanism to offset central hypoxic inhibition. However, the paper aims to shed light on the possibility of powerful cardiovascular spinal oxygen sensors (SOS), using artificial perfused in situ and en bloc preparations to overcome limitations of previous in vivo studies. The research aims to confirm the oxygen sensitivity of spinal cord sympathetic preganglionic neurons, determine their critical function, cellular identity, and mechanism.

RESULTS

The study by Barioni et al. (2022) investigates the oxygen sensitivity of sympathetic preganglionic neurons (SSPNs) in the thoracic spinal cord and their role in sympathetic responses to hypoxia.

The researchers used both in situ perfused spinal cord preparations and en bloc transverse thoracic spinal cord section preparations. They found that SSPNs have endogenous oxygen sensitivity, as evidenced by c-fos expression being induced in ChAT+ IML SSPNs by hypoxia.

The study suggests that NOS1 may function as an oxygen-dependent sink for NADPH, regulating its availability for other NADPH-dependent molecules. Inhibition of NOX by apocynin decreased sympathetic nerve activity responses to hypoxia, while SOD mimetics amplified the hypoxic response.

The study also identified TRPA1 and TRPM4 as potential effector molecules involved in transducing ROS to SSPN excitability. Two TRPA1 antagonists provoked g-SN activity and abolished the hypoxic response, while 9-PNT, a TRPM4 antagonist, abolished hypoxic g-SN responses and SSPN single-cell responses.

The findings contribute to a better understanding of the mechanisms underlying sympathetic responses to hypoxia and could have implications for treating hypoxic-induced pathologies such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) and ischemic heart disease.

DISCUSSION

The research paper 'Novel oxygen sensing mechanism in the spinal cord involved in cardiorespiratory responses to hypoxia', published in Science Advances, presents a novel mechanism involving nitric oxide synthase 1 (NOS1) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase 2 (NOX2) that produces reactive oxygen species (ROS) in response to low oxygen levels, triggering sympathetic nerve activity. The study was funded by several institutions and the authors thank R. Yates and P. Whelan for providing mice strains and M. Izumizaki for facilitating patch clamp experiments.

The research employed various experimental methods, including in-situ thoracic spinal cord preparation, pharmacological and gene KO in-situ experiments, in-situ triple perfused preparations, in-vivo experiments, histochemical cell labeling (c-fos/ChAT immunohistochemistry), and others. The paper also references several scientific articles related to topics such as hypoxia-inducible factors, nitric oxide synthases, NADPH oxidases, astrocyte signaling, and more.

The research suggests that spinal cord sympathetic preganglionic neurons (SSPNs) may play a role in prolonging survival mechanisms triggered during severe hypoxia or asphyxia-like conditions, with potential implications for various medical conditions such as obesity, atherosclerosis, diabetes, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, sleep apnea, and acute mountain sickness. The paper was published on March 25, 2022.
