A Review of Pharmacological Therapies for Obstructive Sleep Apnea and Impact on Cardiovascular Outcomes

Robinson, De Sarkar, Moore, Malhotra | 2026

Keywords: Obstructive sleep apnea, GLP-1 receptor agonists, Pharmacological therapies, Cardiovascular disease

Final Summary

This scientific review published in Current Sleep Medicine Reports (2026) addresses the heterogeneous nature of Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), a prevalent condition affecting over 1 billion people worldwide. The paper suggests that OSA has multiple underlying endophenotypes, including upper airway anatomy, ventilatory control instability, and other factors, which differ among individuals with OSA, supporting the possibility of personalized treatment approaches.

The research question investigates current pharmacological strategies for OSA, focusing on their impact on cardiovascular disease. Strategies include augmenting upper airway dilator muscle activity, stabilizing control of breathing, blocking cardiovascular consequences of OSA, addressing OSA symptoms, promoting CPAP adherence, and facilitating weight loss. GLP-1 receptor agonists have shown improvements in both OSA and CVD biomarkers, particularly in promoting weight loss.

The key findings summarize the potential future of OSA management as a combination of therapies that target mechanical airway obstruction, underlying obesity, and cardiovascular risk factors. The conclusion highlights advancements in treating OSA, with Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) therapy remaining the primary treatment, but other approaches including preventing recurrent apneas, facilitating PAP adherence, blocking OSA consequences, promoting weight loss, and exploring pharmacotherapy being considered. The study discusses promising agents like dual agonist medications, bimagrumab, and amylin-based agents for weight loss.

Despite these advancements, ongoing research is crucial for further improvements in clinical outcomes for OSA patients, particularly regarding the integration of pharmacologic strategies into treatment processes. The paper suggests that tirzepatide offers substantial benefits for OSA patients but more research is necessary to determine its ideal integration.

PREAMBLE

The review article published in Current Sleep Medicine Reports (2026) discusses the heterogeneous nature of Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA). It suggests that OSA has multiple underlying endophenotypes, including upper airway anatomy, ventilatory control instability, upper airway dilator muscle function, arousal threshold, and other factors. These endotypes differ among individuals with OSA, supporting the possibility of personalized treatment approaches.

OSA phenotypes, defined by clinical expression of the disease, also vary widely, with some patients being asymptomatic, having disrupted sleep, or experiencing severe excessive daytime sleepiness. The group with excessive sleepiness is associated with a higher cardiovascular risk. The article argues against a one-size-fits-all strategy for treating OSA and advocates for a personalized approach based on the underlying mechanisms and the risk of disease consequences.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the paper discusses Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), a prevalent condition affecting at least 1 billion people globally, with estimates suggesting an increase due to the obesity pandemic and aging population. OSA is associated with significant cardiometabolic and neurocognitive complications [1,2]. Although Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) can be beneficial for some patients [4-7], it's not always well-tolerated. CPAP has been linked to improvements in cardiovascular mortality and all-cause mortality [5], but definitive trials showing hard outcome benefits from CPAP are still lacking. The paper further explores the need for alternative pharmacological treatments for OSA.
