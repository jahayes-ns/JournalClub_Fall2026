Beyond Breathing: Lung as a Sensory Organ A Report from the NHLBI Workshop on Lung Sensing and Its Implication in Diseases

Jendzjowsky, Kuo, Reznikov, Chen, Li, Prescott, Liu, Seeholzer, Tschumperlin, Taylor-Clark, Rajagopal, Haber, Noël, Kotas, Drake, Pabelick, Rosenblatt, Mouradian, Su, Gomez, Lin, Lu, Mongodin, Vuga, Zhou, Prakash, Sun | 2026

Final Summary

The scientific paper explores the complex role of the lungs as a multisensory interface, focusing on its chemosensors (including olfactory and taste receptors, ASICs, TRPV1) and mechanosensors (such as Piezo1, Piezo2). The research question is to understand the neuroendocrine cells (NECs) in the lung's mechanical sensing and their role in regulating ventilation.

Methods employed include an examination of various components within the lung's sensory circuits involving pulmonary nerve endings, airway smooth muscle cells, immune cells, and epithelial cells. The study also delves into the lung-brain axis, focusing on afferent inputs from pulmonary receptors, central integration in neural hubs like the NTS, and efferent outputs regulating airway smooth muscle tone, mucus secretion, breathing, and immune states.

Key findings reveal the sensory component in various conditions such as fibrosis caused by increased extracellular matrix production due to mechanosensation by Piezo channels and its implications in diseases like asthma. The study also highlights the potential therapeutic opportunities using exogenous hydrogen sulfide donors.

In conclusion, this research emphasizes the importance of understanding lung sensing mechanisms for addressing various health conditions. Future research aims to fill knowledge gaps on diversity of sensory and neuroendocrine cells, molecular mechanisms of lung sensing, technological and resource limitations, and building a research ecosystem. Strategies include naturalistic and longitudinal tracking, studying plasticity and memory in sensory cells, fostering collaborative consortia, providing data science support, and creating a centralized repository of resources.
