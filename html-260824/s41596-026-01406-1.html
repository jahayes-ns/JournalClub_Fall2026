Protocol for head-restrained drug self-administration in mice

Final Summary

Title: Head-Fixed Drug Self-Administration Protocol in Mice for Substance Use Disorders Research

In this Nature Protocols paper, a multi-institutional research team presents a low-cost protocol for drug self-administration experiments in head-fixed mice. The goal is to develop an efficient and stress-reducing method compatible with neurotechnologies like two-photon calcium imaging and holographic optogenetics for studying substance use disorders mechanisms.

The protocol includes detailed guidelines for constructing custom equipment, implementing open-source software (REACHER/Labrynth), catheter implantation, and conducting head-fixed self-administration experiments using heroin, sucrose, ethanol, and cocaine. Extinction and reinstatement sessions are also included to assess drug-seeking behavior maintenance.

Results show that head-fixed mice can learn to self-administer nondrug and drug reinforcers similarly to freely moving animals, effectively modeling key behavioral features of substance use disorders. This approach allows for studying cell-type and circuit-specific mechanisms related to substance use disorders with unprecedented resolution.

ABSTRACT

This scientific paper outlines a protocol for building low-cost, head-fixed operant chambers using 3D-printed components and open-source software (REACHER/Labrynth) for drug and nondrug self-administration in mice. The aim is to make drug self-administration experiments compatible with neurotechnologies requiring mechanical stability, such as two-photon calcium imaging and holographic optogenetics. This allows for the study of neurobiological mechanisms underlying substance use disorders with unprecedented resolution.

The protocol includes detailed descriptions for implementing custom equipment construction, software implementation and adaptation, catheter implantation, and unique considerations for conducting head-fixed self-administration experiments. The goal is to make this a standalone guide suitable for researchers with varying levels of experience.

INTRODUCTION

The scientific paper introduces a revolutionary approach for studying cell-type and circuit-specific mechanisms controlling behavior associated with substance use disorders. This method utilizes advanced technologies like single-cell calcium imaging, optogenetics, and pharmacogenetic manipulations within a head-fixed system.

The study focuses on drug self-administration in mice using heroin, sucrose, ethanol, and cocaine. The protocol provides guidelines for setting up the experimental apparatus, determining appropriate doses, loading and positioning mice, and conducting sessions to monitor self-administration behavior.

Extinction and reinstatement sessions are also included in the study design to assess maintenance of drug-seeking behavior. Troubleshooting guides are provided for common issues related to catheter problems, animal behavior, equipment malfunctions, and animal learning difficulties.

The equipment described offers affordable and durable alternatives to commercially available hardware required for self-administration experiments. The intrajugular catheterization procedure is compatible with both freely moving and head-fixed applications and has been successfully implemented in rats and mice.

Results show that head-fixed mice readily learn to discriminate between reinforced and nonreinforced levers, demonstrating a similar ability to freely moving animals in voluntarily self-administering both nondrug and drug reinforcers. This approach effectively models key behavioral features of substance use disorders.
