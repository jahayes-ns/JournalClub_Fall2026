Vip+ vagal neurons control allergen-induced responses

Zhu, Su, Sun | 2026

Final Summary

The study by Zhu et al. investigates the role of Vip+ vagal neurons in allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 inflammation associated with asthma. Through scRNA-seq datasets and cell-type-specific genetic tools, they found that Vip+ neurons, a subset of Trpv1+ neurons, play a critical role in mediating these responses. Allergen challenges alter the transcriptional landscape of vagal neurons, with Ngfr, enriched in Vip+ neurons, identified as essential for this process. Pharmacological blockade of NGFR signaling impaired Vip+-mediated asthmatic hyperresponsiveness. The study also revealed that Vip+ neurons project to the nucleus of the solitary tract (nTS) in the brainstem, suggesting a neuroimmune mechanism along the body-brain axis contributing to asthma pathogenesis. These findings highlight the importance of neural mechanisms in asthma and potentially identify Vip+ neurons as therapeutic targets for asthma treatment.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Zhu et al. identifies Vip+ vagal neurons as crucial for allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 inflammation in the lung. These neurons are necessary for these responses, and their activation leads to elevated responses. The research also found that Ngfr signaling is essential for Vip+-mediated airway hyperreactivity. Furthermore, it was discovered that Vip+ neurons project to the nucleus of the solitary tract (nTS) in the brainstem, suggesting a neuroimmune mechanism along the body-brain axis contributing to asthma pathogenesis.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction discusses the role of the lungs, specifically the lung-innervating sensory neurons originating from the vagal ganglia, in various pulmonary physiological processes and protective reflexes such as breathing, lung stretch, coughing, bronchoconstriction, airway defense, gasping, and their involvement in pathological conditions like chronic asthma. Asthma, a chronic inflammatory disease affecting over 300 million people worldwide, is characterized by airflow obstruction due to mucus hypersecretion, increased immune infiltrates, and exacerbated airway smooth muscle constriction triggered by inhaled allergens such as pollen or house dust mites.

The study focuses on Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons, two molecularly distinct subtypes within the Trpv1+ vagal sensory lineage, to investigate their role in allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and asthma pathogenesis. The research aims to identify signaling pathways utilized by these neurons, their transmission of allergen-derived signals from lung to brainstem, and potential therapeutic targets for asthma treatment.

RESULTS

The study explores the impact of allergens on vagal neurons, particularly Vip+ neurons, in asthma pathophysiology. Repeated allergen challenges alter the transcriptional landscape of vagal neurons, with genes related to myelination, mitochondrial respiration, immune-modulatory pathways, and anti-apoptotic signaling downregulated, while cytoskeletal remodeling, protein homeostasis, and complex assembly genes are upregulated.

Activation of sensitized Vip+ neurons causes asthmatic hyperreactivity and selective type 2 cytokine expression, supporting their functional roles in asthma. The study also finds increased Ngfr expression in HDM-treated vagal ganglia, suggesting its role in mediating airway hyperreactivity. Blocking NGFR attenuates respiratory system resistance and elastance during Vip+ neuron activation, implying that NGFR signaling is necessary for airway hyperreactivity but has a modest effect on type 2 cytokine expression.

Lastly, the study finds that Vip+ vagal neurons primarily project to the nTS and area postrema, with unilateral Vip+ neuron ablation reducing nTS activation following allergen exposure, suggesting their requirement for allergen-induced nTS activation.

DISCUSSION

The discussion section of the scientific paper summarizes research on vagal sensory neurons' role in asthma. The study used scRNA-seq datasets and cell-type-specific genetic tools to investigate defined vagal neuron subtypes' contribution to allergen sensing in asthmatic contexts. The findings suggest that Vip+ neurons, a subset of Trpv1+ neurons, play a critical role in mediating allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 cytokine responses. Ngfr, an upregulated gene highly enriched in Vip+ neurons, was identified as essential for this process, as pharmacological blockade of NGFR signaling impaired Vip+-mediated asthmatic hyperresponsiveness. The study also revealed that Vip+ neurons are required for relaying allergen-derived signals from the lung to the brainstem.

The research highlights the importance of neural mechanisms in asthma, with allergens or cytokines directly activating sensory neurons, which can transmit signals to the brainstem and release factors that exacerbate allergic responses. The findings suggest that Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons are distinct subtypes of Trpv1+ neurons, exhibiting non-overlapping expression patterns and distinct lung innervation profiles. Vip+ neurons were found to be necessary for HDM-induced allergen responses, while Tac1+ neurons did not appear to contribute to the immune response in this study.

The bulk RNA-seq of vagal ganglia following repeated allergen exposure revealed widespread transcriptional changes, providing insights into how vagal sensory neurons adapt to chronic inflammatory stimuli. Notably, Ngfr was identified as one of the most significantly upregulated genes in Vip+ neurons, and pharmacological blockade of NGFR signaling impaired Vip+-mediated asthmatic response.

The study underscores the importance of defining molecular and functional sensory specificity in advancing our understanding of physiology and pathophysiology, particularly given the small number of vagal sensory neurons controlling various interoception processes. The research also highlights the potential for a small, functionally specific sensory population to drive robust physiological outcomes in asthma.
