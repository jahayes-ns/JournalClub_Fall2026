Final Summary

The study published in Nature Metabolism (2026) investigates the role of hyperglycosylation in Alzheimer's disease (AD). By integrating various metabolic analysis methods, researchers found that increased glycan biosynthesis leads to brain hyperglycosylation, which may function as a critical driver of AD pathology.

In AD mouse models and post-mortem human AD samples, genetic knockdown of glycan biosynthetic enzymes improved cognitive outcomes, while oral glucosamine supplementation worsened them. A retrospective analysis of electronic health records showed an association between glucosamine use and adverse outcomes in dementia cohorts, including reduced survival in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD) patients and a higher conversion rate from Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) to AD among glucosamine users compared to non-users.

The findings suggest that hyperglycosylation is not just a secondary feature but may play a crucial role in AD pathology, making glycan metabolism an actionable target in the fight against AD. The study provides evidence supporting the potential of targeting glycan biosynthesis as a novel therapeutic strategy for Alzheimer's disease, with further research planned to investigate mechanisms underlying hyperglycosylation and its role in AD development and progression.

RESULTS

The study published in Nature Metabolism (2026) explores the effects of glucosamine on N-glycosylation and its impact on brain function, particularly social memory.

  1. In mice, increased N-glycosylation through glucosamine supplementation led to impaired social memory in AD model (5xFAD) mice but had no effect on wild-type (WT) mice. This suggests that the normal brain may have resilience mechanisms against metabolic disruptions caused by glucosamine.
  2. Real-world evidence from electronic health records showed an association between glucosamine use and adverse outcomes in dementia cohorts, including reduced survival in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias (ADRD) patients and a higher conversion rate from Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) to AD among glucosamine users compared to non-users.
  3. The study used spatial glycomics to investigate hyperglycosylation in brain tissue from Alzheimer's disease patients and mouse models, finding that it is more pronounced in certain regions of the brain associated with memory, cognitive processing, and neuroinflammation. Hyperglycosylation progressed with the severity of AD.
  4. The research team plans to further investigate the mechanisms underlying hyperglycosylation in order to better understand its role in AD development and progression.
  5. Methods used include formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples, PNGase treatment, MALDI imaging, lentiviral delivery of short hairpin RNA targeting phosphoglucomutase 3 (PGM3), administration of the small molecule OST inhibitor, NGI-1, and glucosamine supplementation.
  6. The study found no significant impact on the number of reactive astrocytes or the abundance of β-amyloid plaques or tauopathy during the experimental period. However, more research is needed to fully understand the complex interplay between glycosylation, AD pathology, and cognitive function.

DISCUSSION

The study utilizes MALDI-MSI to discover a hyperglycosylation phenotype in Alzheimer's Disease (AD) brains, which is present in two mouse models of AD - 5xFAD and PS19. Both models showed increased N-glycans across vulnerable brain regions, suggesting that this hyperglycosylation is a shared metabolic signature of neurodegeneration rather than being confined to a single driver mutation.

The findings highlight an intriguing reprogramming of UDP-GlcNAc metabolism in AD, favoring expansion of complex glycosylation at the expense of cytosolic and extracellular glycan branches. This compartment-specific redirection of hexosamine flux underscores a unique metabolic bias in AD brains that amplifies N-glycan biosynthesis while constraining other UDP-GlcNAc-dependent processes.

The study tested three approaches to target hyperglycosylation as a potential strategy against dementia: shRNA against PGM3, a small-molecule inhibitor of OST, and glucosamine supplementation. Both modalities reduced N-glycan levels in the AD mouse brains and markedly improved memory performance. However, glucosamine use was associated with worse survival in patients with AD/ADRD across real-world EHR analysis.

The study emphasizes the urgent need for a well-designed, double-blind clinical trial to systematically evaluate the impact of glucosamine on cognitive decline in patients with dementia. The findings underscore the broader potential of MALDI imaging and spatial biology in AD research, suggesting promising avenues for therapeutic exploration through pharmacological interventions targeting the hexosamine pathway in animal models.

METHODS

The methods section of the scientific paper outlines several key procedures in biology and medicine, including molecular biology techniques, behavioral testing, immunohistochemistry, and mass spectrometry.

  1. Molecular Biology Techniques: DNA/RNA extraction using TRIzol, RT-PCR, digital PCR, glycoproteomics analysis, and protein quantification using BCA assay are employed to analyze gene expression, glycopeptides, and protein concentration.

  2. Behavioral Testing: Mice undergo social memory testing using DeepLabCut software for pose estimation.

  3. Immunohistochemistry analysis: Paraffin-embedded brain tissue sections from mice and humans are stained with various markers, followed by digital image acquisition and quantitative analysis using HALO software.

  4. Mass Spectrometry: Metabolomics analysis is carried out to identify metabolites in samples on a Thermo Eclipse mass spectrometer.

Additional methods include immunohistochemistry staining procedures using a ZEISS Axio Scan.Z1 platform, antibody pre-adsorption assays for antibody specificity verification, primary neuron culture from mouse embryos, and retrospective cohort study identification through EHR data analysis for glucosamine stratification. Survival and transition analyses are also performed on EHR data using Kaplan–Meier analyses and propensity score matching.

Data distribution is assumed to be normal, and statistical analyses are carried out using GraphPad Prism. All raw MALDI datasets and proteomics datasets are available for access, with source data provided in the paper.
