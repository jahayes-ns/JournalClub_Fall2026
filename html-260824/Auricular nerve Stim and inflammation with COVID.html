Final Summary

Title: Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation for Inflammation Treatment in COVID-19

In the Revista da Associacao Medica Brasileira (2023), a review article proposes auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS) as a novel approach for managing inflammation associated with COVID-19. The research question investigates the potential of aVNS in modulating immune responses, reducing inflammatory cytokines, and improving outcomes for severe COVID-19 cases.

The authors explore various methods of aVNS, including burst stimuli at 20-25 Hz, continuous stimuli at 1 Hz, and a new burst concept for effective Aβ fiber excitation, using electrodes or needles on the superior concha or both superior and inferior conchae. They discuss the safety, efficacy, and feasibility of aVNS in clinical trials for diseases like epilepsy, depression, and migraines.

Key findings suggest that aVNS may help modulate excessive immune responses in COVID-19 cases, potentially reducing disease severity. However, the authors emphasize the need for further research to validate these benefits specifically in the context of SARS-CoV-2 infection and to develop optimal protocols for its application.

The study calls for larger clinical trials before solid recommendations can be made about using aVNS for COVID-19 treatment. The paper was co-authored by Sant'Anna FM, LBS, RCLR, MBS, RBSP, EK, SLMC, LWC, and JCS.

Title: Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation as a Novel Approach for Inflammation Treatment in COVID-19

Published in: Revista da Associacao Medica Brasileira (2023) Link: https://doi.org/10.1590/1806-9282.20230345

The review article discusses the potential of auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS) as a novel approach for managing inflammation associated with COVID-19. The authors investigate the underlying mechanisms of aVNS, its benefits in various diseases, and its potential application in treating severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection.

The study explores the effects of aVNS on neuroendocrine immune regulation, inflammatory cytokines, and oxidative stress, all of which are critical factors in COVID-19 pathogenesis. It also examines the safety, efficacy, and feasibility of aVNS in clinical trials for other diseases such as epilepsy, depression, and migraines.

The authors suggest that aVNS may help modulate the excessive immune response observed in severe COVID-19 cases, which could potentially reduce disease severity and improve outcomes. They call for further research to validate the potential benefits of aVNS specifically in the context of SARS-CoV-2 infection and to develop optimal protocols for its application.

The study was conducted by Fernando Mendes Sant’Anna, Rodrigo César Lima Resende, Lucas Bonacossa Sant’Anna, Sérgio Lívio Menezes Couceiro, Romero Bonacossa Soares Pinto, Mariana Bonacossa Sant’Anna, Liaw Wen Chao, Jozsef Constantin Szeles, and Eugenijus Kaniusas.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the scientific paper discusses auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS) as a potential new analgesic and anti-inflammatory intervention. Both cervical VNS and aVNS have comparable physiological effects with favorable therapeutic results. Pre-clinical studies show aVNS can reduce inflammation in rats, prevent shock, and reduce inflammation in colitis models.

The article presents three types of aVNS using a cutaneous electrical stimulation device: burst stimuli at 20-25 Hz, continuous stimuli at 1 Hz, and a new burst concept with a fast pulse sequence within a 1 Hz period for more effective Aβ fiber excitation. Electrodes or needles are placed on the superior concha or both the superior and inferior concha for stimulation, which should be performed daily for hospitalized patients, with at least 3-hour intervals between sessions.

While pre-clinical studies show promising results, more research is needed to understand aVNS's benefits and risks in human subjects, particularly for COVID-19 treatment. The paper proposes that taVNS may have a promising role in treating inflammation associated with COVID-19 but emphasizes the need for larger clinical trials before solid recommendations can be made about its use. The paper is co-authored by Sant'Anna FM, LBS, RCLR, MBS, RBSP, EK, SLMC, LWC, and JCS.
