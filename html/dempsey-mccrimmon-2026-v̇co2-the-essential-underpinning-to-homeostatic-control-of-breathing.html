Final Summary

Title: Chemoreception and Breathing Regulation across Scales

Research Question: The study investigates the role of carbon dioxide partial pressure in arterial blood (V_CO2) in homeostatic control of breathing across various biological scales, particularly during metabolic changes or environmental conditions.

Methods: The authors review existing literature on chemoreception, respiratory control mechanisms, and the impact of V_CO2 on breath rate and depth among vertebrates, invertebrates, single-celled organisms, and various physiological conditions.

Key Findings: CO2/H+ chemoreception plays a crucial role in compensatory feedback regulation of breathing during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep. Contrary to the belief that chemoreception of PaCO2 is the primary regulator of homeostasis, the study suggests a role for pulmonary CO2 exchange in maintaining ventilatory control. This is evidenced by adjustments in ventilation when alveolar ventilation (V_A) or dead space/tidal volume ratio (Vd/VT) are altered, or the resting PaCO2 set point is changed.

Conclusions: The study proposes that V_CO2-mediated mechanisms serve as a dedicated tracking system for alveolar ventilation, contributing significantly to traditional models of homeostatic ventilatory control. The authors call for renewed investigation into the mediation of V_CO2 as a key component of PaCO2 homeostasis using modern neuroscience approaches.

PREAMBLE

Title: Chemoreception and Breathing Regulation across Scales

Authors: Jerome A. Dempsey (University of Wisconsin School of Medicine and Public Health), Donald R. McCrimmon (Northwestern University Feinberg School of Medicine)

In this review, Dempsey and McCrimmon discuss the vital role of V_CO2 (carbon dioxide partial pressure in arterial blood) in homeostatic control of breathing across various biological scales. They emphasize that V_CO2 is a crucial factor influencing breath rate and depth, particularly during metabolic changes or environmental conditions.

The authors outline the mechanisms of chemoreception (the process by which organisms perceive chemical changes) and its impact on respiratory control. They also discuss the differences in chemosensitivity among vertebrates, invertebrates, and even single-celled organisms, as well as the physiological implications of these variations.

The review highlights recent findings related to the interplay between V_CO2, pH, and respiratory drive, shedding light on the complex regulatory processes that maintain homeostasis in breathing. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding V_CO2's role in chemoreception and its implications for various biological systems.

ABSTRACT

The abstract discusses the regulatory mechanism of Partial Pressure of Carbon Dioxide (PaCO2) during normal and increased breathing (hyperpnea). The paper argues that CO2/H+ chemoreception plays a crucial role in compensatory feedback regulation of breathing, particularly during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep. Tonic contributions to respiratory drive originate from the carotid chemoreceptors and retrotrapezoid nucleus (RTN).

However, the paper challenges the notion that chemoreception of PCO2 is the primary regulator of PaCO2 homeostasis, citing conditions where precise PaCO2 control exists despite subnormal or nonexistent ventilatory responsiveness to raised PCO2.

Instead, it suggests a role for pulmonary CO2 exchange in homeostatic ventilatory control, as evidenced by adjustments in ventilation when alveolar ventilation (V_A) or dead space/tidal volume ratio (Vd/VT) are altered, or the resting PaCO2 set point is changed. These changes allow for near-proportional V_A:V_CO2 linkages at rest and exercise without coincident changes in CO2 chemoreception.

The paper proposes that this V_CO2-mediated mechanism serves as a dedicated tracking system for alveolar ventilation, contributing significantly to traditional models of homeostatic ventilatory control. Potential mediators of these V_CO2 effects, such as the RTN, are suggested but remain inadequately explored. The paper calls for renewed investigation into the mediation of V_CO2 as a key component of PaCO2 homeostasis using modern neuroscience approaches.

INTRODUCTION

The combined introduction of the scientific paper discusses the role of carbon dioxide (CO2) in regulating air-breathing eupneic partial pressure of CO2 (PaCO2). While CO2 chemoreception may not be essential for maintaining normal PaCO2 levels during physiologic conditions, it plays a crucial role in regulating breathing stability during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep.

The text emphasizes the importance of ventilation (V_E) and carbon dioxide production (V_CO2) as key factors in maintaining near-proportional relationships under both eupnea and hyperpnea. This relationship, known as the V_A:V_CO2 linkage, is thought to be regulated by various mechanisms such as pulmonary sensors of CO2 exchange, the Retrotrapezoid Nucleus (RTN), adaptive control/associative learning, and multiple inputs from chemosensitive and non-chemosensitive signals.

The authors encourage further investigation into this complex physiological process by the respiratory neuroscience community, acknowledging the contributions of several professionals in the field. The paper was published in AJP-Lung Cell Mol Physiol with the DOI: 10.1152/ajplung.00141.2026.
