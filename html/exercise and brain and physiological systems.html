Final Summary

The scientific paper explores the interplay between various organ systems (skeletal muscle, heart, liver, adipose tissue, spleen) and the brain, highlighting how pathological changes in these organs can lead to cognitive decline and neurological diseases such as Alzheimer's disease (AD). Conversely, brain diseases can affect peripheral organ functions.

The study finds that heart failure is associated with cognitive impairment, while congenital heart disease in children leads to cognitive deficits due to chronic cerebral hypoperfusion. Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) impacts the blood-brain barrier and promotes AD progression, and dysfunctional adipose tissue alters cerebral energy metabolism in stroke patients. Splenectomy accelerates AD progression, exacerbating neuroinflammation, Aβ deposition, and tau hyperphosphorylation.

The research emphasizes the potential benefits of physical exercise on these organ-brain axes. Exercise engages neural and endocrine pathways, improves cardiorespiratory fitness, regulates lipid metabolism, epigenetic states, and secretes bioactive factors like irisin (improving neural plasticity) and lactate (promoting neuroplasticity and cognitive function).

The study suggests that further research is needed to fully understand these interactions and their implications for human brain health and neurological disorders. The paper acknowledges BioRender for creating the figures in the study.
