Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The 'preamble (combined)' section of the scientific paper discusses the organ-brain axis, a bidirectional communication system between the brain and peripheral organs. This axis is significant in understanding how pathological changes in organs like skeletal muscle, heart, liver, and visceral fat can impact the brain, as well as how brain diseases affect these organs.

Exercise plays a crucial role in reinforcing the heart-brain axis, liver-brain axis, and gut-brain axis to regulate neurological disorders such as Alzheimer's disease (AD) and depression. Exerkines, signaling molecules produced by exercising tissues, cross the blood-brain barrier and exert direct or indirect effects on the brain.

Key exerkines mentioned include irisin from skeletal muscle, which enhances neural plasticity, reduces inflammation, and promotes neurogenesis; and lactate, a metabolic intermediate that activates the SIRT1–PGC-1α–FNDC5 signaling pathway to promote neuroplasticity and improve cognitive function.
