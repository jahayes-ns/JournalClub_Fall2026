Final Summary

This study investigated the effect of ventral cervical electrical stimulation (EES) on respiratory activity during opioid-induced respiratory depression in anesthetized humans undergoing anterior cervical spinal impingement surgeries. The research question aimed to determine if mild to moderate intensity, ventral cervical EES at frequencies below 100 Hz could reset the respiratory rhythm, change tidal volume, respiratory frequency, or end-tidal PCO2 during opioid-induced respiratory depression.

The methods employed involved 24 patients receiving remifentanil anesthesia, with EES delivered to the ventral spinal cord at different amplitudes and intensities between C3 and C7. Patient safety was prioritized, and statistical analyses included mixed effects models and paired comparisons adjusted for multiple comparisons.

Key findings revealed that ventral cervical EES failed to produce any significant impact on respiratory activity during both the ON-state (when respiratory activity was present) and OFF-state (remifentanil-induced complete suppression of respiration). Despite an increase in end-tidal PCO2, no increased respiratory activity or EMG responses were observed during stimulation.

In conclusion, the study suggests that mild to moderate intensity, ventral cervical EES at frequencies below 100 Hz may not directly access or indirectly activate ventral motor neurons during opioid-induced respiratory depression. Further research is needed to explore other potential mechanisms and stimulation parameters for ventral cervical EES in modulating respiratory activity during opioid-induced respiratory depression.
