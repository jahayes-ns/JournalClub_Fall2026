Title: Ventral cervical EES in opioid-induced respiratory depression

This study investigates the effectiveness of ventral cervical epidural electrical stimulation (EES) on opioid-induced respiratory depression during anesthesia with remifentanil. The research involved 24 patients undergoing anterior cervical spinal cord surgery and stimulated different levels of the cervical spinal cord (C3-C7). However, ventral cervical EES failed to reset respiratory rhythm or significantly alter tidal volume, respiratory frequency, or end-tidal PCO2.

The study reveals that dorsal cervical EES augmented respiratory activity and increased ventilation during opioid-induced respiratory depression in a previous study. The findings suggest that ventral cervical EES at the tested frequencies and field strength may not access or augment ventral respiratory motor neurons during opioid-induced respiratory depression, requiring higher current strengths or frequencies for activation.

INTRODUCTION

This scientific study titled "Ventral cervical EES in opioid-induced respiratory depression" investigates the impact of ventral cervical epidural electrical stimulation (EES) on opioid-induced respiratory depression, a potentially life-threatening complication during anesthesia with opioids.

The study involved 24 patients receiving remifentanil for cervical spinal surgeries. The researchers stimulated different levels of the cervical spinal cord (C3-C7) using a monopolar electrode, monitoring changes in respiration during 'ON-State' (spontaneous breathing under low doses of remifentanil) and 'OFF-State' (high doses of remifentanil to suppress endogenous respiratory rhythm).

The experiment protocol includes administering remifentanil, electrical stimulation methods, vital sign monitoring, EEG recordings, video capture, and a masking procedure to reduce bias. The study will employ a mixed effects model for statistical analysis, with each subject serving as their own control through SHAM stimulation. However, due to surgical limitations, complete stimulation under all conditions may not be achievable in every subject at every cervical location.

In summary, this experiment aims to explore the impact of electrical stimulation of the ventral cervical spine on respiratory activity and brain wave patterns in subjects under remifentanil sedation.

RESULTS

In this study, 24 patients who underwent anterior cervical spinal cord surgery were evaluated for ventral cervical EES in opioid-induced respiratory depression. The patients' ages ranged from 30 to 77 years, with the most common diagnoses being disc degeneration and disc herniation.

The degree of T2 attenuation at the site of stenosis and the severity of stenosis were scored in each patient using MRI evaluation. The responses to ventral cervical stimulation during both the ON-state (when respiratory activity was present) and OFF-state (when respiratory activity was suppressed by increased remifentanil doses) were assessed for changes in tidal volume, respiratory frequency, and end-tidal PCO2.

In the ON-state, there were no consistent activations of respiratory activity or EMG responses to ventral cervical epidural spinal cord stimulation. In the OFF-state, epidural stimulation had no impact on respiratory activity at any spinal level tested, and there was no increased respiratory activity during stimulation.

The vital signs remained stable throughout the OFF-state studies, and there were no changes in SpO2, heart rate, systolic blood pressure, or diastolic blood pressure at any level of stimulation. However, limited vital sign data is available from the ON-state due to a technical failure.
