Review on Extracellular Spike Waveforms: Morphology, Biophysics, and Classification Strategies

Shi Hai Sun, Ibbotson

Keywords: biophysics, electrophysiology, extracellular recording, neuronal classification, spike waveforms

Final Summary

The paper "Extracellular Waveforms: Morphology and Biophysics" by Sun and Ibbotson investigates various extracellular spike waveforms in neurons, categorizing them into negative-spiking (standard negative spikes, triphasic spikes, compound spikes) and positive-spiking waveforms (standard positive biphasic spikes, high-amplitude positive spikes). Factors like membrane properties, ion channels, and synaptic input influence these diverse shapes.

The research uses both traditional classification methods focusing on specific features and recent advancements employing multi-dimensional approaches with detailed voltage sampling points for precise cell type assessment. However, the paper acknowledges challenges in waveform classification due to the necessity of single-unit isolation via spike sorting for reliable classifications. It discusses unsupervised and supervised techniques for classification, pointing out their benefits, limitations, and the need for standardized frameworks to enhance reproducibility and facilitate cross-study comparisons.

Looking ahead, the paper suggests efforts to improve understanding of extracellular spike biophysics, clarify roles of rare waveform types, utilize waveform features as indicators of tissue health, and address current sampling biases in the field.
