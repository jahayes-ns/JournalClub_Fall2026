Final Summary

This scientific paper investigates Nobiletin (NOB), a naturally occurring compound found in citrus peels, as a potential circadian clock amplitude enhancer that combats metabolic syndrome. The research question focuses on the effect of NOB on energy homeostasis and behavior in mice fed a high-fat diet (HFD).

The study employed high-throughput chemical screens and experiments on mice to determine that NOB improves circadian clock transcript oscillations, restores clock protein rhythms in the liver, and modulates the expression of metabolism-related genes. The key finding is that NOB functions as an agonist for Retinoid-related orphan receptors (RORs) in the circadian oscillator, which when administered to diet-induced obese (DIO) mice, counters metabolic syndrome by augmenting energy expenditure and locomotor activity. In db/db mutant mice, NOB's mitigating effects on metabolic disorders require the clock.

The research concludes that NOB shows potential as a pharmacological intervention for combating metabolic disease via the circadian gene network. The study suggests benefits for treating diseases like mood and sleep disorders, as well as aging-related conditions with dampened circadian amplitude. The paper was revised on February 1, 2016, and accepted on March 14, 2016.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

In a high-throughput chemical screen, researchers identified Nobiletin (NOB), a naturally occurring compound found in citrus peels, as a circadian clock amplitude enhancer. This small molecule protects against metabolic syndrome in a Clock gene-dependent manner. The study found that NOB is an agonist for the ROR nuclear receptors in the circadian oscillator, and when administered to diet-induced obese (DIO) mice, it counteracted metabolic syndrome by augmenting energy expenditure and locomotor activity. In db/db mutant mice, NOB's mitigating effects on metabolic disorders required the clock. In DIO mouse liver, NOB enhanced clock protein levels and elicited gene expression remodeling. The research suggests a pharmacological intervention that enhances circadian rhythms to combat metabolic disease via the circadian gene network.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction discusses the circadian clock system present in most living organisms, which is responsible for anticipating and adapting to daily environmental changes. In mammals, this system is organized hierarchically with the suprachiasmatic nuclei (SCN) serving as the central pacemaker coordinating peripheral tissue clocks. The cell-autonomous molecular oscillator, composed of interlocked feedback loops, generates molecular rhythms and regulates Bmal1 expression for stability and robustness. This oscillator drives tissue-specific gene expression throughout the circadian cycle, impacting metabolism. Metabolic disturbances such as glucose intolerance and hyperlipidemia have been linked to circadian misalignment in humans. Genetic studies reveal overlapping metabolic deficiencies in clock-disrupted mice like ClockD19/D19, exhibiting metabolic disorders including obesity and hyperglycemia.

Recent research explores manipulating circadian rhythms to alleviate the metabolic syndrome, with strategies including time-restricted intake of high-fat diets and pharmacological approaches using clock-modulating small molecules. One such natural compound identified is Nobiletin, which enhances the clock and offers protection against metabolic syndrome in a Clock gene-dependent manner. The introduction also mentions RORs as potential direct targets of NOB.

RESULTS

The study explores the impact of a compound named NOB on energy homeostasis and behavior in mice fed a high-fat diet (HFD). Results show that NOB improves circadian clock transcript oscillations and restores clock protein rhythms in the liver, and modulates the expression of metabolism-related genes like Cidec/Fsp27, Pdk4, and Pkm2. NOB was initially identified as an inhibitor of RORgt but instead activates it, suggesting a complex relationship with ROR family receptors.

In this study, NOB binds to the LBDs of RORa and RORg with higher affinity for RORg, and increases Bmal1 promoter-driven luciferase activity in the presence of RORa or RORg. This enhancing activity requires Rora/c genes encoding RORa/g. Treatment with NOB improves glucose and lipid homeostasis in DIO mice, lowering blood insulin levels, total TG and TC levels in blood and liver, and reducing liver fat accumulation compared to vehicle-treated mice. The study was conducted on 8-15 mice at two time points.

DISCUSSION

This scientific article reports two main findings:

  1. NOB as a clock-enhancing agent: NOB (likely Naringenin Butyrate) has been identified as a compound that extends the period of the circadian clock in mouse fibroblast cells by activating Retinoid-related orphan receptors (RORs). Experiments on mice showed improvements in metabolic parameters such as glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, and energy expenditure in diet-induced obese mice treated with NOB. The study suggests potential benefits for treating diseases like mood and sleep disorders, as well as aging-related conditions with dampened circadian amplitude.

  2. Autophagy regulation and lipid droplet remodeling: The research project investigated the role of autophagy in regulating lipid droplet (LD) remodeling. The findings showed that autophagy inhibition led to an increase in LDs, which was reversed by autophagy activation. Key proteins involved were identified, including ATG9A, SQSTM1/p62, and LC3.

Both studies used various experimental methods, including chemical screens, mouse treatments, metabolic analyses, microarray analysis, immunofluorescence, transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and lipidomics analysis. The data from these studies have been deposited in the National Center for Biotechnology Information's Gene Expression Omnibus under accession number GSE78848.
