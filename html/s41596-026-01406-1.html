Final Summary

Title: Head-Fixed Drug Self-Administration in Mice

The authors of this protocol article describe a cost-effective method for training head-fixed mice to self-administer drugs using a low-cost, 3D-printed equipment setup and the open-source software suite REACHER/Labrynth. The objective is to facilitate research on drug reward and motivation in rodents using head fixation systems, which allows for precise control of experimental variables.

Key steps include habituating mice to the head fixation apparatus, training mice to associate a specific nose poke with drug delivery (e.g., intranasal cocaine), using operant conditioning to shape the nose poke behavior, validating the self-administration procedure, and implementing experimental manipulations such as modulating the dose of the drug or changing the reward schedule. The protocol provides detailed instructions for setting up the equipment, as well as troubleshooting tips to ensure successful implementation.

The authors expect that their method will facilitate further research on drug reward and motivation using head-fixed mice, making it compatible with neurotechnologies requiring mechanical stability, such as two-photon calcium imaging and holographic optogenetics, allowing the study of neurobiological mechanisms underlying substance use disorders with unprecedented resolution.

The paper provides guidelines for implanting jugular vein catheters in mice and conducting head-fixed drug self-administration experiments, specifically for heroin. The work was supported by multiple grants and was conceptualized by E.M.D. and J.M.O., with methods developed and designed by E.M.D., R.E.C., A.G.G., and J.B. Experiments were performed and data collected by a team, with the initial draft written by E.M.D. and A.G.G., and all authors reviewing and revising the manuscript. The paper is published in Nature Protocols.

ABSTRACT

The scientific paper presents a detailed protocol for building low-cost, head-fixed operant chambers using 3D-printed equipment and an open-source software suite (REACHER/Labrynth) for drug and non-drug self-administration in mice. This setup makes drug self-administration compatible with neurotechnologies requiring mechanical stability, such as two-photon calcium imaging and holographic optogenetics, allowing the study of neurobiological mechanisms underlying substance use disorders with unprecedented resolution.

INTRODUCTION

The scientific paper introduces a head-fixed drug self-administration paradigm for monitoring and manipulating neurons during drug seeking in mice. It provides instructions on constructing the necessary equipment for this setup, including sound-attenuating chambers, lever apparatuses, infusion pumps, Ethernet jack plug boxes, and more. The protocol is designed to be cost-effective, using open-source software called Rodent Experiment Application Controls and Handling Ecosystem for Research (REACHER) with a user-friendly Graphical User Interface (Labrynth).

The paper also provides guidelines for implanting jugular vein catheters in mice and conducting head-fixed drug self-administration experiments, specifically for heroin. This includes details on the experimental timeline, session designs, and considerations for designing and running these types of experiments. Troubleshooting solutions are also provided for various issues that may arise during a drug self-administration study in head-fixed mice, such as catheter problems, animal behavior issues, positioning problems, and timing issues.
