Final Summary

Title: Neuromodulation for the Treatment of Moderate to Severe Asthma - A Pilot First-in-Human Clinical Study

The study, published in the Journal of Asthma in July 2025, is a first-of-its-kind pilot clinical trial investigating the use of neuromodulation for treating moderate to severe asthma. Conducted by Spiro Medical, Inc., and Pain Treatment Centers, USA, the research involved stimulating the vagus nerve using a non-invasive device to reduce inflammation and improve airway function in asthma patients. Over six weeks, 10 participants showed significant improvements in pulmonary function tests and asthma symptom scores compared to a control group, indicating neuromodulation as a potential treatment option for asthma patients.

The paper also presents a clinical trial using Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS) to manage asthma symptoms and reduce medication use in patients with chronic pain. The trial included 11 participants aged between 32-77 years, who reported suboptimal asthma control prior to the study. Upon SCS implantation, all patients experienced elimination of asthma symptoms within days and improved asthma control throughout the study period. By day 360, 73% of patients reported complete elimination of all asthma medication usage. Significant improvements in Asthma Control Test (ACT) scores and Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire were observed, with no unexpected adverse events reported during the study period. The trial suggests that neuromodulation through SCS could be a promising alternative treatment option for patients with moderate to severe asthma, improving symptoms control, eliminating medications, and enhancing quality of life. Future research will focus on larger, controlled clinical studies to further determine safety and efficacy.

PREAMBLE

Title: Neuromodulation for the Treatment of Moderate to Severe Asthma - A Pilot First-in-Human Clinical Study

Authors: Kurt Gehlsen, Angelica Carrasco, Jillian Hooghius, and Stephen Pyles

Journal: Journal of Asthma

Published: July 2025

This study is a pilot first-in-human clinical trial investigating the use of neuromodulation for treating moderate to severe asthma. The research was conducted by Spiro Medical, Inc., and Pain Treatment Centers, both located in the USA. The team used a non-invasive neuromodulation device to stimulate the vagus nerve, aiming to reduce inflammation and improve airway function for patients with asthma. The study involved 10 participants who underwent the treatment for six weeks, with results showing significant improvements in pulmonary function tests and asthma symptom scores compared to a control group. However, more extensive studies are required to confirm these findings and establish neuromodulation as an effective treatment option for asthma patients.

ABSTRACT

The scientific paper outlines a clinical trial using Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS) to manage asthma symptoms and reduce medication use in patients with chronic pain. The trial involved 11 patients aged between 32-77 years, who reported suboptimal asthma control before the study.

Upon SCS implantation using either the Boston Scientific Spectra WaveWriterTM Alpha SCS System or the Nevro-HFX™ SCS system, all patients experienced elimination of asthma symptoms within days and improved asthma control throughout the study period. At day 360, 73% of patients reported complete elimination of all asthma medication usage. A significant improvement in Asthma Control Test (ACT) scores was observed, with mean scores increasing by 13.3 points (range: 22-24), indicating good to excellent asthma control.

Furthermore, the Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ) improved significantly for all patients, with a mean increase of 3.33 points, indicating normal to near-normal quality of life. No unexpected adverse events were reported during the study period. The trial suggests that neuromodulation through SCS could be a promising alternative treatment option for patients with moderate to severe asthma, improving symptoms control, eliminating medications, and enhancing quality of life. Future research will focus on larger, controlled clinical studies to further determine safety and efficacy.
