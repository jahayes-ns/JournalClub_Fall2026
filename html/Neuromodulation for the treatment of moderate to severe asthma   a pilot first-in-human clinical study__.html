Neuromodulation for the treatment of moderate to severe asthma – a pilot first-in-human clinical study

Final Summary

This pilot study, published in the Journal of Asthma (2025), explores the application of Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS) as a novel treatment for managing moderate to severe asthma. Conducted by Spiro Medical, Inc and Pain Treatment Centers in the USA, the research involved eleven participants who had poor asthma control despite medication use, with an initial Asthma Control Test (ACT) score average of 8. After SCS implantation, subjects demonstrated significant improvement in asthma control at day 360, achieving a mean ACT score of 23 and complete asthma control in all patients. Additionally, there was a notable enhancement in quality-of-life, with an average increase of 3.33 points on the Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ). No unexpected adverse events occurred during SCS implantation. The study suggests that neuromodulation using SCS could be a promising alternative for managing moderate to severe asthma, potentially achieving a clinical remission rate of 73%. Future research, including larger, controlled clinical trials, will confirm these findings and evaluate the long-term safety and efficacy of SCS as an asthma treatment.

This study is a pilot, first-in-human clinical trial investigating the use of neuromodulation for treating moderate to severe asthma. The research was conducted by Spiro Medical, Inc and Pain Treatment Centers in the USA. A small group of participants underwent non-invasive neuromodulation treatments over a specified period. The study aims to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of this new treatment approach for managing asthma symptoms.

ABSTRACT

In a small-scale study, Spinal Cord Stimulation (SCS) was used to manage moderate to severe asthma in eleven patients. All patients had poor asthma control prior to treatment despite using various medications, with an average Asthma Control Test (ACT) score of 8. After SCS implantation, patients showed significant improvement in asthma control at day 360, with a mean ACT score of 23 and complete asthma control in 100% of the patients. Quality-of-life also significantly improved, with an average increase of 3.33 points on the Asthma Quality of Life Questionnaire (AQLQ). No unexpected adverse events were reported during SCS implantation. The study suggests that neuromodulation using SCS could be a promising alternative for managing moderate to severe asthma, with a potential clinical remission rate of 73%. Further research is planned to confirm these findings and evaluate the safety and efficacy of SCS in a larger, controlled clinical trial.
