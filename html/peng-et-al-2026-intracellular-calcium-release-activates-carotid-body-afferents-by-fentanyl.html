Title: Fentanyl Activates Carotid Body Afferents via KOR-Gaq-PLC-IP3R-Ca2+ Signaling Pathway

Research Question: The study aims to elucidate the cellular mechanisms underlying carotid body afferent activation by fentanyl, focusing on how Fentanyl elevates intracellular calcium concentration in glomus cells and the involved signaling cascade.

Methods: Adult Sprague–Dawley rats were used to investigate the effects of fentanyl on carotid body sensory nerve activity and intracellular calcium concentration in glomus cells, employing electrophysiology, immunoprecipitation assays, colorimetric assays, and competitive ELISA.

Key Findings: The research reveals that fentanyl activates carotid body afferents via a KOR-Gaq-PLC-IP3R-Ca2+ signaling pathway, which increases intracellular calcium levels in glomus cells and counteracts opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD). Blocking any component of this pathway abolished both the fentanyl-induced increase in intracellular calcium and associated carotid body afferent nerve activation.

Conclusions: The findings offer novel insights into the mechanism underlying opioids' respiratory depression effects, providing potential targets for developing new therapies to mitigate undesirable side-effects associated with opioid use in anesthesia and pain management. This study highlights a previously unknown interaction between opioids and oxygen-sensing circuits, which may help reduce opioid-related respiratory complications and enhance opioid safety.

Title: Intracellular calcium release activates carotid body afferents by fentanyl

Authors: Ying-Jie Peng, Oren Kabil, Ning Wang, Zheng Xie, Aaron P. Fox, Nanduri R. Prabhakar (The University of Chicago)

Key Findings: The study investigates the mechanism by which fentanyl, an opioid analgesic, activates carotid body afferents (nerve fibers sensitive to oxygen levels), leading to altered respiratory responses.

Methods: The research employed a combination of electrophysiology and molecular biology techniques in rat carotid body cells and isolated afferent nerve fibers. Fentanyl's effects on intracellular calcium release, ion channel activity, and gene expression were examined.

Data: Results indicate that fentanyl activates carotid body afferents by increasing intracellular calcium levels through the activation of L-type voltage-gated calcium channels (LTCCs) and transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1) channels. Additionally, fentanyl upregulates the expression of genes related to these channels, suggesting a prolonged effect on carotid body cells.

Implications: These findings offer novel insights into the mechanism underlying opioids' respiratory depression effects, providing potential targets for developing new therapies to mitigate undesirable side-effects associated with opioid use in anesthesia and pain management.

ABSTRACT

The study investigates the cellular mechanisms underlying carotid body (CB) afferent activation by fentanyl. The research suggests that calcium signaling in glomus cells mediates CB activation by fentanyl. Findings show that fentanyl increases intracellular calcium levels even in the absence of extracellular calcium, and this response is abolished upon thapsigargin treatment, indicating internal Ca2+ stores are involved.

In human embryonic kidney cells expressing KOR and G protein alpha q subunit (Gaq), fentanyl promotes KOR-Gaq complex formation and stimulates phospholipase C (PLC), leading to an increase in IP3 levels in the CB. Pharmacological blockade of KOR, Gaq, PLC, or IP3 receptors prevents both the rise in intracellular calcium and CB afferent activation. In summary, a new KOR-Gaq-PLC-IP3R-Ca2+ signaling pathway in glomus cells was identified that mediates CB afferent activation by fentanyl, providing insight into the mitigation of opioid-induced respiratory depression.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the scientific paper discusses Fentanyl's use in pain management and anesthesia, its potential for causing fatal respiratory depression known as Opioid-induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD), and the mechanisms involved. OIRD is suggested to be a result of both inhibition of brainstem respiratory neurons and activation of pulmonary C fibers. The study also explores the role of the carotid bodies in monitoring oxygen levels, stating that fentanyl activates carotid body afferents via kappa-opioid receptors (KORs), and that this activation can counteract opioid-induced respiratory depression.

The research then focuses on the cellular mechanisms underlying carotid body afferent activation by fentanyl, specifically investigating how Fentanyl elevates intracellular calcium concentration in glomus cells, the primary chemosensory elements of the carotid body. The study found that fentanyl releases calcium from intracellular stores in glomus cells via a signaling cascade involving kappa-opioid receptors (KOR), Gaq, phospholipase C (PLC), and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) receptors. Blocking any component of this pathway abolished both the fentanyl-induced increase in intracellular calcium and associated carotid body afferent nerve activation.

METHODS

In the study, adult Sprague–Dawley rats were used to investigate the effects of fentanyl on carotid body sensory nerve activity and intracellular calcium concentration in glomus cells.

Carotid body sensory nerve activity was recorded ex vivo from urethane-anesthetized rats, while primary glomus cell cultures were prepared by enzymatic dissociation of carotid bodies followed by plating onto collagen-coated coverslips. The intracellular calcium concentration in glomus cells was measured using Fura-2 AM dye and a Leica microscope.

To assess the effects of fentanyl, both carotid body sensory nerve activity and intracellular calcium concentration were recorded before and during exposure to the drug. Additionally, an immunoprecipitation assay was performed on HEK293 cells expressing kappa opioid receptor (OPRK1) and Gaq to investigate the interaction between fentanyl and these proteins.

The effects of fentanyl on phospholipase C (PLC) activity and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP3) levels were also measured in carotid bodies using colorimetric assays and competitive ELISA, respectively. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism and SigmaPlot.

RESULTS

The provided text is a scientific article about the effects of fentanyl on the carotid body and glomus cells, which are responsible for chemoreception in vertebrates. Fentanyl is a powerful opioid analgesic that has been shown to activate carotid body afferents, leading to increased breathing rates and blood pressure.

The article discusses the signaling pathways involved in fentanyl-induced activation of glomus cells. It explains how fentanyl binds to kappa opioid receptors (KOR) on these cells, triggering a series of events that result in an increase in intracellular calcium ([Ca2+]i).

The findings suggest a novel respiratory control mechanism that may help reduce opioid-related respiratory complications and enhance opioid safety.
