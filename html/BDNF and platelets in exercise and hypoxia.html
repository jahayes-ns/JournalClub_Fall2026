Final Summary

This scientific study, published in The Journal of Physiology (2025), investigates the impact of exercise on Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) and Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) release at both sea level and high altitude. The research involved 12 healthy participants performing exhaustive cycling exercises at each altitude.

The key findings suggest that maximal exercise doubled free BDNF levels in both normoxia and hypoxia, with no significant change in total BDNF or platelet saturation. Hypoxia also promoted free BDNF release from the brain, while exercise led to platelet aggregation. The study concludes that platelets play a significant role in liberating BDNF during exercise and hypoxia.

PREAMBLE

The study published in The Journal of Physiology (2025) explores the stimulation of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and platelet factor 4 (PF4) release by exercise at sea level and high altitude. Twelve healthy adults participated, performing exhaustive exercise at both levels.

At sea level, maximal exercise doubled free BDNF with no difference between arterial and venous circulations. At high altitude, a net release of BDNF was observed at rest, and exercise caused a similar doubling in free BDNF. Platelet concentration decreased by a third across the brain during exercise at high altitude, and PF4 release was strongly correlated with BDNF release, suggesting platelet activation contributes to the neurotrophic effect of exercise. This research indicates that platelets play a significant role in liberating BDNF during exercise and hypoxia.

INTRODUCTION

This study investigates the primary source of circulating Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) in the human brain, and how it is affected by exercise and hypoxia. The research design involves 12 healthy individuals participating in a repeated-measures cross-over experiment, involving high-intensity cycling at sea level and high altitude (3800 m). Blood samples are collected for BDNF and Platelet Factor 4 (PF4) analysis to measure brain's trans-cerebral exchange of these substances during exercise. The study hypothesizes that the brain is the primary source of circulating BDNF, with release enhanced by platelet activation through cerebral circulation.

The research quantifies total BDNF (sBDNF), free BDNF (pBDNF) from platelet-poor plasma, and BDNF per platelet (BDNF-per-platelet). Additionally, PF4 is measured as an index of platelet activation across the cerebral circulation. Simultaneous measurements of internal carotid and vertebral artery diameters and velocities were obtained using duplex ultrasound in a separate study involving 10 participants to account for regional differences in CBF, with data analysis including linear mixed-effects models, Pearson's correlations, and ANCOVA. The study aims to assess the effects of light cycling, exercise, and altitude on biomarkers such as pBDNF, sBDNF, BDNF-per-platelet, PF4, and cross-brain exchange.

RESULTS

The study investigates the effects of maximal exercise on brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and platelet-activating factor 4 (PF4) in normoxia (sea level) and hypoxia (high altitude).

In normoxia, maximal exercise led to a doubling of free BDNF with no significant changes in total BDNF or platelet saturation. PF4 also doubled during exercise with no difference between arterial and venous circulation. At recovery, free BDNF returned to near-baseline levels.

In hypoxia, free BDNF was higher in venous blood than arterial, indicating release from the brain. The response of free BDNF to exercise at high altitude was similar to that at sea level. PF4 also showed a pattern similar to free BDNF, with consistent greater concentrations in jugular venous circulation suggesting platelet activation across the cerebral circulation. Exercise led to a 26% decrease in platelet concentration and a correlation between platelet loss/aggregation and release of both PF4 and total BDNF. The study suggests that hypoxia promotes free BDNF release from the brain alongside a conserved BDNF response to exercise, while exercise leads to platelet aggregation.

DISCUSSION

This text appears to be a research article about a study investigating the role of platelets in the release and uptake of Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) during exercise and hypoxia. The study found that approximately 98% of BDNF is bound to platelets, and exercise and hypoxia stimulate platelet activation, aggregation, and the selective release of their contents, including BDNF. The study also found that PF4, an index of platelet activation, was elevated in hypoxia to a greater extent on traversing the brain and that PF4 and pBDNF show similar patterns of release across the brain, providing evidence that both are co-released from platelets via activation within the cerebrovasculature. The study suggests that platelet activation may be at least partly responsible for the increase in free BDNF demonstrated in this study. The article also mentions the importance of exercise and BDNF in maintaining brain health, and it discusses some methodological considerations and perspectives related to the study's findings.
