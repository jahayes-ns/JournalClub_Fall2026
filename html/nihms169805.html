Research Question: This paper reviews recent advancements in understanding the fundamental aspects of breathing, focusing on the neural mechanisms that generate rhythmic patterns (rhythmicity), adaptive changes in breathing patterns (plasticity), and the role of chemoreceptors in regulating respiratory drive based on blood gas changes (chemosensitivity).

Methods: Data from various experimental models, including mammals and invertebrates, are presented to support the findings. Key Findings: The study identifies the preBötzinger Complex as a key site for generating the respiratory rhythm, discusses serotonin-dependent long-term facilitation during intermittent hypoxia, and highlights neurons with chemosensitivity spread throughout the brainstem.

Conclusions: These studies contribute significantly to our understanding of the neural mechanisms controlling breathing patterns and the potential plasticity of these systems, with an emphasis on mammalian respiratory physiology. The provided text discusses two main topics:

  1. Respiratory rhythm generation in mammals, specifically focusing on the preBotzinger complex (preBötC) and related brainstem loci. The preBötC is a network of neurons thought to play a critical role in generating the basic rhythm of breathing, but evidence suggests that other brainstem regions may also contribute to rhythmogenesis. The article highlights the importance of the preinspiratory (pre-I) network and "pacemaker neurons" within the preBötC, but recent findings suggest alternative mechanisms for respiratory rhythm generation.

  2. Long-term potentiation (LTP) in the hippocampus and central chemoreception in the brainstem. LTP is a persistent strengthening of synaptic connections in the brain that underlies learning and memory processes, while central chemoreception refers to the ability of certain neurons in the brainstem to detect changes in chemical composition, particularly CO2 levels, in the blood. The text describes several key findings related to central chemoreception, including its role in regulating breathing, methods for studying it, and the potential role of these neurons in other functions beyond chemosensitivity.
