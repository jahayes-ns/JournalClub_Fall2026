Final Summary

Title: Exercise-Induced Neural Plasticity in Mice's Mesencephalic Locomotor Region and Spinal Cord

The study by Dai et al., published on January 2nd, 2026, investigates how chronic exercise influences neural plasticity within the Mesencephalic Locomotor Region (MLR) and spinal cord in mice. The research employed a 3-week treadmill exercise regimen and various experimental methods such as voluntary wheel running and pharmacological interventions.

Key findings include: 1. Exercise increased neuronal excitability in both MLR and spinal cord, with a more significant effect on ventral spinal cord neurons. 2. Persistent inward currents (PICs) were enhanced by exercise, particularly in terms of hyperpolarizing onset voltage and increased amplitude, with stronger effects observed in spinal cord neurons. Calcium-mediated PICs affected firing duration/frequency while sodium-mediated PICs impacted threshold/capacity. 3. Exercise led to an increase in dendritic complexity within both MLR and spinal cord neurons, with a more pronounced effect observed in spinal cord neurons. 4. Ventral spinal neurons exhibited greater dendritic complexity than dorsal neurons and were more affected by exercise. 5. The study concluded that chronic exercise leads to coordinated plasticity throughout the locomotor system, with spinal circuits, particularly ventral components, demonstrating greater functional and structural adaptability than MLR. This research provides valuable insights into activity-dependent neural adaptation.

The study aims to contribute to a better understanding of how exercise influences neural mechanisms underlying motor control, potentially opening new avenues for rehabilitation strategies after neurological injuries or diseases.

Title: Exercise Induces Neural Plasticity in the Mesencephalic Locomotor Region to Spinal Cord in Mice

Author(s): Yue Dai, East China Normal University; Liming Yang, Yi Cheng, and Xinyi Wei (Corresponding Author)

Published: January 2nd, 2026 DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-8363166/v1 License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License Competing Interest: None declared

The study investigates how exercise coordinates neural plasticity from the Mesencephalic Locomotor Region (MLR) to the spinal cord in mice. The research employs various experimental methods, including voluntary wheel running and pharmacological interventions, to assess changes in motor control, locomotion, and neuromodulation within the central nervous system of mice undergoing exercise regimens. The findings demonstrate that exercise promotes neural plasticity and improves motor function by enhancing the connectivity and activity between the MLR and spinal cord networks. This study contributes to a better understanding of how exercise influences neural mechanisms underlying motor control, potentially opening new avenues for rehabilitation strategies after neurological injuries or diseases.

ABSTRACT

The abstract of the scientific paper discusses the impact of chronic exercise on neuroplasticity within the mesencephalic locomotor region (MLR) and spinal cord (SC). Three-week treadmill exercises were conducted on P42-P45 mice, and key findings include:

  1. Exercise increased neuronal excitability in both MLR and SC, with a more significant effect on ventral SC neurons.
  2. Persistent inward currents (PICs) were enhanced by exercise, particularly in terms of hyperpolarizing onset voltage and increased amplitude, with stronger effects observed in SC neurons. Calcium-mediated PICs affected firing duration/frequency while sodium-mediated PICs impacted threshold/capacity.
  3. Exercise led to an increase in dendritic complexity (total length, branch points, and terminals) within both MLR and SC neurons, with a more pronounced effect observed in SC neurons.
  4. Ventral spinal neurons exhibited greater dendritic complexity than dorsal neurons and were more affected by exercise.
  5. A correlation suggests that exercise-driven dendritic plasticity potentiated PICs and excitability, collectively promoting locomotor adaptation. The study concluded that chronic exercise leads to coordinated plasticity throughout the locomotor system, with spinal circuits, particularly ventral components, demonstrating greater functional and structural adaptability than MLR. The research provides valuable insights into activity-dependent neural adaptation.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper outlines the fundamental role of locomotion in vertebrates, initiated within the mesencephalic locomotor region (MLR) and controlled by central pattern generators (CPGs) in the spinal cord. The system generates rhythmic locomotor output and integrates feedback to adapt to dynamic environments. Adaptive plasticity in locomotor circuits is influenced by acute and chronic exercise, leading to alterations in neuronal excitability, dendritic plasticity, channel modulation, gene expression, and protein synthesis.

Recent studies on mouse midbrain and spinal cord neurons have shown that chronic exercise enhances persistent inward currents (PICs) and increases the excitability of serotonin neurons in the dorsal raphe nucleus. Similarly, treadmill exercise has been found to increase neuronal excitability, strengthen PICs, and promote dendritic plasticity in spinal cord interneurons.

The study aims to investigate the differences in the regulatory effects of exercise on neuronal excitability between MLR neurons and spinal interneurons, the simultaneous effect of exercise on PICs in both regions, synchronous promotion of neuronal dendritic plasticity, and the dominant role of either region in the adaptability of the locomotor system. The research will address these questions by subjecting mice to a 3-week treadmill exercise regimen and examining the concurrent effects on the electrophysiological, morphological, and ionic properties of MLR neurons and spinal interneurons using whole-cell patch-clamp recording. The study aims to provide insights into the cellular and ionic mechanisms underlying exercise-induced adaptation and coordination within the midbrain-spinal motor system.

RESULTS

The study summarized discusses the effects of exercise on the excitability and persistent inward currents (PICs) in spinal cord (SC) and midbrain lateral reticular (MLR) neurons. Exercise enhances both types of neurons through the modulation of calcium-activated potassium (Ca-PIC) and sodium channels (Na-PIC).

Exercise increases Ca-PIC, which lowers the current threshold for sustained firing and prolongs firing duration in both SC and MLR neurons. Enhanced Na-PIC results in a decreased voltage threshold and current threshold for sustained firing, leading to prolonged firing duration in both regions. The modulatory strength of Ca-PIC and Na-PIC is higher in SC neurons than in MLR neurons.

The study also found that exercise induces dendritic plasticity in both regions, although the specific morphological features were not detailed. Exercise enhances the excitability of ventral neurons more than dorsal neurons, with a greater reduction in rheobase (minimum stimulus intensity for repetitive action potentials) and AP half-width (duration of an action potential). The increase in total dendritic length was significantly greater in ventral neurons compared to dorsal neurons.

The findings suggest that the motor system adapts differently during exercise, with implications for rehabilitation after spinal cord injuries or neurological disorders. However, more information is needed regarding the specific methods used, species of origin for the neurons, and data analysis techniques.

Additionally, the study suggests that exercise-induced hyperpolarization of PIC onset, particularly Na-PIC onset, is the primary cause of Vth (voltage threshold) hyperpolarization. The increase in dendritic length due to exercise may result in an increase in PIC amplitude, particularly Ca-PIC amplitude. Riluzole reduces the correlation coefficients, indicating that exercise-induced hyperpolarization of Na-PIC onset and an increase in Na-PIC amplitude are potential mechanisms underlying the enhanced excitability of SC neurons. The study concludes that the increased dendritic length and enhanced regulatory function of ion channel PICs play a crucial role in the plasticity of the motor system in response to environmental changes, with SC neurons demonstrating greater plasticity than MLR neurons following exercise.

DISCUSSION

The study by Ge & Dai (2020) investigates how moderate-intensity treadmill exercise affects neuronal plasticity in the motor cortex (MLR) and spinal cord (SC). The findings suggest that exercise enhances excitability, potentiates persistent inward currents (PICs), and promotes dendritic plasticity in both regions.

In the spinal cord, ventral interneurons showed greater enhancement of excitability than dorsal interneurons, indicating a dominant role in adaptive remodeling during exercise. However, no significant changes were observed in dendritic complexity following exercise intervention.

The study used whole-cell patch-clamp recordings to assess membrane parameters and found that exercise led to increased neuronal excitability through the modulation of PICs. The persistent sodium current (Na-PIC) was blocked using riluzole, while the persistent calcium current (Ca-PIC), specifically the L-type calcium channel current (Cav1.3), was blocked by nimodipine.

Data analysis involved Microsoft Excel for data formatting and Prism for statistical analysis. The study reported no competing interests and outlined author contributions.
