Astrocytes connect specific brain regions through plastic networks

Cooper, Selles, Cammer, Redd, Gildea, Sall, Chiurri, Cheung, Wheeler, Saab, Liddelow, Chao

Final Summary

The combined study explores two research areas focusing on astrocytes and their role in various biological contexts, including neurophysiology and neurodegenerative diseases.

In the first part of the study, a new method was developed to visualize astrocyte networks using a virus vector (AAV5) to deliver a gene encoding for a biotin ligase fused with Cx43, a protein found in astrocyte gap junctions, and an HA tag for visualization. The research was validated in cultures of primary immunopanned rat astrocytes and in vivo experiments were conducted on male C57BL/6 mice injected in the motor cortex, hypothalamus, or prefrontal cortex, revealing distinct network morphologies across regions and consistent patterns of regional connectivity. Key findings include bidirectional communication across frontal cortex and hypothalamic networks.

In the second part, changes in astrocyte connectivity and neuronal dendritic arbors following whisker trimming in mice were investigated. The researchers found altered astrocyte connections and reduced neuronal dendritic arbours post-whisker trimming, with the effect extending to both horizontal and coronal sections. This study suggests that long-range astrocyte networks can exhibit structural plasticity and significantly diverge from the neuronal connectome.

The research methods encompass cell culture, western blotting, qPCR, gel electrophoresis, perfusion of mice, slice immunohistochemistry, viral vector transfection in astrocytes, primary immunopanning of astrocytes, and biotin supplementation for detection. The findings contribute to understanding the functions of astroglia and radial glia and potential therapeutic targets, as well as investigating astrocyte networks and their connections in different brain regions.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The combined preamble of the scientific papers discusses two separate studies.

The first study developed a new method to visualize astrocyte networks using a virus vector (AAV5) to deliver a gene encoding for a biotin ligase fused with Cx43, a protein found in astrocyte gap junctions, and an HA tag for visualization. The research was validated in cultures of primary immunopanned rat astrocytes and in vivo experiments were conducted on male C57BL/6 mice injected in the motor cortex, hypothalamus, or prefrontal cortex, revealing distinct network morphologies across regions and consistent patterns of regional connectivity. The study also found that most regions enriched in the frontal cortex and hypothalamic networks were enriched in both networks, suggesting bidirectional communication across these brain regions.

The second study investigates changes in astrocyte connectivity and neuronal dendritic arbors following whisker trimming in mice. The researchers found altered astrocyte connections and reduced neuronal dendritic arbours post-whisker trimming, with the effect extending to both horizontal and coronal sections. In the naive brain, astrocyte networks in the barrel cortex project to the prefrontal cortex, exhibiting limited contralateral signals, which are largely absent after whisker trimming. However, midbrain connectivity remained relatively stable. The findings suggest that long-range astrocyte networks can exhibit structural plasticity and significantly diverge from the neuronal connectome.

CONCLUSION

The study highlights various research on gap junction channels and their roles in diverse biological contexts, including neurophysiology and neurodegenerative diseases. Key findings include:

  1. Astrocytes form extensive networks throughout the brain called "astroglial metabolic networks," facilitating small molecule movement and regulating neuronal activity and energy metabolism (Valiunas et al., 2018; study mentioned).
  2. Gap junction intercellular communication in astrocytes is essential for resistance to oxidative stress (Le et al., 2014).
  3. Gap junctions have various roles in cellular processes, acting as both good samaritans and executioners (Spray et al., 2013).
  4. Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain (Xie et al., 2013).
  5. The cryo-EM structure of human Cx36/GJD2 neuronal gap junction channel was revealed (Lee et al., 2023).
  6. Connexin43 plays a role in neonatal excitatory neurons for short-term motor learning (Wang et al., 2019).
  7. Gap junctional communication occurs among developing and injured motor neurons (Chang & Balice-Gordon, 2000; Chang et al., 2000).
  8. Nerve injury induces gap junctional coupling among axotomized adult motor neurons (Chang et al., 2000).
  9. Astroglial glutamine is essential for physiological synaptic activity and recognition memory (Cheung et al., 2022).
  10. Repetitive diffuse mild traumatic brain injury causes an atypical astrocyte response leading to spontaneous recurrent seizures (Shandra et al., 2019).
  11. Astrocytic connexin43 phosphorylation contributes to seizure susceptibility after mild traumatic brain injury (Munoz-Ballester et al., 2025, preprint).
  12. Astrocytic gap junctions remain open during ischemic conditions (Cotrina et al., 1998).
  13. Astrocyte alkalization and gap junction uncoupling exacerbate epilepsy (Onodera et al., 2021).
  14. The astrocyte plays a critical role in functional remodeling in the contralateral hemisphere of somatosensory cortex after stroke (Takatsuru et al., 2013).
  15. Astrocytes in the mouse brain respond bilaterally to unilateral retinal neurodegeneration (Cooper et al., 2025, preprint).
  16. Connexin43 undergoes rapid turnover in the adult rat heart (Beardslee et al., 1998).
  17. The degradation of connexin43 occurs via the ubiquitin proteasome pathway (Laing & Beyer, 1995).
  18. Astrocyte structural plasticity regulates neural circuit function and behavior (Lawal et al., 2022).

METHODS

The scientific paper discusses two approaches to investigate the role of connexin43 (Cx43) in astroglia and radial glia. The first approach uses a Cre-LoxP system to delete Cx43 specifically in these cells, while the second approach employs an antibody that inhibits Cx43 expression.

Methodologies include cell culture, western blotting, qPCR, gel electrophoresis, perfusion of mice, slice immunohistochemistry, and viral vector transfection in astrocytes. In the second study, primary immunopanning of astrocytes was used to express Connexin 43 (Cx43) via a viral vector (AAV5-GfaABC1D-Cx43:TID:HA), followed by biotin supplementation for detection.

Key findings include altered hearing and increased spreading depression in the hippocampus due to Cx43 deletion, affected oligodendrocyte progenitor differentiation with antibody-mediated inhibition of Cx43, and specificity of astrocyte networks in various brain regions. Additionally, whisker trimming reduced the size of the astrocyte network in barrel cortex. The methods used contribute to understanding the functions of astroglia and radial glia and potential therapeutic targets, as well as investigating astrocyte networks and their connections in different brain regions.
