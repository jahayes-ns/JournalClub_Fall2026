Trends in Neurosciences: Making of BDNF: role of promoters, enhancers, and untranslated regions

Esvald, Avarlaid, Koppel, Tuvikene, Timmusk | 2026

Final Summary

This study investigates the activity-dependent control of Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) translation, focusing on its role in synaptic plasticity and neural circuit remodeling. The research question is centered around understanding the regulatory mechanisms governing BDNF gene expression, particularly transcription factors like CREB, AP1, MEF2, ATF4, EGR1, NPAS4, TBR1, and enhancers such as intronic, upstream, 237 kb, and +36 kb. The methods employed include analyzing BDNF gene expression in various brain regions and cell cultures, with a focus on its alternative splicing and modulation by neurotransmitters like dopamine, norepinephrine, and glutamate.

The key findings of the study reveal that these regulatory mechanisms are crucial for understanding BDNF's role in neurological disorders. However, several unanswered questions remain, including the specific mechanisms governing individual promoters, the balance between activation and repression, the role of antisense transcripts, and the regulatory landscape of enhancers active within specific cell types.

The conclusions suggest that understanding these complex regulatory mechanisms could potentially lead to therapeutic interventions aimed at treating neurodegenerative disorders and cognitive impairments by targeting BDNF. The study underscores the importance of further research to fully elucidate the intricate regulatory landscape of the BDNF gene in various neural contexts.
