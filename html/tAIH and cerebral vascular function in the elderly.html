Final Summary

Title: Moderate Intermittent Hypoxic Conditioning and Cerebrovascular Function in the Elderly

This scientific paper is a single-blind, randomized controlled trial that investigates the effects of an 8-week moderate Intermittent Hypoxic Conditioning (IHC) program on cerebrovascular reactivity to carbon dioxide (CVRCO2) in healthy elderly individuals aged between 60 and 80 years. The study aimed to determine if IHC could enhance cerebrovascular function, but contrary to initial expectations, the data showed no significant change in CVRCO2. Despite some inconsistencies with previous findings regarding improved peripheral vascular function in the same population, potential limitations in sensitivity with traditional CVRCO2 measurements may explain the divergent results. The authors suggest future studies to incorporate alternative methodologies and investigate the combined effects of exercise and IHC for improving CVRCO2 responsiveness. The research was supported by the French National Research Agency and no conflicts of interest were declared.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

Title: Moderate Intermittent Hypoxic Conditioning to Enhance Cerebrovascular Function in the Elderly

This scientific paper is a randomized controlled trial investigating the impact of moderate intermittent hypoxic conditioning on cerebrovascular function in elderly individuals. The study was conducted by researchers from Univ. Grenoble Alpes, France, and involved participants aged 65 and older. The key findings, methods, and data are as follows:

  • The study aimed to determine if moderate intermittent hypoxic conditioning could improve cerebrovascular function in the elderly population.
  • A randomized controlled trial was conducted with a group of elderly individuals, with half receiving intermittent hypoxic conditioning and the other serving as the control group.
  • The intermittent hypoxic conditioning involved exposing participants to brief periods of reduced oxygen levels followed by normal oxygen levels for recovery.
  • Cerebrovascular function was assessed using various measures, such as cerebral blood flow, cerebral perfusion, and neuropsychological tests.
  • The data collected from the study will be analyzed to determine if intermittent hypoxic conditioning has a significant impact on cerebrovascular function in elderly individuals.

ABSTRACT

This study investigated the effects of Intermittent Hypoxic Conditioning (IHC) on cerebrovascular reactivity to carbon dioxide (CVRCO2) in elderly individuals over short- and mid-term periods. The participants were assigned to either an IHC or control group, undergoing 8 weeks of moderate exposure to either intermittent hypoxia or sham hypoxia.

Contrary to the initial hypothesis, 8 weeks of IHC did not significantly improve any CVRCO2 parameters compared to the control group at any time point (Pre, Post 1, and Post 2). The study suggests that IHC may not provide potential benefits on cerebrovascular function in older adults. Future research is required for further confirmation.

Keywords: cerebrovascular function, cerebrovascular reactivity to carbon dioxide, intermittent hypoxia conditioning, vascular aging.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the role of endothelial cells in maintaining vascular health and their significance in preventing cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, particularly in the elderly. Age-related endothelial dysfunction can disrupt cerebrovascular homeostasis, contributing to neurovascular and neurodegenerative diseases. Intermittent hypoxic conditioning (IHC) has shown benefits for the cardiovascular system, metabolism, and cognitive function across various populations. IHC is believed to enhance endothelial function by stimulating nitric oxide synthase (NOS) isoforms synthesis, leading to improved peripheral vascular function. In the cerebrovascular domain, hypoxic exposure can induce vasodilation and increase blood flow, potentially triggering vascular adaptations. While a single exposure may not induce lasting functional improvements, repeated exposures might lead to significant increases in cerebral blood flow and shear rate, improving cerebrovascular endothelial function. However, previous studies focused on young adults, and the effects of IHC on the elderly, who are more vulnerable to cerebrovascular dysfunction, have not been fully explored. This study aims to determine whether IHC can enhance brain health in elderly individuals and if its effects would be sustained over time. The hypothesis is that IHC will enhance cerebrovascular reactivity within a few days post-intervention (short-term), with effects persisting for at least two months post-intervention (medium-term).

METHODS

This study is a single-blind, parallel group, randomized controlled trial with 18 healthy Caucasian elderly individuals aged between 60 and 80 years, who were recruited from October 2021 to May 2024. Participants had no chronic cardiovascular, metabolic, or respiratory diseases (except hypertension and treated obstructive sleep apnea) and were not engaged in structured exercise training. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by a national ethics committee.

Participants underwent clinical examinations before being randomly assigned to either an Intermittent Hypoxic Conditioning (IHC) group or a sham normoxic control group (CTL). Each participant completed 3 sessions per week over an 8-week period, with each session lasting 63 minutes including 35 minutes of hypoxic breathing.

Cerebrovascular reactivity to carbon dioxide was evaluated during each experimental visit by measuring changes in Middle Cerebral Artery velocity (MCAv) in response to fluctuations in end-tidal pressure in carbon dioxide (PetCO2). Participants breathed through a mouthpiece connected to a respiratory flow head and gas analyzer, enabling continuous monitoring of PetCO2. The assessment began with a 20-minute period of supine rest and was followed by a 3-minute hyperventilation task, a 10-minute wash-out period, and a carbon dioxide enriched gas mixture delivered for a 10-minute ramp-like protocol.

Data were processed using digital converters and recorded for offline analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using Jamovi, and all tests were two-sided with statistical significance set at p < 0.05. The study aimed to examine the effect of IHC on cerebrovascular reactivity in elderly individuals, with a sample size calculated based on previous results showing a ~25% improvement in CVRCO2 following exercise training and reporting some variability in CVRCO2 assessment.

RESULTS

The study, initially involving 31 participants, ended up analyzing data from only 18 due to difficulties in MCA insonation and incomplete adherence to the conditioning protocol. No adverse events were reported during the study period. Both groups showed no significant differences in anthropometric, cardiovascular, or pulmonary function parameters. One participant in the Interhemispheric Inhibition (IHC) group had obstructive sleep apnea and two had treated glaucoma.

In response to hypercapnia and hypocapnia, both MCAv (Middle Cerebral Artery velocity) and PetCO2 (End-tidal carbon dioxide pressure) were consistent between groups across all time points, with no significant group or time effects observed. As a result, Cerebrovascular Reactivity to Carbon Dioxide (CVRCO2) remained unchanged in both groups throughout the study, for both absolute and relative changes. During hypocapnia, the calibrated hyperventilation task also produced similar physiological responses in both groups, with no differences over time points. Overall, the IHC protocol was well-tolerated by the participants.

DISCUSSION

This single-blind, randomized controlled study investigated the effect of an 8-week moderate Intermittent Hypoxia-Hyperoxia Conditioning (IHC) program on cerebrovascular function in healthy elderly individuals. Contrary to initial hypothesis, no significant change in cerebrovascular function was observed using Carbon Dioxide Reactivity (CVRCO2) as a measure. Despite limited support for IHC improving CVRCO2, the mechanism suggests hypoxia can enhance NO bioavailability and trigger cerebral vasodilation. However, the results are inconsistent with previous findings showing improved peripheral vascular function in the same population. Differences in methodology, such as the technique used to assess cerebrovascular reactivity, may explain the divergent findings. Limitations include technical issues in central hemodynamic monitoring and difficulty in identifying vessels due to thickening of skull bones in some participants. The small sample size is a result of stringent inclusion criteria and demanding study design.

CONCLUSION

The study aimed to assess the impact of an 8-week IHC program on cerebrovascular function in the elderly but did not find a positive influence of such intervention. Due to potential limitations in sensitivity with traditional CVRCO2 measurements, the authors suggest future studies to incorporate alternative methodologies. They also propose investigating the combined effects of exercise and IHC for improving CVRCO2 responsiveness. The data will be available upon request. The research was supported by the French National Research Agency and no conflicts of interest were declared. The authors thanked the participants and paramedical staff for their contributions.
