Low-­Dose CO2 Inhalation to Improve Oxygenation and Exercise Capacity at High Altitude: A Double-­Blind Pilot Study

Liang, Catcheside, Wellman, Wu, Wang, Steier, He, Schwartz, Eckert, Luo | 2026

Keywords: CO2 inhalation, exercise, high altitude, novel device

Final Summary

This original research paper, published in Respirology in 2026, explores the benefits of low-dose CO2 (2.5%) inhalation on oxygenation, exercise capacity, and Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) symptoms at high altitudes. A double-blind pilot study involving 28 healthy adults was conducted using a novel device for controlled CO2 delivery. Participants performed an exercise shuttle walk test (ESWT) at sea level and high altitude while inhaling either ambient air or 2.5% CO2.

Key findings demonstrate that participants who inhaled 2.5% CO2 covered greater distances during ESWT at high altitude, indicating improved exercise performance. Inhalation of 2.5% CO2 also led to increased PaCO2 and pH levels, potentially contributing to the enhanced exercise performance at high altitude. Some participants who developed moderate to severe AMS symptoms experienced relief when offered 2.5% CO2 via the device as emergency aid.

The research was conducted by a multidisciplinary team from institutions such as the University of Sydney, University of Technology Sydney, and Beijing Tiantan Hospital in China. Funding came from various sources like the National Health and Medical Research Council of Australia and the National Natural Science Foundation of China. Primary author Peter Catcheside led the study, which was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University and follows the Declaration of Helsinki.

It's worth noting that one author, Yuanming Luo (an inventor of the device), has potential conflicts of interest. Data from this study can be found in the article and its Supporting Information, and it is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05798416). Participants provided written informed consent for their involvement.

ABSTRACT

Title: Inhalation of Carbon Dioxide Enhances Oxygenation, Improves Exercise Performance, and Alleviates Acute Mountain Sickness Symptoms at High Altitude

This research study, published in Respirology in 2026, investigates the effects of inhaling a low dose of carbon dioxide (2.5%) on oxygenation, exercise performance, and symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) in healthy adults after rapid ascent to high altitude.

The study involved 28 participants who performed an exercise shuttle walk test (ESWT) both at sea level and at high altitude, while inhaling either ambient air or a continuous flow of 2.5% CO2 through a specialized mask.

Key findings include: 1. Participants who inhaled 2.5% CO2 covered greater distances during ESWT at high altitude compared to breathing ambient air, indicating improved exercise performance. 2. Inhalation of 2.5% CO2 led to an increase in PaCO2 and pH, which could contribute to the improved exercise performance at high altitude. 3. Some participants who developed moderate to severe symptoms of AMS experienced relief when offered 2.5% CO2 via the device as emergency aid.

The research was conducted by a team of authors from various institutions, including the University of Sydney, University of Technology Sydney, and Beijing Tiantan Hospital in China. Funding was provided by several sources, such as the National Health and Medical Research Council of Australia and the National Natural Science Foundation of China. The study's primary author is Peter Catcheside.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical University and adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki. It was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05798416) and participants provided written informed consent.

Conflicts of interest have been disclosed, with Yuanming Luo, an inventor of the device, having potential conflicts. The other authors declare no conflicts of interest. The study's data is available in the article and its Supporting Information.
