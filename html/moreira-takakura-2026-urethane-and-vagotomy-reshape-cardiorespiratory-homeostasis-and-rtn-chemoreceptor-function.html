Neural Circuits: Urethane and vagotomy reshape cardiorespiratory homeostasis and RTN chemoreceptor function

Moreira, Takakura

Keywords: arterial pH, brainstem, central chemoreflex, hypercapnia, vagus nerve

Final Summary

The research question addressed in this study is to investigate the effects of urethane anesthesia and vagotomy on cardiorespiratory homeostasis, blood gas composition, and the function of the rostral ventrolateral medulla (RTN) chemoreceptor neurons in rats.

The methods used include whole-body plethysmography to assess cardiorespiratory parameters and arterial blood gas analysis. The study also performed histology on brain tissue using Phox2b antibody to identify chemoreceptor neurons in the RTN. In vivo recordings of identified RTN neurons were conducted under different experimental conditions, including conscious rats, urethane-anesthetized rats with intact and cut vagus nerves, and artificially ventilated rats.

Key findings reveal that urethane anesthesia and vagotomy resulted in changes in cardiorespiratory parameters, blood gases, and RTN neuron activity patterns. Urethane anesthesia led to increased heart rate and respiratory frequency, decreased mean arterial pressure, tidal volume, and ventilation, with an increase in PaCO2 (carbon dioxide) and a decrease in pH and PaO2 (oxygen). Vagotomy exacerbated these effects, increasing mean arterial pressure, heart rate, and tidal volume while reducing respiratory frequency and ventilation.

Artificial ventilation corrected the blood gases but not the cardiovascular parameters. The study found that most RTN cells exhibited early-inspiratory inhibition/post-inspiratory inhibition patterns (pattern 1: early-I/ post-I), with some conversion to a different pattern following vagotomy, resulting in peak activity during inspiration.

RTN cells were sensitive to end-expiratory CO2 levels averaging 8.9 ± 0.9%. The findings suggest that RTN neurons play a significant role in respiratory regulation and are influenced by both vagal innervation and lung receptor inputs. Vagal afferents act as a physiological brake on chemoreceptor activation, ensuring stability of breathing under fluctuating CO2 conditions.

In conclusion, this study provides new insights into the role of neural circuits in maintaining cardiorespiratory homeostasis and sheds light on the critical role of vagal feedback in central respiratory control. The balance between excitatory carotid body drive and inhibitory vagal feedback may represent a fundamental mechanism of respiratory stability, with significant relevance for both basic neuroscience and clinical translation.

ABSTRACT

Urethane anesthesia led to increased heart rate and respiratory frequency, decreased mean arterial pressure, tidal volume, and ventilation, with an increase in PaCO2 (carbon dioxide) and a decrease in pH and PaO2 (oxygen). The study also investigated the activity of retrotrapezoid nucleus (RTN) chemoreceptor neurons, which are involved in cardiorespiratory modulation. RTN neurons were activated by hypercapnia and inhibited by lung inflation. Vagotomy reduced the CO2 threshold, abolished phasic inhibition during inspiration and post-inspiration, and shifted peak activity to inspiration. These findings suggest that vagal feedback plays a critical role in central respiratory control, and these manipulations could significantly alter baseline cardiorespiratory parameters and dynamically modulate RTN chemoreceptor activity.

METHODS

This text appears to be a scientific research article describing an experimental study on the effects of urethane anesthesia and vagotomy on cardiorespiratory parameters, blood gases, and histology in rats.

The study involves two main components: recording cardiorespiratory parameters and measuring blood gases. For cardiorespiratory parameters, they used whole-body plethysmography to assess respiratory frequency (fR), tidal volume (VT), minute ventilation (V_E), and other parameters under different experimental conditions, including conscious rats, urethane anesthesia with intact vagus nerve, urethane anesthesia with cut vagus nerve, and artificially ventilated rats.

For blood gas analysis, they measured arterial pH (pHa), arterial partial pressure of CO2 (PaCO2), arterial partial pressure of O2 (PaO2), and plasma bicarbonate in conscious rats, urethane-anesthetized rats with intact and cut vagus nerves, and artificially ventilated rats.

They also performed histology on brain tissue using paired-like homeobox 2b (Phox2b) antibody to identify chemoreceptor neurons in the rostral ventrolateral medulla (RVLM).

The study found that urethane anesthesia and vagotomy resulted in changes in cardiorespiratory parameters, blood gases, and Phox2b-positive cells. The results are presented in box and whisker plots and statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism.

RESULTS

In the Journal of Neurophysiology, researchers examined chemoreceptor neurons in the rat's rostral ventrolateral medulla (RTN) and vagus nerve. The study involved 400 RTN cells from both vagotomized and intact rats.

The primary findings were that most RTN cells (16 out of 20) exhibited an early-inspiratory inhibition/post-inspiratory inhibition pattern (pattern 1: early-I/ post-I), while four cells showed a late inspiratory inhibition/post-inspiratory inhibition pattern (pattern 2: late-I/ post-I). These patterns were observed in both vagotomized and intact rats, with little difference between the groups.

However, six cases of conversion of pattern 1 to a different pattern were observed following vagotomy. This change resulted in a peak of activity during inspiration, likely due to the loss of inhibitory inputs from lung receptors.

The study also found that RTN cells are sensitive to end-expiratory CO2 levels averaging 8.9 ± 0.9%. The results suggest that RTN cells play a significant role in respiratory regulation and are influenced by both vagal innervation and lung receptor inputs. DISCUSSION

This text is a scientific article discussing the role of the vagus nerve in regulating respiratory activity, specifically focusing on its impact on chemoreceptor neurons in the rostral ventrolateral medulla (RTN). The study was conducted on rats under urethane anesthesia.

The researchers found that lung inflation suppressed RTN firing and phrenic nerve activity, indicating inhibitory vagal afferents influencing brainstem respiratory neurons, including RTN chemosensitive populations. Vagotomy (removal of the vagus nerve) abolished this inhibitory modulation, lowered the carbon dioxide threshold for RTN neurons and phrenic nerve activity, effectively shifting the apneic threshold.

These findings suggest that vagal afferents act as a physiological brake on chemoreceptor activation, ensuring stability of breathing under fluctuating CO2 conditions. The study also highlighted that anesthesia can alter neuronal excitability and feedback loops, emphasizing the importance of considering anesthetic state when interpreting experimental data on central respiratory control.

The researchers concluded that the balance between excitatory carotid body drive and inhibitory vagal feedback may represent a fundamental mechanism of respiratory stability, with significant relevance for both basic neuroscience and clinical translation.
