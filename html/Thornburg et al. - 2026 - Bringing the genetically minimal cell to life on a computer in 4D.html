Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This scientific paper presents a comprehensive whole-cell spatial and kinetic model for the cell cycle of the genetically minimal bacterium JCVI-syn3A, simulating it in 4D (space and time). The simulation includes genetic information processes, metabolism, growth, and cell division. The model integrates hybrid computational methods to account for morphological transformations during growth, which is driven by lipid and membrane protein insertion, while being constrained by fluorescence imaging data.

The model predicts various cellular properties simultaneously, such as doubling time, mRNA half-lives, protein distributions, and ribosome counts, and recovers experimental measurements including the origin-to-terminus ratio from DNA sequencing. However, due to stochasticity, each replicate cell is unique, allowing predictions not only for average behavior but also the heterogeneity among them. The study involves collaboration among multiple institutions and researchers.

INTRODUCTION

The paper aims to understand the rules governing cellular life by predicting and analyzing the complete molecular context of cells over time and space (4D). This is achieved through computational modeling and machine learning, with a focus on JCVI-syn3A, a minimal bacterium with a synthetic genome.

The authors present a 4D whole-cell model (4DWCM) that simulates the entire cell cycle of Syn3A, including gene expression, metabolic network dynamics, ribosome biogenesis, chromosome dynamics, and morphological changes during growth and division. The model uses hybrid stochastic-deterministic kinetics to accurately represent various length scales, concentrations, and rates that define the cell state and cell cycle progression.

The 4DWCM incorporates spatial heterogeneity and its inherent stochasticity on reactions such as DNA replication initiation, transcription, translation, and mRNA degradation, allowing for a more accurate understanding of fundamental behaviors in cellular life. The model was tested on 50 unique replicate cells, marking a significant advancement in probing these behaviors.

RESULTS

In summary, two separate scientific papers were discussed.

  1. A computational model was developed to simulate a minimal bacterial cell (Syn3A), incorporating aspects like chromosome replication, division, and growth. The simulation results for doubling times of membrane and chromosome were in agreement with experimental observations but did not account for certain irregular morphologies.

  2. A model was created to simulate cell growth and protein production in Mycoplasma genitalium bacteria. This model could accurately double the counts of most proteins over a cell cycle, although some proteins were underproduced due to the absence of polysomes. Adjusting mRNA binding rates affected protein production significantly. The model also allowed for stochastic variations in replication initiation times and identified 81 non-transcribed genes across simulated cells.

In the second paper, a 4D model was used to simulate bacterial replication and metabolism. The model showed that successful replication initiation required adjusting DnaA domain III binding rates to single-stranded DNA. Additionally, it demonstrated the codependence of genetic information reactions and metabolism, predicting stochastic partitioning of macromolecules during division. Both models could contribute to understanding bacterial biology and designing targeted therapies.

The research article presents a four-dimensional whole-cell model (4DWCM) of JCVI-syn3A, a minimal cell synthesized by DNA transplantation into Mycoplasma mycoides.
