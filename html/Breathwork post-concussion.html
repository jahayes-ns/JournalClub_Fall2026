Final Summary

The study "Slow Breathing Interventions for Athletes with Sport-Related Concussion: A Randomized Controlled Trial" explores the impact of Resonance Breathing (RB) and Progressive Relaxation Breathing (PRB) on athletes recovering from concussions over a 7-week period. Using a randomized crossover design with 26 participants, the research assessed symptom severity and brain activity changes via Rivermead Post-Concussion Symptoms Questionnaire and electroencephalographic (EEG) signals.

Key findings indicate that both RB and PRB reduced symptoms and improved cognitive functioning by boosting prefrontal theta activity, enhancing attentional regulation. Regarding autonomic modulation, RB increased LF/HF ratio and total SKNA burst area, potentially improving coordination of autonomic signaling. PRB, on the other hand, increased SKNA burst threshold and burst frequency, suggesting sympathetic activity reorganization rather than generalized suppression.

Despite limitations such as a small sample size, gender bias, exploratory correlation analyses, and the lack of a sham control condition, the research demonstrates the potential benefits of slow breathing interventions for athletes recovering from concussions. These respiratory exercises could be a valuable addition to concussion rehabilitation approaches.

PREAMBLE

The study, titled "Slow Breathing Interventions for Athletes with Sport-Related Concussion: A Randomized Controlled Trial," investigates the effects of two breathing exercises - Resonance Breathing (RB) and Progressive Relaxation Breathing (PRB) - on athletes recovering from concussions. The study used a randomized crossover design with 26 participants over 7 weeks, assessing symptom severity using the Rivermead Post-Concussion Symptoms Questionnaire and electroencephalographic (EEG) signals to evaluate brain activity changes.

The findings suggest that both RB and PRB reduced symptoms and improved cognitive functioning by increasing prefrontal theta activity, enhancing attentional regulation. Regarding autonomic modulation, RB increased LF/HF ratio and total SKNA burst area, possibly improving coordination of autonomic signaling, while PRB increased SKNA burst threshold and burst frequency, suggesting sympathetic activity reorganization rather than generalized suppression.

The study demonstrates the potential benefits of slow breathing interventions for athletes recovering from concussions and provides insights into the differential effects of different types of slow breathing techniques on autonomic regulation and cognitive functioning. However, limitations include a small sample size, gender bias, exploratory correlation analyses, and the lack of a sham or non-intervention control condition. Despite these limitations, the findings suggest that respiratory exercises may be a potentially useful adjunctive approach for concussion rehabilitation.
