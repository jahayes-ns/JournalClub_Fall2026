Final Summary

Title: Intermittent Hypoxia Induces Reversible Epigenetic Age Acceleration in Old Mice

This study investigates the impact of intermittent hypoxia on epigenetic aging in old mice, potentially influencing the aging process. The research was conducted by a team from the National Institute on Aging (NIA), Altos Labs, and the Epigenetic Clock Development Foundation. The team exposed old mice to intermittent hypoxia for four weeks and measured epigenetic age using a DNA methylation-based aging clock. Results demonstrated that intermittent hypoxia induced reversible epigenetic age acceleration in old mice, particularly in the lungs, spleen, and heart. The findings were supported by human translational validation, demonstrating rapid, conserved epigenetic aging in young adults at high altitude. This research suggests oxygen availability as a primary modulator of epigenetic aging across tissues and species, with potential therapeutic implications for age-related diseases. Methods included whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) and an Epigenome-Wide Association Study (EWAS). The study was funded by the National Institute on Aging Intramural Research Program of the National Institutes of Health, with key contributors from various institutions and AI language models for manuscript improvement.

PREAMBLE

Title: Intermittent Hypoxia Induces Reversible Epigenetic Age Acceleration in Old Mice

In a pre-print study published in npj Aging, a team of researchers from the National Institute on Aging (NIA), Altos Labs, and the Epigenetic Clock Development Foundation investigated the effect of intermittent hypoxia on epigenetic age acceleration in old mice. The research was conducted by Stefano Donega, Ake T. Lu, Amin Haghani, Markus Horvath, Qi Yan, Mirko Baranzini, Maxine Thea Escote, Martina Rossi, Rolando J. Hernandez, Ross A. McDevitt, Kenneth W. Fishbein, Steve Horvath, and Luigi Ferrucci.

The study used old mice exposed to intermittent hypoxia for 4 weeks. Epigenetic age was measured using a DNA methylation-based aging clock. The results showed that intermittent hypoxia induced reversible epigenetic age acceleration in old mice, suggesting that intermittent hypoxia may influence the aging process.

The research provides insights into the relationship between intermittent hypoxia and aging, potentially leading to new strategies for delaying or treating age-related diseases. The study is significant as it demonstrates that the epigenetic clock can be modulated by environmental factors like intermittent hypoxia, offering a promising avenue for further research in gerontology and translational medicine.

ABSTRACT

The study investigates the role of oxygen availability in aging-associated DNA methylation, a key aspect of epigenetic regulation. The research was conducted on mice, comparing adult and old mice exposed to one month of intermittent hypoxia (IH) followed by normoxic recovery.

In old mice only, IH induced epigenetic aging in the lungs, spleen, and heart, which reversed upon return to normoxia. These shifts were enriched at bivalent domains and PRC2 targets, suggesting oxygen-sensitive chromatin remodeling.

The findings were supported by human translational validation in young adults at high altitude (5,260m), demonstrating rapid, conserved epigenetic aging. This research establishes oxygen availability as a primary, conserved modulator of epigenetic aging across tissues and species, suggesting that oxygen fluctuations could be a potent, reversible driver of epigenetic aging.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper outlines the role of DNA methylation in modulating gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence, serving as a critical interface between the genome and environment. Specific CpG sites exhibit predictable changes in methylation status with aging, forming the basis of an epigenetic clock that approximates an organism's chronological age. Age-related DNA methylation changes are conserved across multiple tissue types and immune cell lineages, with a discrepancy between epigenetic age and chronological age being a significant predictor of adverse health outcomes.

Previous research has identified enrichment of age-related CpGs near the binding motifs for transcription factors ARNT (HIF1B) and REST, central regulators of the cellular response to hypoxia. The study aims to investigate whether a hypoxia regimen accelerates epigenetic aging and assesses the potential reversibility of these effects using an in vivo strategy on adult and old female mice. Tissues analyzed include the lung, heart, and spleen, which represent three physiologically distinct interfaces with hypoxia. The researchers also analyze publicly available blood methylation data from young individuals exposed to low oxygen during high-altitude acclimation to determine whether their observations in mice apply to humans.

RESULTS

The scientific paper discusses the impact of intermittent hypoxia on DNA methylation patterns in mice organs (lung, spleen, and heart) using whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS). Significant differences in DNA methylation were observed between baseline, intermittent hypoxia, and recovery conditions. Key findings include:

  1. In the lung: Hypermethylated genes such as TGF-β3, Twist1, Nrep, Platr20, Shroom4, and Pla2g4a; Hypomethylated genes such as PRICKLE2 and PLAG2.
  2. In the spleen: Hypermethylated genes including Rbfox3, Prickle2, Ndn, Carns1, Kdm5d, and BC049762; Hypomethylated gene Smco4.
  3. In the heart: Hypermethylated genes such as Atg16l2, Eif5, and Ndn.

Additional hyper- and hypomethylated genes were also identified in adult mice for each organ, some linked to hypoxia-related conditions like pulmonary hypertension and breast cancer metastasis.

In the human study, rapid ascent to high altitude led to accelerated epigenetic aging, as measured by several DNA methylation clocks, including DNAmGrimAge2, Universal Clocks 2 and 3, PCHorvath1, DunedinPACE, and the Centenarian clock. The study identified 597 CpGs associated with duration at high altitude, suggesting potential epigenetic changes related to Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2).

DISCUSSION

This study reveals that intermittent hypoxia accelerates epigenetic aging in old mice, particularly in tissues already hypoxic or with impaired adaptation capacity. The findings suggest that oxygen fluctuations can induce unique biological responses affecting epigenetic clocks, partially tracking the failure of physiological homeostatic mechanisms. The study establishes a causal relationship between hypoxia and epigenetic aging but requires further experiments to confirm using other aging markers and methods.

The research suggests that manipulating oxygen levels could influence epigenetic changes crucial in aging processes, such as DNA methylation and histone modifications. However, both hypoxia and hyperoxia have potential negative effects on cellular health, implying careful management is necessary for therapeutic applications. The study emphasizes the need for further research to understand molecular mechanisms and develop targeted interventions for potentially reversing epigenetic aging.

METHODS

The study investigates the effects of intermittent hypoxia (IH) on aging in mice using an epigenetic approach. Three epigenetic clocks were used to predict age, and an Epigenome-Wide Association Study (EWAS) identified CpG sites with A-shaped or U-shaped methylation patterns in response to hypoxia. The study found significant changes in DNA methylation, particularly in genes related to oxidative phosphorylation, metabolism, and stress response, suggesting IH may accelerate aging.

The researchers used various databases for pathway and ontology analysis, including Mouse Phenotype, BioCyc Pathway, and Disease Ontology. Enriched biological pathways were identified using HOMER Analysis, Chromatin State Analysis with ChromHMM, and annotation of PRC2 binding sites.

Additionally, the study analyzed longitudinal DNA methylation data in humans acclimatizing to high altitude at Mt. Chacaltaya. Linear mixed-effects (LME) models were fitted for 35 biomarkers on five time points. These findings offer insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the effects of IH on aging, with potential implications for age-related diseases like sleep apnea and Alzheimer's disease.
