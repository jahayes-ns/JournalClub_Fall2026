Final Summary

The study investigates the test-retest reliability of neuroexcitability measures using threshold-tracking methods in both healthy adults and individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury (SCI). Two methods, T-T RMT and R-R RMT, were compared for corticomotor and nerve excitability.

Results showed excellent relative reliability for TMS measures in healthy adults and SCI groups, while NET using the TROND protocol exhibited high test-retest reliability across all measures and age groups. The T-T RMT test was found to be more efficient with a shorter duration compared to R-R RMT. No significant impact of temperature or age on test-retest reliability was found.

Most parameters had good to excellent reliability over time for both healthy controls and individuals with SCI, but hyperpolarization slope may have lower reliability. The study concludes that threshold-tracking-based techniques provide reliable outcomes, making them suitable for clinical research in populations with spasticity like those with stroke or SCI, while acknowledging limitations such as a small sample size and variations in methodology.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study investigates the test-retest reliability of neuroexcitability measures of the central and peripheral nervous systems in healthy adults and individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury (SCI). The research was conducted using threshold-tracking methods, which offer fast assessments, but whose reliability has not been established.

The study involved 27 healthy controls and ten individuals with chronic cervical SCI who underwent a test-retest assessment with approximately seven days in between tests. Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) of the primary motor cortex (M1) and nerve excitability tests (NET) of the median nerve were performed. Motor evoked potentials were measured at different muscles depending on the group.

The results showed that TMS measures exhibited excellent reliability for resting motor threshold and intermediate-to-good reliability for stimulus-response function. The majority of NET outcome variables showed at least moderate reliability estimates in both the healthy controls and SCI groups.

The study concludes that threshold-tracking-based measures of the central and peripheral nervous systems provide reliable measures of neuroexcitability, both in healthy adults and individuals with SCI. Establishing sufficient reliability is essential as these measures are increasingly used to estimate recovery-related neuroplasticity.

METHOD

The study compared two methods for measuring corticomotor and nerve excitability: T-T RMT (Threshold Tracking at 200 µV) and R-R RMT (5/10 activation criterion). Both were tested on healthy adults and individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI).

The TMS measures showed excellent relative reliability, with ICC values ranging from 0.81 for AUC to 0.97 for MEPmax in healthy adults and from 0.53 for S50 to 0.96 for T-T RMT in individuals with SCI. Nerve Excitability Testing (NET) using the TROND protocol showed high test-retest reliability across all measures and age groups.

The T-T RMT test was more efficient, with a shorter duration compared to the R-R RMT test. Post-hoc analyses found that neither temperature nor age significantly affected the test-retest reliability of NET variables.

The study concludes that threshold-tracking-based techniques provide reliable outcomes, making them suitable for clinical research in populations with spasticity like those with stroke or SCI.
