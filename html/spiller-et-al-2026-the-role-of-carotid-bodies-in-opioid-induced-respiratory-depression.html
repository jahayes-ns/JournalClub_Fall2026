MINI-REVIEW Opioids and Respiratory Depression: The role of carotid bodies in opioid-induced respiratory depression

Spiller, Pedro F., Amorim, Mateus R., Polotsky, Vsevolod Y. | 2023

Keywords: carotid body, opioid receptor, respiratory depression

Final Summary

The mini-review titled "Opioids and Respiratory Depression: The Role of Carotid Bodies" investigates the role of carotid bodies in preventing Opioid-Induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD), a life-threatening side effect associated with opioid use. The authors propose that opioids, primarily through µ receptors, suppress respiration by influencing central drive and peripheral chemosensitivity. Carotid bodies, as important oxygen and carbon dioxide sensors in the neck, play a protective role against OIRD due to their sensitivity to hypoxia, changes in CO2/pH levels, leptin, glucose, insulin, inflammatory cytokines, and blood flow. The review suggests that endogenous opioid peptides (enkephalins) present in carotid bodies may counteract the central respiratory depression effects of morphine and fentanyl. Activating κ opioid receptors in carotid bodies could potentially reduce OIRD without compromising analgesia, offering a novel therapeutic intervention. However, further research is necessary to fully understand the mechanisms involved and to test this approach's effectiveness in humans.

ABSTRACT

This review summarizes recent findings on the role of Carotid Bodies (CBs) in mitigating Opioid-Induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD). Opioids, while effective for pain management, are associated with respiratory depression and overdose deaths. OIRD is caused by activation of opioid receptors μ, d, or κ, predominantly µ receptors in brainstem cardiorespiratory control centers.

CBs, located at the carotid arteries bifurcations, are prototypical oxygen sensors and play a protective role against OIRD. They respond to hypoxia and other stimuli such as changes in CO2/pH, leptin, glucose, insulin, inflammatory cytokines, and blood flow. Enkephalins, endogenous opioid peptides, are present in the CBs of several mammals, and activation of κ opioid receptors in CBs may mitigate the central depressant effects of morphine and fentanyl on respiration. Activating glomus cells with a κ receptor agonist may offer a therapeutic intervention to reduce OIRD without compromising the analgesic properties of opioids. However, further research is needed to fully understand the mechanisms involved and to test the effectiveness of this approach in humans.
