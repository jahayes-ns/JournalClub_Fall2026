Final Summary

Title: Opioids and Respiratory Depression: A Focus on Carotid Bodies as Therapeutic Target

This mini-review explores the role of Carotid Bodies (CBs) in mitigating Opioid-Induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD), a significant public health concern causing over 130 deaths daily in the U.S. The review suggests that CBs, which respond to chemical stimuli including hypoxia and harbor opioid receptors (μ, d, or κ), may protect against OIRD by counteracting its depressant effects on respiration.

Evidence indicates that enkephalins and morphine inhibit CBs' sensory afferent activity through activation of d receptors, while fentanyl stimulates CBs via κ opioid receptors. Activation of these receptors may reduce OIRD without compromising analgesic properties. However, further research is required to confirm the presence of κ opioid receptors in human CBs and to evaluate the effectiveness of κ agonists in blocking OIRD with higher doses of fentanyl. The authors conclude that activating CBs may offer a therapeutic intervention for OIRD, minimizing risks associated with respiratory depression while preserving opioids' analgesic benefits.

PREAMBLE

Title: Opioids and Respiratory Depression: The Role of Carotid Bodies

Authors: Pedro F. Spiller, Mateus R. Amorim, Vsevolod Y. Polotsky

Affiliations: Department of Physiology, School of Medicine of Ribeirão Preto, University of São Paulo (Brazil) and Department of Anesthesiology and Critical Care Medicine, George Washington University (USA)

This mini-review focuses on the mechanism by which opioids induce respiratory depression, specifically highlighting the role of carotid bodies. Opioids are widely used for pain management but can lead to life-threatening respiratory depression due to their suppressive effects on the respiratory center in the brainstem. The carotid bodies, located at the bifurcation of common carotid arteries, play a crucial role in detecting changes in blood oxygen levels and stimulating respiration.

The review presents evidence that opioids act on carotid body glomus cells, leading to a reduction in chemosensory responses and ultimately contributing to respiratory depression. Furthermore, the authors discuss potential therapeutic strategies for minimizing opioid-induced respiratory depression by targeting the carotid bodies, such as α-adrenergic receptor antagonists or nitric oxide donors. The paper underscores the importance of understanding the complex interplay between opioids and the carotid body in managing pain while minimizing the risk of respiratory depression.

ABSTRACT

This review summarizes recent research on the role of Carotid Bodies (CBs) in mitigating Opioid-Induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD). OIRD is a significant public health concern, causing over 130 deaths per day in the US due to opioids' depressant effects on breathing.

CBs are polymodal sensors that respond to various chemical stimuli, including hypoxia, and have emerged as a potential therapeutic target for OIRD. In this review, the authors present evidence suggesting that CBs may play a protective role against OIRD by counteracting its central depressant effects on respiration.

Opioid receptors (μ, d, or κ) are abundant in brainstem cardiorespiratory centers and have been found in CBs of several mammalian species. Studies show that enkephalins and morphine inhibit the sensory afferent activity of CBs, possibly through activation of d receptors, while fentanyl stimulates CBs' chemosensory activity through κ opioid receptors. Activation of these receptors may reduce OIRD without compromising analgesic properties.

However, more research is needed to confirm the presence of κ opioid receptors in human CBs and to evaluate the effectiveness of κ agonists in blocking OIRD with higher doses of fentanyl. The authors conclude that activating CBs may offer a therapeutic intervention for OIRD without affecting the analgesic properties of opioids.
