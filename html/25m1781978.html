Dual Mechanisms for Heterogeneous Responses of Inspiratory Neurons to Noradrenergic Modulation

Venkatakrishnan, Tryba, Garcia III, Wang | 2026

Final Summary

This scientific paper presents an updated mathematical model of neurons in the preBotC (preoptic area of the brain involved in respiratory control), focusing on the effects of the neurotransmitter norepinephrine (NE). The model, which incorporates Hodgkin-Huxley style conductances and calcium dynamics, aims to reproduce and expand upon observed effects of NE on preBotC neurons.

The model reproduces synaptically isolated intrinsic bursting properties in two types of neurons: NaP-dependent bursters (N-bursters) and CAN-dependent bursters (C-bursters). Increasing the calcium conductance gCaN alone does not induce bursting, but increasing inositol-triphosphate (IP3) can do so in some tonic spiking neurons. However, the resulting bursting is not solely dependent on IP3, and eliminating either the sodium current INaP or the calcium current ICA abolishes the bursting dynamics.

The paper also discusses NE's effect on silent neurons, predicting that these neurons, particularly those with low gCa-values, remain quiescent even when both gCaN and [IP3] are elevated. The level of gCa is predicted to be a critical determinant of the response of silent neurons to NE.

Using geometric singular perturbation theory (GSPT), the paper gains deeper insight into the proposed dual-modulation mechanism of NE and its effects on distinct neuronal types. The results suggest that NE differentially modulates distinct neuronal subtypes, which could play a role in shaping network-level responses under both healthy and disease conditions. Overall, the study aims to address gaps in previous models by incorporating additional mechanisms through which NE modulates neuronal activity and sheds light on the role of NE in regulating various types of preBotC neurons.
