Xylazine exacerbates fentanyl-induced respiratory depression and bradycardia

Demery, Moore, Levitt, Anand, Traynor | 2025

Keywords: Opioid overdose, Plethysmography, Fentanyl, Xylazine, Respiratory depression

Final Summary

The study explores the effects of fentanyl and xylazine on respiratory parameters and blood oxygen saturation in mice. By administering varying ratios (1:1 to 10:1) of these drugs, researchers found that a combination of 3.2 mg/kg fentanyl with xylazine leads to a more pronounced decrease in breathing rate compared to fentanyl alone, with minimal impact on other parameters like heart rate and blood oxygen saturation. A positive correlation was established between the number of severe apneas and blood oxygen saturation levels at 10-15 minutes post-injection. The study suggests a synergistic effect of these drugs, potentially increasing the risk of overdose in humans due to enhanced respiratory depression and heart rate suppression by xylazine.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This scientific study investigates the impact of fentanyl and xylazine on respiratory parameters and blood oxygen saturation in mice. The findings show that fentanyl (3.2 mg/kg) combined with xylazine at varying ratios (1:1 to 10:1) leads to a more pronounced decrease in breathing rate compared to fentanyl alone. Both drugs individually have minimal effects on other parameters like inspiration time, expiration time, peak inspiratory flow, peak expiratory flow, blood oxygen saturation, and heart rate.

The study also discovered a positive correlation between the number of severe apneas and blood oxygen saturation levels at 10-15 minutes post-injection. The combination of fentanyl and xylazine seems to have a synergistic effect on respiratory depression in mice, potentially increasing the risk of overdose in humans due to the exacerbation of depression of respiration and heart rate by xylazine.
