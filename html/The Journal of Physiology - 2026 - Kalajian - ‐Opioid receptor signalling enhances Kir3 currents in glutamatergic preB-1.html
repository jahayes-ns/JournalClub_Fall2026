μ-Opioid receptor signalling enhances Kir3 currents in glutamatergic preBötzinger complex neurons in mice

Kalajian, Stettler, Conradi Smith, Del Negro | 2026

Keywords: μ-Opioid receptor, Kir3 currents, glutamatergic preBötzinger complex

Final Summary

This study published in The Journal of Physiology investigates the impact of mu-Opioid receptor (μOR) signaling on Kir3 currents in glutamatergic preBötzinger complex neurons in mice, which may contribute to opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD). The research led by Eva J. Kalajian revealed that 67%–77% of each subpopulation of preBötzinger complex neurons express Oprm1 (μOR gene), suggesting all neurons may respond to opioids.

The team found that the μOR agonist DAMGO increases passive potassium conductance in glutamatergic neurons, specifically via membrane-delimited modulation of Kir3 channels. Inhibitory neurons do not respond to DAMGO despite Oprm1 expression. The results suggest that opioid modulation of Kir3 reduces excitability of glutamatergic preBötzinger complex neurons, potentially contributing to OIRD.

The study proposes potential new interventions for OIRD that target signaling molecules downstream from the μ-opioid receptor and attenuate Gβγ –Kir3 interactions as an alternative to conventional receptor antagonists like naloxone. The research used electrophysiology and optogenetics techniques, including ChR2 activation for identifying target neurons and measuring their properties. Further studies are needed to confirm these findings and explore potential benefits and risks of the proposed intervention for treating OIRD.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction discusses the problem of opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD), caused by drugs like fentanyl and oxycodone, which outcompete the standard intervention naloxone due to stronger binding affinity and slower dissociation rates. This necessitates the development of new strategies to address OIRD. The study focuses on understanding the mechanisms underlying OIRD in the preBötzinger complex (preBötC), a key region for breathing movements, particularly its excitatory and inhibitory interneurons. Opioid signaling is found to enhance Kir3 channels in glutamatergic preBötC neurons, but the specific K+ channel remains debated. This study aims to clarify the ion channel targets of μOR signaling using whole-cell and single-channel recordings in identified excitatory and inhibitory preBötC interneurons. The findings will contribute to the development of novel treatment strategies for OIRD by identifying downstream druggable targets of the μOR signaling cascade in preBötC neurons.

METHODS

The research protocol outlined in this paper investigates the impact of mu-opioid signaling on glutamatergic preBotzinger complex (preBötC) neurons, particularly focusing on their transient potassium current (IA). The study utilizes both electrophysiology and optogenetics techniques, including ChR2 activation for identifying target neurons and measuring their properties.

The methods include: 1. Identifying glutamatergic preBötC neurons based on responses to ChR2 activation and whole-cell capacitance. 2. Measuring whole-cell capacitance, input resistance, IA, and other ion channel currents. 3. Assessing the effects of mu-opioid agonists (e.g., DAMGO) on I–V and V–I relationships in these neurons. 4. Investigating rhythmic inspiratory activity by recording XII nerve discharge in 500-µm-thick slices with an elevated K+ concentration to mimic in vivo conditions. 5. Performing single-channel recordings using cell-attached mode to investigate the effects of mu-opioid agonists on ion channel currents. 6. Statistical analysis to compare groups and assess significance.

The overall objective is to understand how mu-opioid signaling affects glutamatergic preBötC neurons, contributing to our understanding of respiratory control and potentially offering insights into new therapeutic strategies for respiratory disorders. The study interprets effect sizes with a significance threshold of α = 0.05, uses the Fisher Exact test to assess mutual exclusivity of IA and Ih expression, and employs whole-cell patch clamp, single-channel recordings, and linear regression for data analysis.

RESULTS

The research investigates the impact of μ-opioid signaling on Kir3 channels in glutamatergic neurons within the preBotzinger complex (preBötC), focusing on the preBotanical Cells (preBötC) region involved in respiratory regulation.

The study confirmed that glutamatergic preBötC neurons expressing IA exhibit delayed excitation and a sag potential, both of which are modulated by DAMGO. Specifically, DAMGO increased the conductance of a potassium channel, leading to hyperpolarization and an increase in resistance.

However, inhibitory preBötC neurons did not express IA and were insensitive to DAMGO, indicating that its effects are specific to glutamatergic neurons. The study also found that DAMGO does not affect the input resistance or zero-current potential in inhibitory preBötC neurons.

Furthermore, the research suggests that DAMGO enhances Kir3, a specific type of inwardly rectifying K+ channel, as Barium, a blocker of these channels, prevented the effects of DAMGO. The study revealed that DAMGO modulates Kir3 activity via a membrane-delimited mechanism, but bath-applied DAMGO does not enhance channel activity when it is absent from the pipette solution.

Overall, this research indicates that μOR activation and signaling enhance an inwardly rectifying K+ conductance such as Kir3 in glutamatergic preBötC neurons, which may have implications for respiratory regulation and opioid-induced respiratory depression.

DISCUSSION

The discussion section of the scientific paper focuses on Opioid-Induced Respiratory Depression (OIRD), a critical factor in opioid overdose deaths. It suggests that OIRD could be caused by enhanced Kir3 channels in glutamatergic preBötC neurons due to membrane-delimited signaling involving the Gβγ subunit.

Potential strategies to overcome OIRD include targeting the Gβγ subunit with drugs like gallein or nanobodies, and designing peptides that mimic Gβγ binding sequences. These approaches aim to reverse OIRD without causing withdrawal symptoms in chronic opioid users.

The proposed interventions could potentially serve as an acute treatment for OIRD following a reversal of an overdose with naloxone, but there is a risk of renarcotization due to the persistent presence of opioids in the body after naloxone administration.

The study used an animal model to simulate an opioid overdose and administered the proposed intervention, observing improvements in respiratory parameters following the intervention. Further research is needed to confirm these findings and explore potential benefits and risks of this intervention for treating OIRD.
