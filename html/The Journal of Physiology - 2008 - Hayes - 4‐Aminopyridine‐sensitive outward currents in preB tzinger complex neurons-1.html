4-Aminopyridine-sensitive outward currents in preBötzinger complex neurons influence respiratory rhythm generation in neonatal mice

Hayes, Mendenhall, Brush, Del Negro | 2008

Final Summary

The study by Hayes et al. (2008) investigates low-threshold, inactivating potassium currents (IA) in respiratory neurons of the preBötzinger complex (preBötC) in neonatal mice, which play a crucial role in respiratory rhythm generation. The research question focuses on understanding the role of IA in regulating inspiratory burst patterns and rhythmogenesis within the preBötC.

The study used whole-cell patch-clamp recordings to measure the voltage dependence, kinetics, and time course of IA in both inspiratory and expiratory neurons. The methods involved anesthetizing neonatal mice, rapidly decerebrating, sectioning slices containing the preBötC and hypoglossal (XII) nerves, and perfusing them with artificial cerebrospinal fluid (ACSF). Respiratory-related motor output was monitored from XII nerves while voltage- and current-clamp experiments were performed using various amplifiers and software.

Key findings include the presence of IA in 61.8% of inspiratory neurons, but not expiratory neurons. IA activated at -60 mV and fully inactivated below -40 mV. Blocking IA with 4-aminopyridine (4-AP) caused irregular preBötC rhythms with decreased inspiratory activity rise time, altered burst sizes, and increased timing variability, suggesting a unique role for IA in regulating respiratory rhythms.

In conclusion, this study provides evidence for a role of IA in shaping the discharge pattern of rhythmogenic neurons in the mammalian preBötC, contributing to respiratory rhythm generation. The findings suggest potential therapeutic applications for targeting this current in respiratory disorders.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Hayes et al. (2008) examines low-threshold, inactivating potassium currents (IA) in respiratory neurons of the preBötzinger complex (preBötC) in neonatal mice. A significant proportion of inspiratory neurons (61.8%) expressed IA, but not expiratory neurons. IA activated at -60 mV and fully inactivated below -40 mV, indicating its potential influence on membrane trajectory during respiratory rhythm generation. 4-Aminopyridine (4-AP) attenuated IA and disrupted rhythmogenesis, causing irregular motor output and abolishing current pulse-induced delayed excitation. The authors propose that IA opposes excitatory synaptic depolarizations and promotes orderly recruitment of rhythmogenic neurons during respiration. This study suggests a crucial role for IA in regulating the inspiratory burst pattern within the preBötC, contributing to respiratory rhythm generation.

METHODS

The study used neonatal mice to investigate transient K+ currents in preBötC neurons, focusing on the inspiratory (IA) current. Mice were anesthetized, rapidly decerebrated, and slices containing the preBötC and hypoglossal (XII) nerves were sectioned. The slices were perfused with artificial cerebrospinal fluid (ACSF) and rhythmogenic neurons were identified using 9mm K+ in the ACSF to maintain rhythmic network function, then switched to 3mm K+ for isolating IA properties. Data was acquired from a total of 65 inspiratory neurons, 8 expiratory neurons, and 4 field-recordings in a total of 77 slices and 77 animals.

Voltage- and current-clamp experiments were performed using various amplifiers and software. Respiratory-related motor output was monitored from XII nerves with extracellular suction electrodes, and data was analyzed using Chart 5, Igor Pro 5, Excel, and custom software.

The voltage dependence and kinetics of IA were measured using the form: x∞(V) = 1/(1 + e^(-(V −θx )/σx ) ), where x∞ reflects voltage-dependent steady-state activation or inactivation, θx is the membrane potential of half-activation or half-inactivation, and σx is the slope factor. The time course of IA was computed using recorded voltage trajectories from current clamp and the chord conductance equation. Integration step size was 0.25 ms to match the 4 kHz experimental sampling rate.

Inspiratory drive potentials were analyzed using Chart software, with leading and trailing slopes calculated from digitally smoothed traces that minimize spikes but preserve the underlying drive potential characteristics. Sample means were compared using t test or FisherExact test where indicated, with significance set at a P value of 0.05.

RESULTS

The study investigates the transient potassium current IA in the pre-Bötzinger complex (preBötC), a brainstem region crucial for respiratory rhythm generation. Using whole-cell patch-clamp recordings, the researchers found that IA is active during inspiratory bursts and distinct from KCNQ subfamily currents as it is not affected by 4-aminopyridine (4-AP).

During respiratory network activity, 4-AP caused irregular preBötC rhythms with decreased inspiratory activity rise time. The pattern of inspiratory bursts changed from incrementing in control to decrementing with 4-AP application, suggesting IA influences the onset of the inspiratory burst.

Furthermore, 4-AP increased variability in both burst sizes and timing of neuronal activity without affecting baseline membrane potential or drive potential amplitude/CV or respiratory cycle period/CV. The findings suggest a unique role for IA in regulating respiratory rhythms, with potential therapeutic applications for targeting this current in respiratory disorders.

DISCUSSION

The 2004 study by Al. focuses on the role of the A-current (IA), a transient outward potassium current, in regulating respiratory rhythm generation within the preBotzinger complex (preBötC). The authors found that blocking IA in mouse medulla oblongata slices altered the discharge pattern of rhythmogenic neurons, leading to increased variability in respiratory bursts. They propose that IA maintains the orderly recruitment of these neurons during the build-up to an inspiratory burst by delaying neuronal activation until sufficient excitatory input has occurred.

The study also suggests that IA is likely associated with Kv2.x and Kv3.x subtypes of voltage-gated potassium channels, but not non-inactivating K+ currents from the KCNQ subfamily. However, not all rhythmogenic preBotC neurons express somatic IA, indicating a possible unique functional marker for these neurons.

The authors suggest that the influence of IA on synchronizing respiratory rhythmic activity may be stronger in endogenous network activity in vivo due to a higher driving force for IA. They also note the importance of somatic voltage clamp quality in understanding the presence of unrecognized dendritically localized IA, indicating further investigation is needed.

In summary, this study provides evidence for a role of IA in shaping the discharge pattern of rhythmogenic neurons in the mammalian preBötC and suggests potential implications for respiratory rhythm generation.
