Final Summary

The study "4-Aminopyridine-sensitive outward currents in preBötzinger complex neurons influence respiratory rhythm generation in neonatal mice" by Hayes et al. (2008) investigates the role of a transient outward potassium current, IA, in shaping inspiratory bursts during respiratory activity within the preBötzinger complex (preBötC). The researchers used neonatal mice and slice preparations to identify IA as a component of delayed rectifier-like K+ channels such as Kv2.x and Kv3.x subtypes.

The study found that more than half of inspiratory preBc neurons express IA, which contributes to shaping inspiratory bursts. The maximum conductance of IA positively correlates with membrane capacitance and negatively with resistance, with a time constant of inactivation between 20-80ms. 4-aminopyridine (4-AP), an IA blocker, was used to study its role in preBc neurons. The use of 4-AP resulted in disorganized XII output and noisy preBötC rhythms that fluctuate in amplitude and period, suggesting that IA plays a crucial role in the regularity of the cycle period and drive potential magnitude in preBötC neurons.

The study proposes that the molecular identity of channels giving rise to IA could be a unique functional marker for putatively rhythmogenic neurons within the preBötC, and that further research is needed to clarify the specific contributions of different Kv subunits to preBötC IA and its functional significance in respiration. Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study "4-Aminopyridine-sensitive outward currents in preBötzinger complex neurons influence respiratory rhythm generation in neonatal mice" was conducted by Hayes et al. (2008). They investigated low-threshold, inactivating potassium current (IA) in inspiratory neurons of the preBötzinger complex (preBötC) in rhythmically active slice preparations from neonatal mice. Majority of inspiratory neurons expressed IA but not expiratory ones. In whole-cell and somatic outside-out patches, IA activated at -60 mV and was fully inactivated below -40 mV. 4-Aminopyridine (4-AP) attenuated IA and caused irregular respiratory-related motor output on XInerves, disrupted rhythmogenesis as detected with whole-cell and field recordings in the preBötC. The findings suggest that IA opposes excitatory synaptic depolarizations at baseline voltages of approximately -60 mV and influences the inspiratory burst pattern. The researchers propose that IA promotes orderly recruitment of constituent rhythmogenic neurons by minimizing their activity until they receive massive coincident synaptic input, which reduces periodic fluctuations of inspiratory activity. The study provides insight into the role of specific ion channels in rhythmogenesis within central pattern generator networks.

METHODS

The study used neonatal C57BL/6 mice, approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at The College of William and Mary. Mice were anesthetized and dissected to obtain transverse slices containing the preBötC and hypoglossal (XII) nerves from the medulla oblongata. Slices were perfused with artificial cerebrospinal fluid (ACSF) and maintained with 9mm K+ to preserve rhythmic network function, then switched to 3mm K+ for isolation of IA properties. Recordings were made using patch-clamp amplifiers, extracellular suction electrodes, and high-gain differential amplifiers, with data acquired and analyzed using various software. Whole-cell capacitance, series resistance, and input resistance were measured to ensure stable recordings. The voltage dependence and kinetics of IA were studied using Fitmaster software and Igor Pro, and integrated using the 4th order Runge–Kutta method. Inspiratory drive potentials were analyzed using Chart software, with significance set at a P value of 0.05. A total of 65 inspiratory neurons, 8 expiratory neurons, and 4 field-recordings were obtained from 77 slices and 77 animals.

RESULTS

The study investigates the role of a transient outward potassium current, IA, in rhythmogenesis within the pre-Botzinger complex (preBötC) during respiratory activity.

  1. IA is expressed in more than half of inspiratory preBc neurons and contributes to shaping inspiratory bursts.
  2. Maximum conductance of IA positively correlates with membrane capacitance and negatively with resistance, while having a time constant of inactivation between 20-80ms.
  3. 4-aminopyridine (4-AP), an IA blocker, has an IC50 of 2mm for the peak response and attenuates IA in both outside-out patches and whole-cell recordings, making it useful for studying IA's role in preBc neurons.
  4. 4-AP affects pulse-evoked spikes, increasing their width and area, potentially due to attenuation of IA but more likely due to effects on other Kv subfamily channels.
  5. In the context of respiratory network activity, 4-AP causes disorganized XII output, with noisy preBötC rhythms that fluctuate in amplitude and period.
  6. Whole-cell recordings suggest a rapidly activating potassium current may influence the dynamics of inspiratory burst patterns, with 4-AP changing the pattern from incrementing to decrementing, indicating IA's role in influencing the onset of inspiratory bursts.
  7. 4-AP also affects the regularity of both cycle period and drive potential magnitude in preBötC neurons by causing fluctuations in these parameters.

In summary, this study reveals that IA plays a crucial role in shaping inspiratory bursts during respiratory activity and contributes to the regularity of the cycle period and drive potential magnitude in preBötC neurons. The use of 4-AP as a blocker provides insights into IA's function and allows for further investigation of its role in rhythmogenesis within the preBötC.

DISCUSSION

The 2004 study focuses on the identification and role of an A-current (IA) in preBötC neurons, crucial for respiratory rhythmogenesis. IA is suggested to be a part of delayed rectifier-like K+ channels such as Kv2.x and Kv3.x subtypes, extending action potential duration when blocked by 4-AP. However, it's unlikely that IA includes non-inactivating K+ currents from the KCNQ subfamily.

The study finds that most, but not all, putatively rhythmogenic neurons express somatic IA, suggesting non-uniform expression contrary to previous assumptions. It's possible that many recorded neurons may have expressed unrecognized dendritically localized IA.

The researchers propose that the molecular identity of channels giving rise to IA could be a unique functional marker for putatively rhythmogenic neurons within the preBötC. They also hypothesize that IA plays a more substantial role in synchronizing respiratory rhythmic activity in vivo due to a higher driving force and larger hyperpolarizing influence on baseline membrane potential. Further research is needed to clarify the specific contributions of different Kv subunits to preBötC IA and its functional significance in respiration.
