Final Summary

Title: Convergence in Spinal Cord Injury Therapies: A Call for Integrated Approaches

In this editorial, James Guesta and Ona Blooma discuss the need for collaborative, integrated approaches to advance spinal cord injury (SCI) therapies. The authors emphasize the potential of combining neuromodulation, pharmacology, imaging, and rehabilitation strategies to achieve optimal outcomes in SCI treatment.

Key Findings: 1. Neuromodulation techniques, such as epidural stimulation and optogenetics, show promise in promoting neural plasticity and recovery after SCI. 2. Pharmacological interventions, including anti-inflammatory drugs and neurotrophic factors, may help reduce secondary damage and promote neuronal survival post-injury. 3. Advances in imaging technologies, like magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), enable better understanding of SCI pathophysiology and monitoring treatment progress. 4. Rehabilitation strategies should be personalized and combined with other therapies to maximize functional recovery and improve the quality of life for individuals with SCI.

The authors call for interdisciplinary collaborations, clinical trials, and policy efforts to support the development and implementation of integrated SCI treatment approaches. The paper highlights improvements in pharmacologic neuroprotection using riluzole, intraoperative imaging for real-time decision making during SCI surgery, and intensive long-term rehabilitation as promising areas for future research. However, the authors emphasize that these approaches need to be integrated from the acute injury stage through the chronic phase to address persistent gaps and further progress in SCI therapies.

Title: Convergence in Spinal Cord Injury Therapies: Neuromodulation, Pharmacology, Imaging, and Rehabilitation

INTRODUCTION

This introduction summarizes the current advancements in spinal cord injury (SCI) therapeutics, which are experiencing significant progress after decades of limited progress. The convergence of new knowledge and treatments is enhancing recovery of lost functions, including pharmacologic neuroprotection, intraoperative imaging for real-time decision making, intensive rehabilitation programs, and innovations in neuromodulation.

The article highlights interventions once considered experimental, such as pharmacologic neuroprotection using riluzole, ultrasound to measure spinal cord blood flow, intensive long-term rehabilitation, and spinal and deep brain stimulation, that are starting to show translational promise. The key theme is that improved recovery after SCI is rarely the result of a single modality but depends on combining technologies while being delivered within an intensive rehabilitation framework.

The paper discusses pharmacologic neuroprotection using riluzole, which has shown neuroprotective benefits in preclinical through Phase 3 studies but was stopped prematurely in the Riluzole in Spinal Cord Injury Study (RISCIS) trial. However, the data set is rich and informative, especially when combined with studies on biomarkers, drug levels, and distribution.

Intraoperative imaging using ultrasound for real-time decision making during SCI surgery is also highlighted as a promising area for future research. The integration of therapies, including neuromodulation, pharmacology, imaging biomarkers, and rehabilitation, is crucial for the next decade to design clinical pathways and trial platforms that coordinate these modalities rather than testing them in isolation.

CONCLUSION

The conclusion of this Current Opinion in Neurology paper highlights the advancements made in SCI therapies, particularly in neuromodulation, pharmacology, real-time imaging biomarkers, and rehabilitation. However, it also emphasizes that these approaches need to be integrated, starting from the acute injury stage and extending into the chronic phase, to address persistent gaps and further progress in SCI therapies.
