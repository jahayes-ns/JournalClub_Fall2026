GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray mediate fentanyl withdrawal and self-administration in mice

Chen1, Cheng2, Cao1, Xiao1, Chubykin2,3,4,5, Kimbrough1,4,5,6 | 2026

Final Summary

The research study explores the role of GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG) during fentanyl withdrawal and self-administration in mice. The research question focuses on understanding the impact of fentanyl on these neurons, their involvement in nociceptive hypersensitivity during withdrawal, and their influence on fentanyl self-administration.

Methods used include CRACM, whole-cell patch clamp recordings, and immunohistochemistry. Fentanyl was administered to mice through osmotic pumps, and its effects were observed by measuring pain sensitivity, c-Fos expression, and inhibitory synaptic transmission in the vlPAG. Chemogenetic manipulation was employed to inhibit these neurons and assess their impact on withdrawal-induced hyperalgesia and fentanyl self-administration.

Key findings reveal increased activity in GABAergic vlPAG neurons during fentanyl withdrawal, leading to enhanced inhibitory synaptic transmission. Inhibition of these neurons alleviated withdrawal-induced hyperalgesia and reduced escalation in fentanyl self-administration in male mice compared to controls. However, female mice did not show a comparable effect.

The conclusions suggest that GABAergic vlPAG neurons contribute to fentanyl withdrawal-associated nociceptive hypersensitivity and impact self-administration in a sex-dependent manner. These findings highlight the potential of targeting inhibitory signaling within the vlPAG region as a therapeutic strategy to alleviate withdrawal symptoms and limit opioid use, with data supporting these findings available upon request from the corresponding author.

RESULTS

The study investigated fentanyl withdrawal-induced mechanical hyperalgesia in mice. Mice were given varying doses of fentanyl through osmotic pumps for 7 days, followed by pump removal to induce withdrawal. Mechanical pain sensitivity was measured at different time points, revealing a dose- and time-dependent decrease in paw withdrawal thresholds, with the most significant effect observed at 0.4 mg/kg fentanyl and 48 hours of withdrawal.

Further analysis using whole-brain Fos network revealed the periaqueductal gray (PAG) as a hub in the fentanyl withdrawal network, with increased centrality compared to the saline network. Fentanyl withdrawal was found to increase Fos expression and GABAergic activity in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG), leading to enhanced inhibitory synaptic transmission in this region. Inhibition of GABAergic vlPAG neurons reversed fentanyl withdrawal-induced mechanical hyperalgesia and altered fentanyl self-administration in a sex-dependent manner. These findings suggest a critical role for the PAG and its GABAergic neurons in fentanyl withdrawal behavior and associated pain sensitivity.

DISCUSSION

This discussion section of a scientific paper explores the impact of fentanyl withdrawal on the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG), a region crucial for nociceptive processing. Key findings suggest that fentanyl withdrawal intensifies inhibitory synaptic transmission in the vlPAG, contributing to mechanical hyperalgesia. Chemogenetic manipulation revealed that inhibiting GABAergic vlPAG neurons reversed mechanical hyperalgesia during fentanyl withdrawal.

However, the study did not distinguish between local GABAergic vlPAG inputs and long-range afferents projecting into the vlPAG.
