Final Summary

The research question addressed in this study is the role of GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG) during fentanyl withdrawal and self-administration in mice, and how targeting these neurons could potentially alleviate withdrawal-associated hyperalgesia and limit fentanyl use.

The methods employed include a mouse model with osmotic minipumps delivering either saline or fentanyl, followed by spontaneous withdrawal after 7 days. Whole-brain analysis was conducted using the iDISCO+ protocol, light-sheet microscopy, ClearMap, and comparison to the Allen Mouse Brain Atlas to detect neuronal activity (Fos+ cells). Channelrhodopsin-assisted circuit mapping (CRACM) was utilized on brain slices from mice that underwent fentanyl dependence or saline controls. Behavioral experiments involving intravenous catheterization, self-administration of fentanyl, and an FR1 schedule were also conducted.

Key findings reveal an increased activity in the vlPAG during fentanyl withdrawal compared to saline controls, as well as enhanced inhibitory synaptic transmission within this region. During fentanyl withdrawal, there's an increase in c-Fos expression in GABAergic neurons of the vlPAG, indicating their activation. Chemogenetic inhibition of GABAergic signaling in the vlPAG reversed mechanical hyperalgesia observed during fentanyl withdrawal. Circuit-resolved activity mapping showed an overall enhancement and spatial reorganization of inhibitory input strength within the vlPAG during fentanyl withdrawal, suggesting functional enhancement of local inhibitory microcircuits. The study also highlights a bidirectional regulation of GABAergic neurons in normal and pathological pain conditions.

The conclusions suggest that targeting GABAergic vlPAG neurons may be a potential therapeutic strategy for managing opioid withdrawal symptoms, as inhibition of these neurons alleviated withdrawal-induced hyperalgesia in male mice. The study further reveals that modulating this population affects fentanyl self-administration in a sex-dependent manner. The data supporting the findings are available upon request from the corresponding author.

INTRODUCTION

This scientific study explores the impact of fentanyl on neuronal activity within the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray (vlPAG) region of the mouse brain. The research employs a mouse model with osmotic minipumps delivering either saline or fentanyl, followed by spontaneous withdrawal after 7 days.

Whole-brain analysis was conducted using the iDISCO+ protocol, light-sheet microscopy, ClearMap, and comparison to the Allen Mouse Brain Atlas to detect neuronal activity (Fos+ cells). Results indicated increased activity in the vlPAG during fentanyl withdrawal compared to saline controls.

To further investigate synaptic transmission in the vlPAG, channelrhodopsin-assisted circuit mapping (CRACM) was utilized on brain slices from mice that underwent fentanyl dependence or saline controls. The study also includes behavioral experiments involving intravenous catheterization, self-administration of fentanyl, and an FR1 schedule.

Findings suggest that fentanyl induces increased activity in the vlPAG and modulates GABAergic synaptic transmission in this region, potentially shedding light on the neural mechanisms underlying opioid addiction. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (version 10.5.0), and significant differences were set at p < 0.05.

RESULTS

The study investigates the effects of fentanyl withdrawal on mechanical pain sensitivity in mice. Four different doses of fentanyl were administered, and mechanical pain sensitivity was tested at various time points after pump removal. The results showed a dose-dependent decrease in paw withdrawal thresholds, with the 0.4 mg/kg dose showing the most significant effect.

The researchers also performed a whole-brain Fos network analysis to identify brain regions involved in fentanyl withdrawal. They found that the Periaqueductal Gray (PAG) played a central role as a hub in the fentanyl withdrawal network, suggesting its involvement in fentanyl withdrawal behavior and neural signaling.

Further investigation revealed increased Fos expression and GABAergic activity in the ventrolateral Periaqueductal Gray (vlPAG) during fentanyl withdrawal, indicating enhanced inhibitory tone. Inhibition of GABAergic vlPAG neurons reversed fentanyl withdrawal-induced mechanical hyperalgesia and altered fentanyl self-administration in a sex-dependent manner. These findings suggest that targeting GABAergic vlPAG neurons may be a potential therapeutic strategy for managing opioid withdrawal symptoms.
