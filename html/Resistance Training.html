Resistance training-induced adaptations in the neuromuscular system: Physiological mechanisms and implications for human performance

Lecce, Amoruso, Felici, Bazzucchi | 2026

Final Summary

In the Topical Review published in The Journal of Physiology (2026), Edoardo Lecce and colleagues from the University of Rome 'Foro Italico' explore the neuromuscular adaptations induced by resistance training (RT). Their research delves into changes at the motor cortex, spinal cord, and muscle level.

Key findings include increased intracortical excitability and reduced inhibition, leading to enhanced neural drive stability, improved motor unit recruitment thresholds, and peak firing rates in the brain. Spinal adaptations involve reductions in synaptic noise and alterations in motoneuron behavior, as well as heightened intrinsic excitability.

In skeletal muscle, RT triggers hypertrophy, increased myofibrillar protein synthesis, and improvements in endurance capacity via mitochondrial biogenesis and capillarization. The study calls for further investigations to understand distinct effects of subthreshold vs. suprathreshold PICs, the impact of monoaminergic drive, and the balance between changes in ion-channel expression and their neuromodulator-mediated activation in humans.

The study underscores the need for specific exercise programs tailored to desired outcomes like strength, power, endurance, or hypertrophy, with heavy loads optimizing strength, moderate loads promoting hypertrophy, and lighter loads improving endurance. The complex interplay between neural and muscular adaptations in response to RT is emphasized, as well as the importance of considering rest intervals between sets for optimal performance and recovery.

In summary, this study provides a comprehensive overview of neuromuscular adaptations in response to resistance training, with a focus on enhancing our understanding of RT physiology for improved clinical and athletic prescriptions throughout various life stages.

  4. Peripheral adaptations involve muscle fiber hypertrophy, increased myofibrillar protein synthesis, and improved force production capacity at the muscle-tendon junction.
