Final Summary

Title: Inverted U-shaped relationship between sleep duration, exercise, and phenotypic age in US adults

In this study, the authors investigate the relationship between sleep duration, exercise, and biological aging using data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) between 2005–2020. The research aims to examine trends in sleep duration, evaluate the association between sleep duration and phenotypic age, and explore how exercise influences this relationship.

The study finds a declining proportion of adults with insufficient or deficient sleep since 2015-2016. Both extreme short (<7 hours) and long (>9 hours) sleep durations are associated with increased phenotypic age, suggesting that adequate sleep (7-9 hours) is crucial for health and wellbeing. Regular exercise benefits those with extended sleep durations but may exacerbate aging effects for individuals with shorter sleep durations.

The authors use a cross-sectional design and self-reported sleep data, which limits causality establishment and potentially introduces recall bias. Future research should employ objective measures of sleep and explore the role of sleep quality in this relationship. The findings underscore the importance of promoting healthy sleep habits for healthy aging.

Methods include analyzing data from 48,762 participants selected through a probability sampling approach as part of NHANES, with 13,569 participants used for the analysis of sleep duration and phenotypic age. Sleep duration was self-reported, categorized based on National Sleep Foundation recommendations, and phenotypic age was calculated using 10 age-linked variables. Covariates considered included demographics, lifestyle factors, and health conditions. Statistical analyses were conducted using weighted linear regression models, and stratified analyses were performed to investigate the impact of lifestyle factors on the correlation between sleep duration and phenotypic age. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, with informed consent obtained from all participants. Data is openly available through the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's NHANES program website.

PREAMBLE

Title: Inverted U-shaped relationship between sleep duration and phenotypic age in US adults

In this study, the authors aim to investigate sleep trends, the relationship between sleep duration and phenotypic age, and the role of exercise in this relationship using data from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). They found a declining proportion of individuals with insufficient or deficient sleep time since 2015–2016. Both extreme short and long sleep durations were positively associated with phenotypic age in the fully adjusted model, suggesting an inverted U-shaped relationship. The study also highlights that long sleep duration can benefit from regular exercise activity, while short sleep duration with more exercise tended to have higher phenotypic age. The research sheds light on the complex relationship between sleep and healthy aging, providing practical recommendations for promoting healthy sleep habits.

METHODS

Summary of Methods Section:

The study analyzed data from participants selected through a probability sampling approach as part of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), a comprehensive survey conducted on a two-year cycle in the United States. For sleep trend analysis, data from eight continuous NHANES cycles (2005–2020) were analyzed, resulting in 48,762 participants. Considering that phenotypic age data was only available for NHANES 2001–2010, a total of 13,569 participants were used for the association between sleep duration and phenotypic age analysis. Sleep duration was self-reported by participants through standardized questionnaires, and categorized as long (≥8 hours), normal (≥7 to <8 hours), short (≥6 to <7 hours), or extreme short (<6 hours) based on National Sleep Foundation recommendations. Phenotypic age was calculated using 10 age-linked variables including chronological age, albumin, creatinine, glucose, C-reactive protein, lymphocyte percent, mean red cell volume, red blood cell distribution width, alkaline phosphatase, and white blood cell count. Covariates considered confounding factors were age groups, sex groups, race or ethnicity groups, marital status, poverty income ratio, education level, body mass index, smoke group, alcohol drink group, exercise group, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and cardiovascular diseases.

Statistical analyses were conducted using weighted linear regression models, with data combined according to the NHANES protocol and analyzed using survey-weighted analytic suggestions. The study examined the association between sleep duration and phenotypic age, accounting for several confounding variables across three distinct models (crude model, Model 1, and Model 2) and performing dose–response analysis using threshold effect analysis and restricted cubic spline regression. Stratified analyses were also performed to investigate the impact of lifestyle factors on the correlation between sleep duration and phenotypic age. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, with informed consent obtained from all participants. Data is openly available through the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's NHANES program website.
