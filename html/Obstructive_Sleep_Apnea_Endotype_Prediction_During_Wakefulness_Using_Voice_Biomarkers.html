Final Summary

Title: Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) Endotype Prediction During Wakefulness Using Voice Biomarkers

Research Question: The study investigates the potential of voice biomarkers for predicting different OSA endotypes during wakefulness, as a non-invasive alternative to overnight polysomnography (PSG).

Methods: A machine learning approach was employed using a dataset of 390 participants (308 with OSA and 62 healthy controls) who provided voice recordings and underwent PSG. Voice biomarkers such as fundamental frequency, jitter, shimmer, and noise-to-harmonics ratio were analyzed to identify distinctive patterns associated with various OSA endotypes.

Key Findings: Specific combinations of these voice biomarkers significantly differentiated between mild, moderate, and severe OSA endotypes during wakefulness. The study reported high correlation coefficients (r = 0.8 and r = 0.83) for airway collapsibility and muscle compensation endotypes, respectively.

Conclusions: This non-invasive approach could potentially simplify OSA diagnosis and monitoring by utilizing a machine learning framework that effectively compresses high-dimensional acoustic features while retaining physiologically relevant information. Despite the promising results, future validation in larger and more diverse cohorts is necessary to further validate this method's potential. The proposed method outperformed conventional machine learning baselines like Ridge Regression, Random Forest, and a single-layer MLP in predicting OSA endotypes.

PREAMBLE

Title: Obstructive Sleep Apnea Endotype Prediction During Wakefulness Using Voice Biomarkers

Authors: Shiva Akbari, Behrad Taghibeyglou, Atousa Assadi, Dominick Madulid, Devin Brown, Daniel Vena, Scott Sands, Azadeh Yadollahi

This scientific paper explores the potential of voice biomarkers for predicting Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) endotypes during wakefulness. The study employed a dataset consisting of 308 participants with OSA and 62 healthy controls, who provided voice recordings and underwent polysomnography (PSG).

The researchers used machine learning algorithms to analyze the voice recordings, focusing on various features such as fundamental frequency, jitter, shimmer, and noise-to-harmonics ratio. The goal was to identify distinctive patterns associated with different OSA endotypes.

Upon analysis, the study found that specific combinations of these voice biomarkers significantly differentiated between mild, moderate, and severe OSA endotypes during wakefulness. The researchers suggest that this non-invasive approach could potentially simplify OSA diagnosis and monitoring, reducing reliance on overnight PSGs.

ABSTRACT

The scientific paper introduces a non-invasive machine learning framework for predicting Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) physiological endotypes from speech during wakefulness. The framework utilizes a supervised autoencoder with mutual information-based feature selection, dropout regularization, and an enhanced multilayer perceptron architecture.

The model shows high correlation (r = 0.8 and r = 0.83) between predicted and actual values for airway collapsibility and muscle compensation endotypes, indicating potential as a cost-effective alternative to invasive diagnostics. The approach effectively compresses high-dimensional acoustic features while retaining physiologically relevant information, improving generalizability in small-sample conditions.

The study reports promising results with low Mean Absolute Error (MAE) values but acknowledges the need for future validation in larger and more diverse cohorts. The proposed method outperforms conventional machine learning baselines like Ridge Regression, Random Forest, and a single-layer MLP, achieving higher correlations and lower MAE across both endotypes.
