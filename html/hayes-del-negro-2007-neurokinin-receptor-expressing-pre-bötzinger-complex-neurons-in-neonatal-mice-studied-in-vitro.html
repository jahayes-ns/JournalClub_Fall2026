Final Summary

This scientific study investigates the role of Substance P (SP), a neurotransmitter, in exciting inspiratory neurons responsible for generating the rhythmic respiratory pattern in neonatal rats. The research employed current-clamp studies, two-photon confocal microscopy, and two-photon laser-scanning microscopy (TPLSM).

Through in vitro testing, it was found that SP depolarizes inspiratory neurons, although not all were labeled with TMR-SP. The study also hypothesized that gap junctions might mediate the effects of SP on NKR-cells, revealing that Substance P-induced current (ISP) was detected in 7 out of 14 inspiratory neurons, while 7 did not respond to SP when CTX, a gap junction blocker, was used.

Using TPLSM, the rhythmic Ca2+ activity of inspiratory neurons in the pre-BoC was measured, enabling observation and counting of a significant number of inspiratory neurons. The findings suggest that all TMR-SP labeled neurons have inspiratory discharge patterns, with TMR-SP respiratory neurons estimated to account for half of the early and late inspiratory population in the neonatal mouse preBo¨tC.

The paper references various articles and studies from journals such as Journal of Neurophysiology, Science, and others, discussing aspects related to the pre-Bötzinger complex, including electrical properties, synaptic transmission, roles of neurotransmitters, effects of drugs, identification, and characterization of specific types of neurons. In conclusion, this study provides evidence supporting SP's role in exciting inspiratory neurons and contributes to our understanding of the pre-Bötzinger complex in neonatal rats.
