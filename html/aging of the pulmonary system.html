Final Summary

This scientific paper, authored by members of the International Consortium to Classify Ageing-Related Pathologies (ICCARP) respiratory working group, investigates the impact of normative aging on respiratory pathogenesis, focusing on genetic, epigenetic, and inflammatory factors contributing to age-related changes in lung function.

The research question explores how biological aging manifests in the respiratory system, with a particular focus on understanding disease-driven hallmarks of accelerated aging conditions in chronic lung diseases like COPD, IPF, and asthma. The methods used include reviewing existing literature on physiological changes associated with aging, such as sarcopenia, senile emphysema, decreased compliance, and increased alveolar-arterial oxygen gradients, along with examining genetic and epigenetic factors influencing lung function impairment.

Key findings indicate that chronic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging) plays a significant role in age-related changes in the respiratory system due to an impaired immune system and tissue remodeling. Senescent cells, telomere shortening, and altered secretomes are also contributing factors. The paper discusses potential uses of senotherapies (drugs that target cellular senescence) for treating chronic lung diseases but acknowledges the need for further clinical studies to confirm their effectiveness.

The conclusions emphasize that age-related changes in the respiratory system lead to reduced function, increased disease states like sleep apnea and COPD, and increased respiratory infections. The paper proposes classifying pulmonary fibrosis driven by senescence as an ageing-related pathology and advocates for a multiprofessional approach for managing lung aging due to its complex nature involving both physiological and psychological aspects. However, the research relies heavily on inference from currently classified diseases and has a paucity of human data, highlighting the need for further study of human tissue samples across various ages to better understand lung ageing, particularly regarding the mucociliary system.

PREAMBLE

The 'preamble' section of this scientific paper, authored by members of the International Consortium to Classify Ageing-Related Pathologies (ICCARP) respiratory working group, discusses the impact of normative aging on respiratory pathogenesis. Key findings include the examination of physiological structural changes and their effects on lung function associated with aging, as well as an exploration of genetic and molecular factors contributing to the loss of respiratory function over time. The goal is to define how biological aging manifests in the respiratory system and understand disease-driven hallmarks of accelerated aging conditions, which will aid in developing interventions to improve health across different age groups, addressing population health challenges in the coming decades.

INTRODUCTION

The scientific paper discusses the factors contributing to changes in lung function as individuals age, focusing on genetics, epigenetics, and inflammation. Physiological changes include sarcopenia, senile emphysema, decreased compliance, and increased alveolar-arterial oxygen gradients. Genetic studies have implicated genes related to inflammation, epithelial homeostasis, lung remodeling, and cell-wall movement in determining lung function impairment. Epigenetic mechanisms may play a role in structural remodeling and declining lung function. Chronic low-grade inflammation (inflammaging) is thought to contribute to age-related changes in the respiratory system due to an impaired immune system and tissue remodeling.

The review also discusses the aging lung's altered secretome, increased production of reactive oxygen species, and reduced protective functionality of the respiratory microbiome. Inflammageing is reflected by increases in T2 and non-T2 inflammatory phenotypes in individuals with late-onset asthma. Aged fibroblasts can drive fibrogenesis and fibrosis, while senescent epithelial cells can reduce proliferation and induce SASP in fibroblasts. Telomere shortening is associated with increased fibroblast activation and collagen production, which can drive structural changes.

The text also discusses the potential use of senotherapies (drugs that target cellular senescence) to treat chronic lung diseases like COPD, IPF, and asthma, although clinical trials have yet to prove their effectiveness. The challenges in conducting clinical studies of geroprotective therapies for lung diseases are highlighted due to their slow progression and the need for identifying suitable individuals for clinical trials and monitoring anti-aging interventions. More clinical studies with anti-aging drugs in chronic lung diseases are warranted given the growing evidence for accelerated aging in these conditions.

DISCUSSION

The paper discusses the age-related changes in the respiratory system, leading to reduced function, increased disease states like sleep apnea and COPD, and increased respiratory infections. These changes are attributed to reductions in defense mechanisms such as mucociliary clearance, coughing, and macrophage function. The paper also highlights the need for further study of human tissue samples across various ages to understand lung ageing better, particularly in areas like mucociliary system research which is currently limited.

The paper suggests classifying pulmonary fibrosis driven by senescence as an ageing-related pathology and proposes a multiprofessional approach for managing lung ageing due to its complex nature involving both physiological and psychological aspects. The paper also acknowledges the challenges in translating our understanding of lung ageing into clinical practice, particularly due to the interconnectedness of ageing across various organ systems.

The research relies heavily on inference from currently classified diseases and has a paucity of human data. Much of the current understanding comes from genetic studies such as GWAS and whole-genome sequencing, but distinguishing direct effects of biological ageing on an organ system is difficult due to confounding factors. The paper suggests defining pulmonary fibrosis driven by senescence as an ageing-related pathology and emphasizes the need for further study of human tissue samples free from currently classified diseases and syndromes across a range of biological ages.
