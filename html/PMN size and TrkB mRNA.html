Final Summary

Title: Motor Neuron Size-Dependent Differences in Tropomyosin-Related Kinase Receptor B mRNA Expression in Adult Rats

Authors: R.C. Martinez, S. Rana, H.M. Gransee, G.C. Sieck, and C.B. Mantilla (Anesthesiology & Perioperative Medicine, Mayo Clinic, Rochester, Minnesota, USA; Physiology & Biomedical Engineering, Mayo Clinic, Rochester, Minnesota, USA)

Publication: Journal of Neurochemistry, January 2026. DOI: 10.1111/jnc.70381

This original study from the Journal of Neurochemistry investigates the size-dependent differences in Tropomyosin-Related Kinase Receptor B (TrkB) mRNA expression in adult rat motor neurons, focusing on phrenic motor neurons (PMNs), which control breathing. The research employed RNAscope in situ hybridization to reveal that larger PMNs express higher levels of TrkB mRNA transcripts, suggesting a positive correlation between PMN size and BDNF signaling, known for promoting neuroprotection and survival of these crucial neurons (Research Question).

The study also examined the difference in TrkB expression among motor neurons, focusing on those innervating the diaphragm. The findings indicate that larger motor neurons express more TrkB mRNA than smaller ones, suggesting a possible dependency on neurotrophic signaling for survival and function (Methods).

The key finding is the positive correlation between motor neuron size and TrkB mRNA expression, with larger motor neurons expressing higher levels of TrkB. This could have therapeutic implications for preserving neuromuscular function and delaying neurodegeneration by enhancing neurotrophic support (Key Findings). However, selective activation of full-length TrkB receptors is crucial to ensure efficacy. The study discusses the implications for pathological conditions like spinal cord injury and neurodegenerative diseases, emphasizing the importance of understanding the interplay between TrkB isoforms, motor neuron size, and muscle fiber type for enhancing motor neuron resilience and repair (Conclusions). Future research should further explore these areas.
