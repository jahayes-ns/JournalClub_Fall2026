Potentiation of active locomotor state by spinal-projecting serotonergic neurons

Fenstermacher, Vonasek, Cavanagh, Gattuso, Chaimowitz, Jessell, Dymecki, Dasen | 2025

Final Summary

The study by Fenstermacher et al. investigates the structure and activity of spinal-projecting serotonergic neurons during locomotion, revealing their crucial role in modulating motor circuits and behavior over extended time scales. Focusing on the caudal group of serotonergic neurons, particularly those in the ROb/Pa region, the research shows that these neurons receive input from locomotor control regions and exhibit increased activity during movement.

Activating ROb/Pa neurons through blue light stimulation led to longer but fewer locomotor bouts with increased average speed, demonstrating that serotonergic neurons can influence locomotion by increasing the duration and speed of runs. The study also found connections between the ROb/Pa and other brain areas involved in locomotion, such as the CnF (Cuneiform nucleus) and LPGI (Lateral Paragigantocellular Nucleus).

The findings suggest that serotonergic neurons act as a 'go-signal' for maintaining ongoing locomotion, potentially playing a role in regulating behavior over longer timescales. The descending serotonergic system forms a highly branched network that innervates multiple spinal segments, coordinating modulation of multiple circuits for behavioral control.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Fenstermacher et al. reveals the structure and activity of spinal-projecting serotonergic neurons, which are engaged during locomotion in a context-dependent manner. Activation of ventrally projecting 5-HT neurons enhances the speed and endurance of ongoing locomotion, demonstrating that serotonergic neurons can potentiate motor output over extended time scales. The research suggests that these neurons play a crucial role in modulating the temporal dynamics of motor circuits during locomotor behavior.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the scientific paper discusses the role of neuromodulators, particularly serotonin (5-HT), in controlling behavioral flexibility and rhythm-generating circuits in the motor system, including those responsible for walking, breathing, and swimming. Serotonin is shown to be a potent modulator of spinal motor circuits, affecting the excitability of spinal motor neurons and ventral interneurons, which are crucial for locomotion. Despite extensive research, the function of descending serotonergic pathways during movement and their role in locomotor behavior remains unclear.

The paper focuses on a specific group of serotonergic neurons located within the raphe nuclei, divided into rostral and caudal groups. The caudal group, including raphe obscurus (ROb) and raphe pallidus (RPa), project to ventral regions of the spinal cord, while raphe magnus (RMg) targets dorsal laminae. Recent studies have identified multiple anatomically and functionally distinct serotonergic neuron populations in the brainstem and spinal cord.

Physiological studies reveal that the activity of 5-HT neurons correlates with movement, increasing during treadmill-induced locomotion and being relatively silent during REM sleep. The paper aims to define the cellular and synaptic architecture of spinal cord-projecting serotonergic neurons and examine their function in motor control. The study finds that ROb/Pa provides direct input to spinal motor neurons and receives input from locomotor control regions, suggesting the descending serotonergic system is recruited in parallel with motor command systems. The activation of ROb/Pa neurons targeting ventral spinal circuits produces long-lasting increases in running duration and speed. The paper reveals that anatomically and functionally distinct serotonergic neuron populations targeting ventral spinal motor circuits act to regulate locomotor behavior over long timescales.

RESULTS

The combined results from two studies suggest that serotonin neurons in the ROb/Pa (Rostral Oval Peduncle/Parabrachial area) region influence locomotion in animals.

In one study, activation of these neurons through blue light stimulation led to fewer but longer locomotor bouts and an increase in average speed during periods of light stimulation, with effects accumulating over time. The strongest effects were observed during the final light activation period.

Another study using rabbits revealed that activation of ROb/Pa neurons increases the probability of maintaining the active locomotor state, suggesting a role for these neurons in regulating behavior over longer timescales. Connections between the ROb/Pa and other brain areas involved in locomotion, such as the CnF (Cuneiform nucleus) and LPGI (Lateral Paragigantocellular Nucleus), were also identified.

Previous research indicates that serotonin increases the excitability of spinal motor neurons and is involved in various brain functions like mood regulation and sleep-wake cycles. The findings suggest that activation of serotonergic neurons in the ROb/Pa region can influence locomotion by increasing speed and duration of runs, potentially playing a role in regulating behavior over longer timescales.

DISCUSSION

The study reveals that serotonergic neurons, which are known to modulate spinal circuit dynamics, are interconnected with motor circuits involved in locomotion. These neurons targeting rhythm-generating spinal circuits receive input from locomotor control areas, display increased activity during locomotion, and can produce long-lasting potentiation of locomotor behavior. The descending serotonergic system, particularly ventral spinal cord-projecting Pet1 neurons, forms a highly branched system that innervates multiple spinal segments, coordinating modulation of multiple circuits for behavioral control. These neurons receive input from locomotor control regions and exhibit increased activity during spontaneous bouts of locomotion. The study suggests that serotonergic neurons act as a 'go-signal' for maintaining ongoing locomotion, increasing the speed and length of running, and affecting sensory feedback gating.

The anatomical and molecular genetic neuronal tracing studies demonstrate distinct serotonergic populations targeting the dorsal and ventral spinal cord. The descending ROb/Pa Pet1 neurons receive input from locomotor control regions such as LPGi and CnF, with potential context-dependent inputs from other regions like the ventrolateral PAG. Spinal motor neurons (MNs) and interneurons (INs) receive synaptic input from 5-HT neurons originating from ROb, with distinct mechanisms for serotonergic modulation of spinal INs and MNs likely conferred by differences in 5-HT receptor expression among spinal neuronal classes.

Serotonin modulates the excitability of spinal MNs and INs, generating changes in the temporal dynamics of motor output and magnitude and timing of muscle activation. This could allow specific motor commands to yield larger or more sustained motor output, acting like turning up a gain 'knob'. Activation of ventral spinal cord-projecting Pet1 neurons increases the speed and length of running, possibly through enhanced release of 5-HT increasing the excitability of spinal MNs and INs.
