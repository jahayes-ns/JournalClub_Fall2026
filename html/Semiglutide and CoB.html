PREAMBLE

The paper published in Acta Physiologica (2026) discusses the expanding clinical impact of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists (GLP1RAs), previously used for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (TD2M) and obesity. Now, one in eight US adults use a GLP1RA, reflecting their efficacy and expanding indications. The focus of the paper is on the role of GLP1RAs in respiratory physiology and their potential for treating respiratory disorders like Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA).

The authors highlight that OSA is a prevalent respiratory condition associated with higher cardiovascular mortality, and several patients have obesity hypoventilation syndrome (OHS). Positive airway pressure (PAP) is the first-line treatment for OSA and OHS, but alternative therapies are needed due to low treatment adherence. Given the interest in GLP1RAs in controlling body weight and TD2M, their potential activation may be beneficial in the control of breathing and upper airway patency.

Several clinical trials have shown that Liraglutide and Tirzepatide, a dual receiver long-acting agonist of the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP1R, reduce AHI in patients with obesity and moderate/severe OSA. However, it is still unclear whether they have direct effects on respiratory neurons.

The potential of GLP1RAs to enhance the hypercapnic ventilatory response, increase breathing during sleep, and reduce AHI and oxyhemoglobin desaturations suggests a promising role in the treatment of OSA in obesity. Investigating the mechanisms of direct activation of GLP1R in respiratory neurons in the brainstem could open new avenues for treating not only OSA and OHS but also hypoventilation syndromes unrelated to obesity.

Tirzepatide was approved by the FDA for OSA in overweight individuals in conjunction with calorie restriction and increased physical activity, showing benefits in respiratory function. Triple-hormone receptor agonist (retatrutide), a new class of GLP1RA, acts as a GLP1, GIP, and glucagon receptor agonist and has a long half-life, is undergoing clinical trials for the treatment of obesity.

In conclusion, while GLP1RAs are primarily used to manage TD2M and weight loss, they offer several additional benefits, including improvements in respiratory diseases, which can positively impact conditions such as OSA and OHS independent of weight loss.
