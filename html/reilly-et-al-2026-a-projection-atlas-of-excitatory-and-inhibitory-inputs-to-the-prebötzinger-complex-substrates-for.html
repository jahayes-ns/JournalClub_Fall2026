Final Summary

Title: A Projection Atlas of Excitatory and Inhibitory Inputs to the preBötzinger Complex

The research question explored in this study is to create a comprehensive atlas detailing excitatory and inhibitory inputs targeting the preBötzinger complex (preBötC), a key region in the brain responsible for rhythmic breathing. The authors used viral tracers and imaging techniques to map out afferent projections from various brain regions in mice, including the motor cortex, hypothalamus, and brainstem areas. They also identified several types of inhibitory interneurons within the preBötC that may regulate rhythmic breathing patterns during different physiological states.

Key findings reveal multiple sources of excitatory inputs converging on the preBötC, with substantial input from caudal and rostral medullary regions, the rostral ventral respiratory group (rVRG), the lateral parafacial region/retrotrapezoid nucleus (PFl/RTN), and the spinal trigeminal nucleus caudalis (Sp5c). Inhibitory projections were predominantly ipsilateral to the injection site in the caudal medulla, and excitatory projections found in the contralateral cVRG.

The study concludes that the preBötC is not a self-contained oscillator but rather a hub whose function is modulated by diverse long-range excitatory and inhibitory inputs from various brain regions. This atlas provides a foundation for understanding how higher-order brain regions shape breathing across physiological, behavioral, and pathological contexts. The implications of this study could potentially lead to new treatments for respiratory disorders and sleep apnea.

ABSTRACT

The study systematically identifies sources of excitatory (glutamatergic) and inhibitory (GABAergic) inputs targeting the preBötzinger complex (preBötC), a critical brainstem area responsible for rhythm generation during breathing. Using a retrograde tracing approach, an atlas was compiled, highlighting various brain regions that project to the preBötC, underscoring it as a nexus for integrating multimodal signals regulating rhythm generation and patterning in breathing. The research indicates that the preBötC is not a self-contained oscillator but rather a hub whose function is modulated by diverse long-range excitatory and inhibitory inputs. This atlas provides a foundation for understanding how higher-order brain regions shape breathing across physiological, behavioral, and pathological contexts. The study maps the sources of inputs influencing the preBötC, offering a framework for understanding how sensory signals, behavior, and brain state impact breathing rhythms.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper highlights the complexity of respiratory control mechanisms despite the seemingly simple function of breathing. Breathing is an automatic process governed by continuous reflexive mechanisms that maintain homeostasis through precise regulation of blood gases and pH, but it also adapts to behaviors like speech, swallowing, and emotional states. The ventrolateral medulla's ventral respiratory column (VRC) is the primary location for rhythm and pattern generation of breathing motor commands, with the preB€otzinger complex (preB€otC) as the principal rhythmogenic site for inspiration. The preB€otC receives extensive direct projections from various brain regions, including homeostatic, emotional, behavioral, and volitional areas. Previous studies have identified inputs to excitatory SST and inhibitory GlyT2 neurons in the preB€otC using G-deleted rabies viral tracing, revealing that these distinct subpopulations receive largely parallel inputs from the same brain regions. However, limitations of rabies tracing leave questions unanswered, including the molecular identity and prevalence of the projecting populations. The paper aims to assemble a comprehensive survey of the brain-wide excitatory and inhibitory projections to the preB€otC using an efficient dual-recombinase-dependent tracing strategy. This atlas will provide an anatomical framework for understanding multimodal breathing regulation, linking homeostatic control with volitional, behavioral, and affective modulation.

METHODS

The study used transgenic mouse lines from The Jackson Laboratory, including Ai65D, Vglut2-ires-Cre, and Vgat-ires-Cre. At 6 weeks of age, heterozygous offspring received a unilateral stereotaxic injection of rgAAV-EF1a-FlpO mixed with AAV1-EF1a-eYFP into the preB€otC. Mice were anesthetized and secured in a stereotaxic frame for the injection. Approximately 3 weeks after surgery, mice were perfused with ice-cold PBS and 4% paraformaldehyde, and brains were sectioned coronally at 30 lm thickness. Image processing involved selecting well-targeted cases based on eYFP expression, adjusting contrast levels in QuPath to enhance neuron visibility, detecting DAPI-positive nuclei, and manually training a QuPath object classifier for each fluorescence channel. Labeled neurons were then considered within anatomical regions based on their position relative to atlas-aligned landmarks. Atlas overlays were generated by modifying atlas images to retain only contour lines, automatically detecting nuclei in each section, and spatially aligning the classified object images to their corresponding atlas sections using BigWarp in ImageJ. The Vglut2 and Vgat object images were resized to match atlas dimensions and merged to generate composite projection maps for figure assembly in Microsoft PowerPoint.

RESULTS

The study maps the excitatory and inhibitory projections to the preBötzinger complex (preBöC), a crucial region in the brainstem responsible for respiratory rhythm generation. The research used a mouse model and peri-injection site labeling to visualize neurons projecting to the preBöC.

Key findings include: 1. Substantial input from caudal and rostral medullary regions, with inhibitory projections predominantly ipsilateral to the injection site in the caudal medulla, and excitatory projections found in the contralateral cVRG. 2. The rostral ventral respiratory group (rVRG) sends excitatory projections to the preBöC. 3. The lateral parafacial region/retrotrapezoid nucleus (PFl/RTN) sends inhibitory projections to the preBöC, which modulates respiratory rhythm and plays a role in central chemoreception. 4. The spinal trigeminal nucleus caudalis (Sp5c), known for receiving input from various sources like mechanoreceptors and nociceptors, sends excitatory projections to the preBöC, suggesting that sensory information from the face and oral cavity may influence respiratory rhythm generation.

The study provides insights into the complex network of connections between various brainstem regions involved in respiratory control. Further research is needed to understand their functional significance in physiological and pathological conditions.

Additional findings suggest that excitatory neurons were found in various areas such as the periaqueductal gray (PAG), red nucleus (RN), lateral hypothalamic area (LH), parasubthalamic nucleus (PSTH), and dorsomedial hypothalamic nucleus (DM). Inhibitory neurons were found in regions like the zona incerta (ZI) and pontine reticular nucleus (PNc). Some brain areas, such as the midbrain reticular nucleus (mRt), superior colliculus (SC), and cerebral peduncle, have both excitatory and inhibitory neurons projecting to the preBöC.

The study suggests that cortical input neurons are represented broadly across the cortical mantle, and excitatory cortical inputs to the preBöC are widespread, bilateral, and functionally diverse. However, direct functional evidence for their specific respiratory roles remains limited.

DISCUSSION

The discussion in this scientific paper focuses on the various pathways controlling breathing and their relationship with emotions, threats, and behavior.

  1. Key emotional and threat-linked respiratory control pathways include:
  2. Extended amygdala (including Amygdala inputs to preBötzinger complex) for fear or anxiety-associated respiratory changes
  3. BNST projections for sustained threat or anxiety states

  4. Periaqueductal gray (PAG) coordinating respiratory adjustments during fear, stress, and defensive contexts

  5. Cortical inputs may link emotional appraisal and interoceptive awareness to breathing control via descending projections from insular and medial prefrontal regions.

  6. Volitional and behavioral integration is achieved through local brainstem sensorimotor pathways and long-range corticofugal projections that can directly access preBötC circuitry, including:

  7. Excitatory input from the interpolar division of the spinal trigeminal nucleus (Sp5i) for orofacial sensorimotor signals
  8. Rostral trigeminal-reticular inputs for behavioral coupling

The study proposes a distributed control architecture for breathing, involving brainstem reflex circuits, local ventral respiratory column networks, nociceptive and defensive pathways, pontine and midbrain hubs, and forebrain and limbic systems that provide direct access for volitional and affective modulation. The preBötzinger complex is central to this network, integrating convergent excitatory and inhibitory signals to support diverse respiratory modes.
