Final Summary

This scientific paper advocates for a personalized approach to treating Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), acknowledging its heterogeneous nature with distinct endophenotypes. The study explores potential pharmacological treatments for OSA, focusing on six strategies: enhancing upper airway dilator muscle activity, stabilizing breathing control, blocking cardiovascular consequences, addressing symptoms, promoting CPAP adherence, and facilitating weight loss.

Promising results have been shown for a drug combination of atomoxetine and oxybutynin in reducing OSA severity, while GLP-1 receptor agonists like tirzepatide have demonstrated improvements in both OSA and cardiovascular biomarkers. Future pharmacological strategies include orforglipron, an oral GLP1RA, and retatrutide, a triple G agent with promising weight loss results.

The paper suggests that a combination of therapies targeting mechanical airway obstruction, underlying obesity, and cardiovascular risk factors will optimize clinical outcomes for OSA patients. The authors recommend Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) as the standard of care for OSA evaluation and treatment by sleep medicine specialists.

PREAMBLE

The paper under review advocates for a personalized approach to treating Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), recognizing it as a heterogeneous disease with distinct endophenotypes. These include upper airway anatomy, ventilatory control instability, upper airway dilator muscle function, arousal threshold, and other factors. OSA phenotypes, characterized by varying clinical expressions of the disease, have been found to differ widely, with some patients being asymptomatic, having disrupted sleep, or severe excessive daytime sleepiness, particularly among those at high cardiovascular risk. This suggests that a one-size-fits-all strategy for treating OSA may not be effective. Instead, a personalized approach targeting underlying mechanisms and disease risks is proposed as more promising. [Reference list omitted due to space constraints]

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the paper highlights Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) as a prevalent condition affecting at least 1 billion people globally, with potential cardiometabolic and neurocognitive effects [1,2]. The obesity pandemic and population aging are contributing to an increase in OSA prevalence [3]. While Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) treatment can be beneficial for some patients, its tolerance is not always guaranteed [4-7]. CPAP has been linked to improvements in cardiovascular health and all-cause mortality in meta-analyses [5], but definitive trials demonstrating hard outcome benefits are still absent. The study aims to explore potential pharmacological treatments for OSA.

ABSTRACT

This review discusses current and future pharmacological approaches for Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) and their impact on cardiovascular outcomes. The paper summarizes six potential pharmacological strategies: augmenting upper airway dilator muscle activity, stabilizing control of breathing, blocking cardiovascular consequences of OSA, addressing OSA symptoms, promoting CPAP adherence, and facilitating weight loss.

Among the treatments for enhancing upper airway dilator muscle activity, a drug combination of atomoxetine and oxybutynin has shown promising results in reducing the apnea-hypopnea index (AHI), a metric of OSA severity. However, further data are required to determine if these drugs lead to improvements in cardiovascular risk.

Stabilizing control of breathing with oxygen and acetazolamide may be helpful for some patients but has no major studies showing improvements in cardiovascular risk. Blocking the cardiovascular consequences of OSA using beta blockers, antioxidants, or medications targeting gut bacteria is still theoretical or under investigation.

Addressing OSA symptoms with modafinil or solriamfetol is a reasonable approach for treating residual sleepiness in well-treated patients. Treatments to promote CPAP adherence include eszopiclone, nasal decongestion, and office procedures like Vivaer and Rhinaer.

Facilitating weight loss with GLP-1 receptor agonists has demonstrated improvements in both OSA and cardiovascular biomarkers. The SURMOUNT-OSA studies showed that tirzepatide led to significant improvements in the AHI, body weight change, hsCRP, systolic blood pressure, sleep-apnea specific hypoxic burden, and patient-reported outcomes.

Regarding future pharmacological strategies for OSA, orforglipron is an oral GLP1RA undergoing randomized controlled studies, and retatrutide is a triple G agent with promising weight loss results. The review concludes that the future of OSA management will likely continue to include a combination of therapies, targeting mechanical airway obstruction, underlying obesity, and CVD risk factors.

CONCLUSION

The conclusion of the paper highlights significant advancements in OSA treatment approaches, with Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) therapy remaining the primary treatment for many patients. Other strategies include preventing apnea recurrence, enhancing PAP adherence, mitigating OSA consequences, promoting weight loss, and exploring pharmacotherapy. The paper suggests that a combination of these approaches will likely optimize clinical outcomes for OSA patients.

The paper discusses two promising pharmacological agents: an oral dual agonist medication and Bimagrumab, an activin inhibitor promoting skeletal muscle growth. While the results for the former are not yet available from ongoing studies (TRIUMPH), the latter could have a significant role in OSA pharmacotherapy due to the major weight loss it induces.

Amylin-based agents, such as eloralintide and cagrelintide, are also being developed for weight loss using GLP1 independent mechanisms. Though the exact combination and timing of these pharmacological strategies with PAP therapy in OSA treatment remains unclear, tirzepatide provides substantial benefits to patients with OSA.

The paper emphasizes that major changes in OSA clinical practice are imminent due to landmark studies on GLP1RAs, but there is currently insufficient evidence to define when and how pharmacologic strategies should be incorporated into the treatment of OSA. The authors recommend PAP as the standard of care for OSA evaluation and treatment by sleep medicine specialists, although the optimal timing for integrating pharmacological strategies remains uncertain.
