Final Summary

This 2017 scientific paper, published in Nature Scientific Reports, explores gene expression patterns within the preBötC, a respiratory control center in the brain, focusing on Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 neurons. The research question investigates the unique characteristics of these neuron types in regulating breathing.

The study utilizes genetic crossings and micropipette isolation techniques to isolate Dbx1-derived preBötC neurons from neonatal mice, followed by RNA sequencing on an Illumina HiSeq 2500. The key findings reveal that Dbx1 neurons are primary excitatory, glutamatergic neurons, and a subset exhibits chloride-mediated synaptic inhibition. Both types of neurons express tachykinin receptors but lack significant differences in their endogenous ligands. Synaptotagmin-10, involved in synaptic exocytosis, is significantly differentially expressed in Dbx1 samples. Inhibitory non-Dbx1 preBötC neurons express Gata2 and Htr1a, while Dbx1 preBötC neurons express Evx1 and En1. The analysis also identifies transcription factors associated with parafacial and serotonergic raphé neurons but not preBötC neurons.

The study provides a foundation for further comparative analyses during development and presents testable predictions for experiments in adult rodents. The findings can be leveraged to design new experiments aimed at manipulating Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 neuron development and physiology, shedding light on the neural generation and control of breathing.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This study, published in Nature Scientific Reports in 2017, analyzes gene expression in Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 neurons within the preBötC, a respiratory control center in the brain. Key findings include:

  1. Dbx1 neurons express higher levels of glutamate-related genes, suggesting they are primary excitatory, glutamatergic neurons. A subset of these neurons may also use chloride-mediated synaptic inhibition to suppress activity in other respiratory nuclei or modulate orofacial behaviors during breathing.
  2. Both Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 preBötC neurons express tachykinin receptors (Tacr1 and Tacr3), but no significant differences in expression levels were found for their endogenous ligands, neurokinin A and substance P.
  3. Synaptotagmin-10 (Syt10), involved in synaptic exocytosis, was significantly differentially expressed in Dbx1 samples, potentially associated with synaptic depression observed in preBötC rhythmogenic neurons.
  4. Inhibitory non-Dbx1 preBötC neurons express Gata2 and the 5-HT1A receptor (Htr1a), while Dbx1 preBötC neurons express Evx1 and En1.
  5. The analysis identified several transcription factors associated with parafacial and serotonergic raphé neurons but not preBötC neurons, such as Tlx3 and Egr2 (also known as Krox20).

The study offers a baseline for comparative analyses during development and presents testable predictions for experiments in adult rodents. The findings can be used to design new experiments and approaches that manipulate Dbx1 and non-Dbx1 neuron development and physiology, shedding light on the neural generation and control of breathing.

METHODS

In this study, Dbx1-derived preBötC neurons were identified and isolated from neonatal mice using a combination of genetic crossings and micropipette isolation techniques. The animals were anesthetized, their brainstem slices prepared, and individual neurons were isolated under bright-field microscopy. cDNA synthesis was performed using the SMART-Seq v4 ultra low input RNA kit for Sequencing. Six cDNA libraries were generated from the isolated preBötC neurons and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq 2500. Differential gene expression analysis was performed using DESeq. 2, which revealed several genes with respiratory neurobiological relevance that were differentially expressed between Dbx1-derived and non-Dbx1 neurons. The original data is publicly available in the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus database (GSE100356).
