Final Summary

In the study "Vip+ vagal neurons control allergen-induced responses," Ziai Zhu et al. explored the role of Vip+ vagal sensory neurons in asthma pathogenesis, finding that these neurons play a crucial role in controlling allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 inflammation. The research question was to understand how Vip+ vagal neurons mediate asthmatic responses and their connection with the brainstem.

The study used bulk RNA sequencing of allergen-treated vagal ganglia, chemogenetic manipulations, and cell-type-specific genetic tools to investigate the effects of allergens on vagal neurons. Key findings include:

  1. Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons were identified as two distinct subsets with unique expression patterns and innervation profiles.
  2. Genetic ablation of Vip+ neurons led to reduced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 cytokine expression, while activating these neurons increased responses.
  3. Ngfr, a receptor highly enriched in Vip+ vagal neurons, was upregulated following allergen exposure, and NGFR signaling was necessary for Vip+-mediated airway hyperreactivity.
  4. Vip+ vagal neurons project extensively to the nTS region, and their ablation dampened allergen-induced activation of neurons in the ipsilateral nTS.

The conclusions suggest that Vip+ vagal neurons bridge peripheral allergen sensing and central output, revealing a neuroimmune mechanism along the body-brain axis contributing to asthma pathogenesis. The study also highlighted the functional importance of NGFR signaling in Vip+-neuron-mediated allergen-induced responses and identified Vip+ neurons as critical mediators of allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 cytokine responses in asthma.

However, the study had limitations such as using genetic manipulations that targeted molecularly defined Vip+ vagal neurons rather than selectively targeting the lung-innervating subset, and lack of detailed immune profiling and identification of specific postsynaptic targets. Future studies will be essential to determine the role of VIP neuropeptide and its primary functional source in driving allergen responses.

PREAMBLE

In the study "Vip+ vagal neurons control allergen-induced responses", Ziai Zhu, Yujuan Su, and Xin Sun found that Vip+ vagal sensory neurons play a crucial role in controlling allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 inflammation. These neurons act through NGFR signaling to mediate asthmatic responses and relay allergen-derived signals from the lung to the nTS in the brainstem, defining a neuroimmune circuit contributing to asthma pathogenesis. The researchers identified Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons as two distinct vagal subsets with unique expression and innervation profiles. Ablation of Vip+ neurons led to reduced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 cytokine expression, while activating these neurons increased responses. Bulk RNA sequencing of allergen-treated vagal ganglia showed upregulation of Ngfr, a receptor highly enriched in Vip+ vagal neurons, and NGFR signaling was necessary for Vip+-mediated airway hyperreactivity. The findings specify a vagal population that bridges peripheral allergen sensing and central output, revealing a neuroimmune mechanism along the body-brain axis that contributes to asthma.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction discusses the role of lungs, specifically their innervation by vagal sensory neurons, in various physiological processes including breathing, lung stretch, and protective reflexes such as coughing and bronchoconstriction. These neurons are heterogeneous and implicated in pathological conditions like asthma. Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting millions worldwide, characterized by airflow obstruction, mucus hypersecretion, increased immune infiltrates, and exacerbated airway smooth muscle constriction triggered by allergens. The pathogenesis of asthma involves innate and adaptive type 2 immune responses.

This study focuses on Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons, two molecularly distinct subtypes within the Trpv1+ vagal sensory lineage, to understand their role in allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and asthmatic responses. The researchers confirmed their non-overlapping expression patterns and distinct projection profiles in the lung, and found that genetic ablation of Vip+ neurons attenuated HDM-induced airway hyperreactivity and suppressed expression of Il33 and type 2 cytokines Il4 and Il5. Conversely, chemo-genetic activation of sensitized Vip+ vagal neurons was sufficient to induce these responses in the absence of the final dose of allergen. The study also revealed widespread transcriptional changes, including upregulation of genes involved in structural plasticity and cellular stress adaptation, and a functional role for NGFR signaling in Vip+-neuron-mediated asthmatic responses. Lastly, it was found that Vip+ vagal neurons project extensively to the nTS region, and their unilateral ablation dampens allergen-induced activation of neurons in the ipsilateral nTS, suggesting a link between peripheral allergen detection and central airway control.

RESULTS

This study investigates the effects of allergens on vagal neurons and their role in asthma. Repeated allergen challenges alter the transcriptional landscape of vagal neurons, affecting myelination, mitochondrial respiration, energy metabolism, cytoskeletal remodeling, protein homeostasis, and complex assembly.

Vip+ neurons were found to be necessary for asthmatic hyperreactivity and selective type 2 cytokine expression. Chemogenetic activation of sensitized Vip+ neurons increased respiratory system resistance and elastance, and Il33 and Il4 expression, but not Il5 and Il13 levels.

The study focused on the NGFR signaling pathway in the NGFR-enriched Vip+ vagal neurons (75.75% express Ngfr at baseline). Blocking NGFR signaling during chemogenetic activation attenuated respiratory system resistance and elastance, indicating that NGFR signaling is required for Vip+-neuron-mediated allergen-induced responses.

Ablation of Vip+ vagal neurons reduced the number of activated nTS (nucleus tractus solitarius) neurons following allergen exposure, suggesting these neurons are required for allergen-induced nTS activation. The study suggests that NGFR signaling primarily mediates airway hyperreactivity rather than cytokine induction downstream of Vip+ neuron activation.

DISCUSSION

The study investigates the role of Vagal sensory neurons, specifically Vip+ neurons, in asthma. By analyzing published single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data and using cell-type-specific genetic tools, it was found that Vip+ neurons are critical mediators of allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity and type 2 cytokine responses in asthma. The study also identified Ngfr as a highly upregulated gene in Vip+ neurons, and pharmacological blockade of NGFR signaling impaired Vip+-mediated asthmatic hyperresponsiveness, highlighting the functional importance of this signaling pathway.

The study demonstrated that Vip+ and Tac1+ neurons are distinct subtypes of Trpv1+ neurons with non-overlapping expression patterns and different lung innervation profiles. Vip+ neurons were found to be required for HDM-induced allergen responses, as their ablation significantly blunted airway hyperreactivity and reduced expression of Il33, Il4, and Il5. Chemogenetic activation of sensitized Vip+ neurons markedly induced airway hyperreactivity and increased Il33 and Il4 levels.

The findings suggest that Vip+ neurons modulate both neuroepithelial and neuroimmune crosstalk to the magnitude and nature of airway allergic responses. However, whether VIP neuropeptide is mechanistically involved in this context remains unclear. Future studies using cell-type-specific manipulations will be essential to determine the role of VIP and its primary functional source in driving allergen responses.

The study also revealed widespread transcriptional changes in vagal sensory neurons following repeated allergen exposure, with Ngfr emerging as a key player. The neurotrophin-NGFR signaling axis was found to be functionally required for Vip+-mediated asthmatic response, but the specific neurotrophin ligands involved remain to be defined.

Limitations of the study include the use of genetic manipulations that target molecularly defined Vip+ vagal neurons rather than selectively targeting the lung-innervating subset, and the lack of detailed immune profiling and identification of specific postsynaptic targets.
