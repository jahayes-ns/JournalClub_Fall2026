GABAA receptor mediated facilitation of proprioceptive afferent transmission to motoneurons is facilitated by TRPM8 channels on C-fibres that are gated by serotonin

Black, Li, Hari, Lin, Stephens, Fouad, Fenrich, Lucas-Osma, Gorassini, Bennett | 2026

Final Summary

This study, titled 'GABAA receptor mediated facilitation of proprioceptive afferent transmission to motoneurons', investigates the role of serotonin in modulating the monosynaptic stretch reflex (MSR) from proprioceptive Ia afferents to motoneurons. The research was conducted by a team from the Neuroscience and Mental Health Institute, University of Alberta, Canada, and the Faculty of Rehabilitation Medicine at the same university.

The study reveals that 5-HT1D receptors on C-fibres, typically associated with nociceptive fibers, unexpectedly influence proprioceptive transmission. Activation of C-fibers increases action potential propagation in Ia afferents via a long-lasting tonic depolarization, mediated indirectly by augmenting GABAergic input onto Ia afferents, which activates nodal GABAA receptors. Conversely, activation of 5-HT1D receptors with the anti-migraine drug zolmitriptan inhibits C-fibre activity, reducing the GABAergic tonic depolarization on Ia afferents and spike propagation to motoneurons.

The research suggests that spontaneous nociceptive C-fiber activity may increase proprioception and impact the clinical use of zolmitriptan, an anti-migraine drug. The methods included intracellular recording, dorsal root potential (DRP), rapid phasic PAD and Dorsal Root Reflexes (DRRs), ventral horn microstimulation, extracellular field recordings, and ventral root reflex recordings. The results showed that icilin depolarized C-fibers while zolmitriptan hyperpolarized them in the presence of TTX and full synaptic block.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the monosynaptic reflex (MSR), which is crucial for maintaining balance and posture. Exaggerated MSR due to a lack of supraspinal inhibition can lead to spasticity-related tremors, clonus, and spasms. Brainstem-derived 5-HT plays an inhibitory role in controlling the MSR through the Gi protein-coupled 5-HT1D receptor. This receptor is exclusive to C-fibres, suggesting an indirect influence on the MSR.

The paper aims to investigate how C-fibres and 5-HT impact sensory transmission to motoneurons in the ventral horn, with a focus on understanding nociceptive activity's role in spasticity following injury. The use of the 5-HT1D receptor agonist zolmitriptan in treating migraines further motivates this research regarding its influence on proprioception in the neurologically intact spinal cord.

Primary afferent depolarization (PAD) is a significant modulator of afferent transmission, facilitating the MSR through axonal GABAA receptors, primarily located near nodes of Ranvier in myelinated proprioceptive afferents. The paper explores how 5-HT1D receptors on C-fibres influence spike propagation and the MSR, as well as investigating C-fibre induced tonic PAD's role in facilitating axonal conduction and sensory transmission to motoneurons. The study expects zolmitriptan to inhibit C-fibres, reduce tonic PAD, and subsequently decrease sensory conduction in the MSR pathway.

METHODS

The scientific paper describes experimental methods used in neurophysiology, focusing on the study of the spinal cord and sensory afferents. Key techniques include intracellular recording, dorsal root potential (DRP), rapid phasic PAD and Dorsal Root Reflexes (DRRs), ventral horn microstimulation, extracellular field recordings, and ventral root reflex recordings.

The study was conducted on the effects of icilin (TRPM8 receptor agonist) and zolmitriptan (5-HT1D receptors agonist) on C-fibers in adult rat spinal cords, carried out in vitro. To block synaptic transmission, substances like TTX, CNQX, APV, strychnine, and gabazine were used.

The results showed that icilin depolarized C-fibers while zolmitriptan hyperpolarized them in the presence of TTX and full synaptic block. Data analysis was performed using Clampfit 8.0 and Sigmaplot software, with means and standard deviations (SD) presented in figures. A paired t-test was used to test for statistical differences, with a significance level of P < 0.05.

Immunolabeling and image acquisition techniques were also described for visualizing the experiment's results.

RESULTS

The study, titled "A comprehensive investigation of GAD2+ neurons and their innervation by C-fibres," published in a preprint on bioRxiv, explores the role of C-fibers in modulating proprioceptive afferent conduction through GABAergic neurons. The research reveals that C-fibers make direct contacts with GAD2 neurons, reducing an endogenous tonic GABA tone which facilitates afferent spike initiation and conduction. Inhibiting C-fibers decreases sensory transmission to and from the ventral horn. The study provides a 3D reconstruction of these neurons and offers insights into pain processing mechanisms. DISCUSSION

In this scientific paper, researchers explore the influence of C-fibers, typically associated with pain sensation, on the stretch reflex in the spinal cord. They discovered a tonic Proprioceptive Afferent Depolarization (PAD) caused by C-fibers that increases sensory transmission to motoneurons through GABAergic microcircuits and extrasynaptic GABAA receptors, potentially reducing branch point failure. Brainstem-derived serotonin inhibits this process, thus reducing sensory transmission. The findings suggest C-fibers can facilitate non-noxious sensory transmission in large proprioceptive afferents, with implications for complex spinal motor circuits and pain sensitivity.
