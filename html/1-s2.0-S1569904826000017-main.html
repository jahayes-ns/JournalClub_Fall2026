Final Summary

The study investigates the impact of dexmedetomidine, a sedative and analgesic drug, on swallowing and respiration in response to superior laryngeal nerve (SLN) stimulation in rats. The research question explores the effects of dexmedetomidine on physiological functions such as heart rate, blood pressure, and arterial blood gases during infusion.

The methods involved monitoring these parameters during SLN stimulation and continuous dexmedetomidine infusion. Key findings reveal a biphasic temporal profile of swallowing and respiration, with initial suppression leading to hypoventilation in Phase I, followed by resuscitation in Phase II. Circulatory suppression, including hypotension and bradycardia, was also reported during SLN stimulation.

The authors suggest that the SLN contains afferent fibers from the aortic arch, and activation of the SLN significantly contributes to baroreflex. They propose that animals are sensitive to dexmedetomidine-induced bradycardia due to minimal potentiation of GABAergic pathways.

Under hypoxic conditions (PaO2 < 75 mmHg), dexmedetomidine suppresses both respiration and swallowing, but not hypercapnic conditions (PaCO2 up to 80 mmHg). Continuous injection of dexmedetomidine proves useful for studying the interaction between swallowing and respiration in animal experiments.

The study was funded by department resources with no conflicts of interest or personal relationships affecting the work reported. Data is available upon request, with tables detailing changes in body temperature, pH, PaCO2, PaO2, glucose levels, hematocrit, and hemoglobin at different time points during dexmedetomidine infusion provided in Tables 1 and 2.

The authors acknowledge technical assistance from Takashi Hashimoto and Yoichiro Kitajima, with Mina Ooka Takahashi assisting in manuscript preparation.
