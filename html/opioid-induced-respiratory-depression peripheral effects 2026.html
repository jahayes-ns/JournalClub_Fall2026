ABSTRACT

This review paper focuses on the impact of opioids and enkephalins on peripheral sensory inputs controlling breathing. Morphine and enkephalins activate vagal pulmonary C-fiber afferents, causing apnea in rodent models, accompanied by increased laryngeal nerve activity and airway constriction. These effects can be reversed with naloxone. However, the influence of opioids on other vagal afferents or central processing is unclear.

Previous studies have suggested that opioids inhibit carotid body activity, but recent evidence reveals fentanyl stimulates carotid body activity via κ-opioid receptors (k -ORs), involving calcium signaling in glomus cells. k-OR agonists reduce fentanyl-induced respiratory depression in awake animals.

Opioids also cause skeletal muscle rigidity, with increased intercostal activity and reduced diaphragmatic function, potentially through spinal reflexes. The findings suggest that opioids significantly impact peripheral sensory systems related to breathing, offering new insights into previously unexplored roles in opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD).

Keywords: Pulmonary vagal C fibers, Laryngeal constriction, Carotid body, Glomus cells, k-opioid receptors, Ca2+ signaling.
