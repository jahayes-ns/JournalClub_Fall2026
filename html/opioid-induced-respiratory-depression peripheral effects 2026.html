ABSTRACT

This review summarizes the impact of opioids and enkephalins on peripheral sensory pathways controlling breathing. Morphine and enkephalins activate vagal pulmonary C-fiber afferents, causing apnea in rodents, increased laryngeal nerve activity, and airway constriction. These effects can be reversed with naloxone, but the extent to which opioids affect other vagal afferents or central processing is unclear. Previous research suggested opioids inhibit carotid body activity, but recent evidence shows fentanyl stimulates carotid body activity via κ-opioid receptors (k-ORs), involving calcium signaling in glomus cells. k-OR agonists reduce fentanyl-induced respiratory depression in awake animals. Opioids also cause skeletal muscle rigidity, with increased intercostal activity and reduced diaphragmatic function. The study highlights the significant role of peripheral sensory systems in opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD) that was previously unexplored.
