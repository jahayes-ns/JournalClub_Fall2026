The review article in Clinical Science (2026) investigates the impact of circadian gene expression on blood pressure, particularly in relation to Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), which consists of intermittent hypoxia and sleep fragmentation. The study aims to understand how disruptions in circadian rhythm genes like BMAL1 and CLOCK exacerbate hypertension through mechanisms such as hypoxia-induced gene regulation (HIF-1α).

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This review article from Clinical Science (2026) explores the role of circadian gene expression in the influence of intermittent hypoxia and sleep fragmentation on blood pressure. The paper focuses on obstructive sleep apnea (OSA), which consists of two key components: intermittent hypoxia and sleep fragmentation, and their effects on hypertension pathophysiologic processes.

The study investigates the impact of circadian disruption on exacerbating hypertension via mechanisms such as hypoxia-induced gene regulation, primarily through hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α). The review also highlights that conditioning with controlled moderate intermittent hypoxia could potentially have beneficial effects in the heart and vasculatures by modulating PER2, HIF-1α, and BMAL1 expression.

The authors propose that novel therapies, like intermittent hypoxia conditioning (controlled exposures to mild hypoxic episodes followed by periods of normoxia or hyperoxia), could be developed for treating OSA and improving cardiovascular health, particularly in populations with disrupted sleep patterns or heightened salt sensitivity.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the scientific paper discusses the relationship between Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA), chronic intermittent hypoxia, and circadian clock genes. OSA leads to recurrent hypoxia during sleep, affecting circadian rhythm genes such as BMAL1 and CLOCK, which regulate sleep-wake cycles and metabolism. This disruption contributes to sleep deprivation, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, and its complications.

Treatment options for OSA include continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), oral appliances like mandibular advancement splints (MAS), and surgical interventions. CPAP's effectiveness can be influenced by factors like OSA severity, baseline hypertension, adherence, and obesity. MAS are less effective than CPAP but may have comparable overall health outcomes due to higher patient adherence. Surgical interventions have potential risks and benefits, and further research is needed on their safety, effectiveness, and long-term physiological impact.

Intermittent hypoxia conditioning (IHC) could potentially improve cardiovascular health by modulating HIF-1α and other pathways similar to those seen in sleep apnea. IHC has been used as a non-pharmacological intervention for various diseases, including hypertension. A 2022 study showed that mild IHC reduced systolic blood pressure over 24 hours after 15 days of exposure. However, sleep fragmentation (SF), independent of IH, also plays a significant role in the pathophysiology of OSA by contributing to circadian rhythm disruption and cardiac dysfunction.

The paper also highlights the complex interaction between salt sensitivity, genetic alterations in circadian rhythm genes, and systemic hypertension. Genetic alterations in core circadian clock genes can disrupt blood pressure rhythmicity, altered salt sensitivity, impaired renal sodium handling, and dysregulated aldosterone signaling. These findings could lead to more personalized treatment approaches for salt-sensitive hypertension, informed by circadian rhythm regulation, and novel therapies targeting circadian clock genes dysregulated by OSA hold potential for improving cardiovascular health.
