Astrocyte activation in the ventrolateral medulla modulates breathing and arousal states

Oliveira, Miranda, Lim, Severs, Takakura, Moreira, Li, Kalume, Ramirez | 2026

Final Summary

This scientific paper explores the role of astrocytes in the ventral respiratory column (VRC) in modulating both breathing and arousal states, particularly during hypoxia. The study was conducted on Aldh1l1Cre mice using optogenetics, fiber photometry, and electrophysiology to investigate neural activity in the VRC.

Key findings reveal that astrocyte activation increases respiratory frequency, irregularity, and arousal responses, activating C1/A1 neurons in the VRC. This interaction is mediated by purinergic signaling through Kir4.1 channels and P2Y1 receptors. Astrocytes were found to be activated during hypoxia, increasing tidal volume (VT) and inducing arousal followed by sighs, particularly during non-rapid eye movement sleep (NREMS) and rapid eye movement sleep (REMS).

The findings suggest that central medullary astrocytes play an essential role in complementing oxygen sensing by the carotid bodies (CB) and in modulating adaptive responses to low oxygen environments, potentially contributing to the failure to arouse in sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS). The study provides evidence for functional astrocyte-to-neuron signaling within the VRC, suggesting potential therapeutic targets for treating sleep disorders and respiratory diseases.

PREAMBLE

The preamble of this scientific paper presents a study on the impact of astrocyte activation in the ventrolateral medulla on breathing and arousal states. The research was conducted by a team of scientists from the Norcliffe Foundation Center for Integrative Brain Research, Fred Hutchchinson Cancer Center, Instituto de Ciencias Biomedicas (Universidade de Sao Paulo), and the Department of Physiology and Biophysics (Instituto de Ciencias Biomedicas, Universidade de Sao Paulo). The study is currently under review for publication in Nature Communications. The research findings suggest that astrocyte activation in the ventrolateral medulla modulates both breathing and arousal states, providing new insights into the neurophysiological mechanisms underlying these essential functions.

ABSTRACT

The scientific paper focuses on the role of astrocytes, specifically those in the ventral respiratory column (VRC), in regulating arousal and sigh generation in mice. The study reveals that a particular subset of Aldh1l1 cells in the VRC are activated prior to sighing during periods of hypoxia, and their activation increases the likelihood of arousals with sighs. This activation also leads to increased calcium transients in catecholaminergic neurons in the VRC before arousal with sighs. The findings suggest that medullary astrocytes can modulate sigh generation and arousal transitions, and are essential for the ventilatory and arousal response to hypoxia.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper highlights the importance of breath regulation for maintaining life, with a focus on the brainstem network that controls respiration. This network includes specialized neurons and astrocytes in the ventral respiratory column (VRC), particularly the pre-Bötzinger Complex (preBötC) which acts as the primary rhythm generator for breathing. Astrocytes have been found to play a crucial role in maintaining network stability, managing transitions between breathing phases, and responding to oxygen and carbon dioxide levels. They also influence the sleep-wake cycle and cognitive functions. Recent research suggests that astrocytes may generate sighs through the release of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), which activates P2Y1 receptors in the preBötC. This study aims to investigate the role of VRC astrocytes in regulating state-dependent breathing and arousal, and their connection to sighing events.

RESULTS

The research focuses on the role of astrocytes in the ventral respiratory column (VRC) in regulating arousal and sigh generation, particularly their interaction with C1 neurons. Key findings include:

  1. Photo-stimulation of astrocytes increases respiratory frequency, irregularity, and arousal responses, as well as activating C1/A1 neurons in the VRC.

  2. Astrocyte-neuron interaction is maintained by P2Y1 signaling, as shown by the complete abolishment of astrocyte-driven sigh generation and arousal when both Kir4.1 channels and P2Y1 receptors were blocked.

  3. Astrocytes in the VRC are activated during hypoxia, as indicated by increased Fos expression.

The study provides evidence for functional astrocyte-to-neuron signaling within the VRC, suggesting potential therapeutic targets for treating sleep disorders and respiratory diseases.

DISCUSSION

The study explores the role of astrocytes in hypoxia-induced arousal and sigh generation in freely behaving mice. Astrocytes in the ventral respiratory column (VRC), specifically the preBötC, were found to increase tidal volume (VT) and induce arousal followed by sighs, particularly during non-rapid eye movement sleep (NREMS) and rapid eye movement sleep (REMS). Conversely, astrocyte stimulation in the rostral ventrolateral medulla (RTN) did not evoke sighs or arousal but increased eupneic breathing.

The study also reveals that purinergic signaling through Kir4.1 channels and P2Y1 receptors is essential for astrocyte-induced arousal with sighs. Partial depletion of astrocytes in the VRC did not abolish the hypoxic ventilatory response to hypoxia, suggesting the important role of carotid bodies (CB) in mediating the hypoxic response and the generation of sighs.

The findings support the idea that central medullary astrocytes play an essential role in complementing oxygen sensing by the CB and in modulating adaptive responses to low oxygen environments, potentially contributing to the failure to arouse in sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS). The study illustrates a broader role for astrocytes and neurons in coordinating the hypoxic response across multiple brain regions.

METHODS

The study conducted on Aldh1l1Cre mice utilized a combination of optogenetics, fiber photometry, and electrophysiology to investigate neural activity in the Ventral Respiratory Column (VRC) of the mouse brain. Mice were injected with AAV-CAG-FLEX-jGCaMP8m-WPRE into the VRC, and optogenetic stimulations were performed using a laser to activate specific neurons. Fiber photometry was used to record changes in calcium levels (indicating neural activity), while electrophysiology measured diaphragm muscle activity and respiratory parameters.

During the study, Kir4.1 blocker and P2Y1-antagonist drugs were administered to modulate neural activity in the VRC. Sigh events were defined based on diaphragm muscle activity, and correlations between specific regions of interest (ROI) and time differences between cell activity and sigh generation were investigated during hypoxia. Two-Photon Astrocytes calcium imaging, electrophysiology, and Two-Photon Cross-Correlation Analyses were performed. Photoconversion was achieved using a Bruker Investigator microscope, Prairie-View imaging software, and a Spectra-Physics InSight X3 tunable laser for imaging. Data analysis was conducted using ImageJ and Sigma Stat version 11.0 software, with all data available in the main text or supplementary information, accessible upon request from the corresponding authors (L.M.O. or J.M.R.). It's unclear whether plethysmography chamber measurements for ventilation, sighs, and breathing regularity were conducted during these experiments or as separate studies.
