In vivo multiplex imaging of dynamic neurochemical networks with designed far-­red

Zheng, Cai, Wang, Zhang, Zhuo, Dong, Zhang, Y. Zhang, Wang, Deng, Ji, Cui, Fang, Xinxin Zhang, Huang, Kecheng Zhang, Jinxu Wang, Grimm, Johnsson, Schreiter, Lavis, Chen, Mu, Li

Final Summary

The scientific papers under investigation develop and apply novel dopamine sensors (HaloDA1.0, HaloDA1.0-JF646, rACh1h) to examine the complex interplay among dopamine, acetylcholine, and cyclic adenosine monophosphate in D1-medium spiny neurons of the nucleus accumbens in mice.

HaloDA1.0 was created for far-red and NIR imaging applications, offering optimized properties. A three-color imaging method, utilizing this sensor along with rACh1h and another dopamine sensor (HaloDA1.0-JF635 or SiR650), was employed to monitor these neurotransmitters in the nucleus accumbens.

In a separate study, transgenic mice expressing HaloDA1.0, rACh1h, and GFlamp2 in D1-MSNs (DIO-GFlamp2 mice) were used to investigate interactions between these neurotransmitters under various conditions (spontaneous activity, sucrose solution, foot shock, and cocaine administration). The data was normalized and analyzed for correlations at specific time points.

Both studies contribute valuable insights into drug addiction and other neuropsychiatric disorders by providing evidence of new interactions between ACh and endocannabinoid signaling pathways.

Simultaneously, Regan et al. have experimentally demonstrated the first realization of a topological quantum error correction (QEC) code using single photons on a programmable linear optics quantum processor. The QEC process was verified through Clifford+T gate operations and Bell state measurements, suggesting potential implications for enhancing the reliability and scalability of quantum computing systems.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The combined preambles of these scientific papers detail research on various dopamine sensors (HaloDA1.0, HaloDA1.0-JF646, and rACh1h) and their applications.

  1. HaloDA1.0 is a novel dopamine sensor developed for far-red and NIR imaging applications with optimized properties. It was characterized in HEK 293T cells and used in a study to create a three-color imaging method to monitor dopamine, acetylcholine, and endocannabinoids in the nucleus accumbens of mice. The sensor's performance was compared to other sensors like HaloDA1.0-JF635 and SiR650.

  2. Another study investigated the interaction between dopamine, acetylcholine, and cyclic adenosine monophosphate in D1-medium spiny neurons (D1-MSNs) of the nucleus accumbens using a three-color recording technique. The experiment utilized transgenic mice expressing HaloDA1.0, rACh1h, and GFlamp2 in D1-MSNs (DIO-GFlamp2 mice). The data was normalized and correlations were analyzed at specific time points under different conditions (spontaneous activity, sucrose solution, foot shock, and cocaine administration).

Both studies provide insights into the complex interplay between these neurotransmitters, with potential implications for drug addiction and other neuropsychiatric disorders. The research on HaloDA1.0 also demonstrated new interactions between the ACh and endocannabinoid signaling pathways.

DISCUSSION

The paper by Regan et al. presents the first experimental realization of a topological quantum error correction (QEC) code using single photons on a programmable linear optics quantum processor. Clifford+T gate operations were employed to encode, manipulate, and measure a seven-qubit surface code. Three types of errors, including bit flips, phase flips, and dephasing errors, were introduced and corrected using ancillary qubits through topological QEC codes. The QEC process was verified by successfully simulating five-qubit and seven-qubit Bell state measurements, demonstrating the feasibility of QEC in quantum information processing. Key findings include the successful experimental realization of topological QEC with single photons, Clifford+T gate operations, and verification through Bell state measurements. The research has potential implications for improving the reliability and scalability of quantum computing systems.
