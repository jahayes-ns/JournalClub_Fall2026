In vivo multiplex imaging of dynamic neurochemical networks with designed far-­red dopamine sensors

Zheng, Cai, Wang, Zhang, Zhuo, Dong, Zhang, Wang, Deng, Ji, Cui, Fang, Xinzhang, Huang, Kecheng Zhang, Jinxu Wang, Grimm, Johnsson, Schreiter, Lavis, Chen, Mu, Li

Final Summary

Title: Development and Application of HaloDA1.0 Sensor for Studying Dopamine Dynamics in Living Animals and Investigating Cocaine's Effects on Neurotransmitter Release

This study presents the development of a new dopamine sensor, HaloDA1.0, using a circularly permutated HaloTag protein and far-red dye JF646. The sensor exhibits strong, transient fluorescence upon ligand application with an EC50 of 150 nM and maximum ΔF/F of ~900%. The sensor was shown to be effective in mice and zebrafish for measuring dopamine dynamics during various behaviors and in studying the effects of cocaine on dopamine signaling.

The study also explores the modulation of dopamine (DA) and acetylcholine (ACh) release in D1-Medium Spiny Neurons (D1-MSN) of D1R-Cre mice using LED illumination, three-color recording, and fiber tip techniques. Key findings include suppression of both DA and ACh release by cocaine administration, significant negative correlation between cAMP levels and both DA and ACh during cocaine administration, and differences in the modulation of DA and ACh release by sucrose solution and foot shock.

The research advances genetically encoded indicators of neuronal activity through advancements in near-infrared and far-red fluorescent proteins, optimized for live-cell and single-molecule microscopy, super-resolution imaging, chemigenetic indicators, HaloTag protein labeling technology, environmentally sensitive color-shifting fluorophores, deuterated auxochromes, rational design of fluorogenic labels, and improved GRAB sensors for monitoring neurotransmitter release. The study aims to provide a better understanding of brain function, particularly in the context of dopamine release and addiction.

In addition, the paper presents a novel design for an artificial photosynthesis system using earth-abundant materials. This system efficiently splits water molecules under sunlight to produce hydrogen fuel, achieving stable operation for over 100 hours without significant performance degradation. The study highlights the potential of this technology for sustainable energy production using abundant renewable resources like sunlight and water. Future research may focus on increasing efficiency and reducing costs.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The research paper discusses the development of a new dopamine sensor, HaloDA1.0, which uses a circularly permutated HaloTag protein and far-red dye JF646. The sensor was optimized for strong, transient fluorescence upon ligand application, with an EC50 of 150 nM and maximum ΔF/F of ~900%. The sensor has potential uses in various applications, including multiplex imaging to measure dopamine, acetylcholine, and endocannabinoids simultaneously.

The study demonstrates the sensor's effectiveness in mice and zebrafish, showing robust increases in fluorescence in response to field stimuli or electrical body shock. The results provide insights into cross-talk among these key neurotransmitters and could lead to a better understanding of neurological disorders and novel therapeutic strategies.

In another part of the paper, HaloDA1.0 is used to measure dopamine dynamics in mice, accurately detecting changes during various behaviors such as reward and aversion responses. The sensor was also used to study the effects of cocaine on dopamine signaling. Overall, the study highlights the potential utility of HaloDA1.0 as a tool for studying dopamine dynamics in living animals.

The Modulation of Dopamine and Acetylcholine Release in D1-Medium Spiny Neurons (D1-MSN) during Cocaine Administration study uses LED illumination, three-color recording, and fiber tip techniques to investigate the modulation of dopamine (DA) and acetylcholine (ACh) release in D1-MSN of D1R-Cre mice. Key findings include suppression of both DA and ACh release by cocaine administration, significant negative correlation between cAMP levels and both DA and ACh during cocaine administration, and differences in the modulation of DA and ACh release by sucrose solution and foot shock. The study used various experimental conditions such as spontaneous activity, 5% sucrose solution, foot shock, cocaine injection, and their respective correlations. Data was collected at different time intervals and presented using Z-scores, correlation coefficients, and merged images of DAPI, GFlamp2, rACh1h, and HaloDA1.0.
