Molecular mechanism of NMDA receptor potentiation by lactate

Fiumelli, Herrera-López, Lemtiri-Chlieh, Mottier, Girgis, Ben-Adiba, Jourdain, Carrano, Mahmood, Ooi, Arold, Di Luca, Gardoni, Magistretti | 2026

Final Summary

The scientific paper published in The Journal of Physiology (2026) investigates the role of lactate as a signaling molecule that enhances neuronal energy demands, promotes synaptic plasticity, and aids in memory consolidation. Lactate modulates NMDA receptor (NMDAR) currents in cortical neurons, increasing their amplitude and decay time constant through an interaction with Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII). This effect is mediated by monocarboxylate transporters and lactate dehydrogenase, requiring cellular uptake of lactate, its metabolic conversion to pyruvate, and NADH formation within neurons. The process involves calcium signaling, specifically Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII), which interacts directly with the GluN2B subunit of the receptor. Lactate strengthens the interaction between CaMKII and GluN2B through redox-sensitive cysteine residues in GluN2B, facilitating synaptic localization of this complex and enhancing synaptic responses. This research offers a molecular pathway linking brain metabolism with learning and memory processes.

PREAMBLE

The paper published in The Journal of Physiology (2026) presents a mechanism by which lactate enhances neuronal NMDA receptor (NMDAR) function and synaptic plasticity. Lactate enters neurons via monocarboxylate transporters (MCTs), is oxidized by lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) to pyruvate, generating NADH and increasing the intracellular NADH/NAD+ ratio. This redox shift promotes Ca2+ release from ryanodine-sensitive stores (RyR), leading to recruitment of Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) to GluN2B-containing NMDARs at intracellular redox-sensitive cysteine residues. This interaction enhances NMDAR currents and prolonged decay kinetics, strengthening NMDAR–CaMKII signalling which increases the presence of NMDARs at postsynaptic sites, facilitating molecular processes underlying long-term synaptic plasticity and memory formation. RESULTS

The study discusses the effects of lactate on NMDA receptors (NMDARs) and its interaction with Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII).

  1. Lactate potentiates NMDAR function in a CaMKII-dependent manner, increasing the conductance of NMDAR currents.
  2. This effect requires cellular uptake of lactate and an interaction between CaMKII and GluN2B subunits of NMDARs.
  3. Two redox-sensitive cysteine-rich sequences within the disordered C-terminal domain (CTD) of the GluN2 subunit are implicated in lactate-induced potentiation of NMDARs.
  4. Lactate treatment enhances the association between CaMKII and GluN2B compared to pyruvate treatment, leading to an increase in synapses where GluN2B is expressed in close proximity to PSD-95. This effect is prevented by a CaMKII inhibitory peptide.

Overall, the study suggests that lactate may play a critical role in modulating CaMKII-dependent synaptic plasticity and NMDAR function in neurons through redox modifications within the C-terminal domain of the GluN2 subunit. However, further research is needed to fully understand these mechanisms.

DISCUSSION

In this study, researchers investigated the molecular mechanisms linking lactate, a signaling molecule involved in synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory, to the enhancement of NMDAR currents that underlie these processes. Lactate was found to enhance NMDAR responses by increasing both the amplitude and decay time constant of NMDAR currents in cultured cortical neurons. This effect is specific to NMDARs containing GluN2B subunits, which play a crucial role in neuronal development and synaptic plasticity. The concentration range used in the study suggests that local concentrations within perisynaptic microdomains may transiently reach higher levels due to astrocytic glycogen mobilization and glycolytic bursts.

The mechanism of this amplification involves the uptake of lactate into neurons, its metabolic conversion to pyruvate, concomitant NAD+ to NADH conversion, a shift in cellular redox state, and stimulation of NMDAR. The redox homeostasis shift promotes Ca2+ mobilization from the endoplasmic reticulum and creates an environment that affects the CTD of NMDAR via cysteine modification, facilitating the interaction between CaMKII and GluN2B subunits, thus boosting synaptic plasticity. This study provides a molecular resolution of the interaction between lactate and NMDARs, emphasizing the importance of the neuron-astrocyte functional unit in linking energy metabolism with neurotransmission and higher brain functions.
