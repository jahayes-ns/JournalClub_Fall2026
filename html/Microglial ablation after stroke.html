Timed microglia depletion promotes functional network reorganization and motor recovery after ischaemic stroke

Isla Cainzos, Quandt, Borggrewe, Altjohann, Magnus, Biskamp | 2026

Keywords: ischaemic stroke, CSF-1R inhibitor, microglial repopulation, sensorimotor networks, motor rehabilitation

Final Summary

Title: Transient Microglia Depletion Promotes Cortical Network Reorganization and Motor Recovery After Stroke

In this study, researchers from the Department of Neurology at University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf, Germany, investigate the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase after ischemic stroke on functional recovery and neuronal network dynamics in rats. The research question was to understand how modulating microglial activity affects cortical reorganization following stroke, potentially leading to new strategies for treating neurodegenerative diseases.

The methods involved inducing strokes via middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO), followed by PLX5622 treatment for 7 days, a 3-week monitoring period, histological analysis, single-cell sequencing of microglia, behavioral testing using the Sprint-Precision-Reaching (SPR) task, and in vivo electrophysiology to analyze ECoG data from freely behaving mice.

Key findings include accelerated motor recovery in PLX5622-treated mice compared to controls, as well as changes in sensorimotor network dynamics such as increased lower beta-band connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex and reduced gamma synchronization in the contralesional sensorimotor system. These alterations were linked to improvements in fine motor skills.

The conclusions suggest that microglia depletion may contribute to recovery from stroke by modulating local network reorganization within the sensorimotor system. The study found an increase in functional connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex, which was significantly associated with improved contralateral motor performance. The findings could represent a compensatory mechanism facilitating recovery, although limitations such as the exclusive use of male animals, reliance on a single behavioral assay, and short duration of ECoG recordings were noted.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study investigates the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase after ischaemic stroke on functional recovery and neuronal network dynamics. Using a C57BL/6 wild-type stroke model, researchers assessed fine motor performance and bilateral cortical activity through epidural electrocor-ticography. Microglia were depleted using PLX5622, a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor inhibitor, between days 3 and 7 post-stroke. The depletion resulted in near-complete restoration of fine motor function by day 7, accompanied by significant functional connectivity changes in bilateral sensorimotor networks, including increased beta-band connectivity in the ipsilesional motor cortex, which correlated with contralateral fine motor improvement. Following repopulation, microglial cells showed altered morphology and gene expression profiles. The findings suggest that transient microglial modulation during the subacute phase after stroke promotes cortical network reorganization and motor recovery. The study was conducted at the Department of Neurology, University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf, Germany.

INTRODUCTION

This study explores the effects of PLX5622, a drug that inhibits CSF1R signaling and causes microglia depletion, on cortical reorganization following stroke in rats. The experiment includes stroke induction via middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO), PLX5622 treatment for 7 days followed by a 3-week monitoring period, histological analysis, single-cell sequencing of microglia, and behavioral testing using a SPR task. The research aims to understand how depletion and repopulation of microglia impact cortical reorganization post-stroke, potentially leading to new strategies for treating neurodegenerative diseases. Additionally, the study employs in vivo electrophysiology, analyzing ECoG data from freely behaving mice, to investigate the role of microglia in cortical organization.

RESULTS

The study investigates the effects of short-term microglia depletion after a stroke on motor recovery and changes in sensorimotor network dynamics.

  1. Microglia depletion accelerates motor recovery: Treating mice with PLX5622 following a stroke led to faster recovery of fine motor skills, as measured by the Sprint-Precision-Reaching (SPR) task, compared to placebo or vehicle treatment groups.

  2. Changes in sensorimotor network dynamics: After a stroke, the study found increased lower beta-band connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex and reduced gamma synchronization in the contralesional sensorimotor system. These alterations were linked to improvements in fine motor skills.

The findings suggest that microglia depletion may contribute to recovery from stroke by modulating local network reorganization within the sensorimotor system. The study used two groups of mice: PLX-treated (n=7 for chronic treatment, n=14 for stroke treatment) and a vehicle control group (n=12 for both), with a sham group (n=7) receiving no treatment. Results showed an increase in imaginary coherence in PLX-treated mice compared to the vehicle-treated stroke group, indicating a role for microglia in cortical reorganization. Data are presented as mean ± SEM.

DISCUSSION

The study demonstrates that short-term microglia depletion between days 3 and 7 after sensory cortical stroke in mice accelerates fine motor recovery and modulates functional connectivity within bilateral sensorimotor networks. Microglial activation states influence neuronal network dynamics, affecting synaptic structure and function. The study suggests that targeted suppression of activated microglia during a critical sub-acute window after ischaemic injury may facilitate synaptic plasticity and network reorganization, thereby enhancing functional recovery.

The study found that timed microglia depletion led to improved performance in the SPR task by day 7 post-stroke, coinciding with the peak of depletion, while vehicle-treated controls required 14 to 21 days to reach similar functional levels. The behavioral improvement persisted through microglial repopulation, reaching day 28 post-stroke.

The study also found an increase in functional connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex, which was significantly associated with improved contralateral motor performance. This region includes primary and secondary motor cortices, consistent with human data demonstrating beneficial effects of enhanced connectivity between primary and secondary motor areas in stroke recovery.

The study suggests that low beta (10-20 Hz) connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex may reflect the formation of expanded motor networks involving secondary motor areas and could represent a compensatory mechanism facilitating recovery. However, the study has limitations, including the exclusive use of male animals, reliance on a single behavioral assay, and short duration of ECoG recordings. Future studies will be necessary to delineate the underlying pathways and investigate the dynamic influence of microglia on functional connectivity in more detail.
