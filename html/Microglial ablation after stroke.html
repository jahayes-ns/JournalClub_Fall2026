Cainzos, Quandt, Borggrewe, Altjohann, Magnus, Biskamp | 2026

Keywords: ischaemic stroke, CSF-1R inhibitor, microglial repopulation, sensorimotor networks, motor rehabilitation

Final Summary

This study investigates the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase after ischemic stroke on functional recovery and neuronal network dynamics in mice. The researchers used PLX5622, a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor inhibitor, to deplete microglia between days 3 and 7 post-stroke. Using a skilled reaching task and epidural electrocorticography (ECoG), they assessed fine motor performance and recorded bilateral cortical activity longitudinally.

The study found that transient microglial modulation during the subacute phase after stroke led to near-complete restoration of fine motor function by day 7, accompanied by significant functional connectivity changes in bilateral sensorimotor networks, including increased beta-band connectivity in the ipsilesional motor cortex, which correlated with contralateral fine motor improvement. Following repopulation, microglial cells showed altered morphology and gene expression profiles.

The findings suggest that transient microglial modulation during the subacute phase after stroke promotes cortical network reorganization and motor recovery. This study highlights the potential of targeted suppression of activated microglia during a critical subacute window after ischaemic injury to facilitate synaptic plasticity and network reorganization, enhancing functional recovery. However, the study has limitations, including the exclusive use of male animals and reliance on a single behavioral assay. Future studies are needed to delineate the underlying pathways and investigate the influence of cortical spreading depolarizations on function connectivity. Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

In this study, researchers investigated the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase after ischemic stroke on functional recovery and neuronal network dynamics. Using a C57BL/6 wild-type stroke model, they assessed fine motor performance with a skilled reaching task and recorded bilateral cortical activity longitudinally through epidural electrocorticography. Microglia were depleted between days 3 and 7 post-stroke using PLX5622, a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor inhibitor. The results showed near-complete restoration of fine motor function by day 7, accompanied by significant functional connectivity changes in bilateral sensorimotor networks, including increased beta-band connectivity in the ipsilesional motor cortex, which correlated with contralateral fine motor improvement. Following repopulation, microglial cells showed altered morphology and gene expression profiles. These findings suggest that transient microglial modulation during the subacute phase after stroke promotes cortical network reorganization and motor recovery.

INTRODUCTION

The study explores the effects of short-term PLX5622 treatment on microglia and their impact on cortical reorganization post-stroke, using a mouse model. The research employs various methods, including MRI imaging, microglia depletion assessment, histology, single-cell sequencing, behavior evaluation, and electrophysiological data recording using ECoG during free behavior exploration in a circular arena.

The study designs three groups: sham, vehicle (VEH), and stroke (STR) treated with PLX5622. After four days of treatment and seven days of withdrawal, MRI scans, flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, and single-cell RNA sequencing were conducted to analyze the microglia remaining in the penumbra area, their complexity, cell subsets, and differentially expressed genes. Fine motor skills were evaluated using a SPR task, with individual baselines defined for each mouse.

Concurrently, electrophysiological data was collected using ECoG during free behavior exploration in a circular arena, identifying active and resting behaviors, and computing power spectral density (PSD) across frequency bands like theta, low beta, high beta, and gamma. Connectivity analysis used the imaginary part of cross-spectral coherence, while statistical analyses were performed in Prism and R, with various ANOVA and multiple comparison tests applied based on data distribution and group comparisons.

This study aims to elucidate how microglia affect cortical reorganization following stroke in mice by investigating changes in their numbers, complexity, and function as well as the associated neurophysiological effects.

RESULTS

In this study, researchers investigated the impact of short-term microglia depletion using PLX5622 on motor recovery and sensorimotor network dynamics following a stroke.

  1. Motor Recovery: PLX treatment accelerated motor recovery as indicated by improved scores in the SPR task compared to vehicle-treated mice.

  2. Sensorimotor Network Dynamics: The study found increased lower beta connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex of PLX-treated mice on day 7 post-stroke, only during active periods. Additionally, an increase in theta connectivity between ipsilesional motor and sensory cortices was observed in PLX-treated animals compared to vehicle-treated stroke animals on days 7 and 14. Conversely, a decrease in gamma synchronization in the contralesional sensorimotor system was found beginning as early as day 3 post-stroke. These alterations were correlated with motor function recovery.

The analysis of ch1-ch3 imaginary coherence spectrum of PLX-treated mice revealed a more significant increase in imaginary coherence for the PLX-treated stroke group compared to the vehicle-treated group, suggesting that microglia play a role in cortical reorganization after a stroke. The study involved 15 PLX-treated and 12 vehicle-treated mice across various time points post-stroke, with each data point representing an individual mouse. Statistical tests like the mixed-effects model (REML) and Mann–Whitney test were employed to compare different groups and their fold-changes in imaginary coherence.

DISCUSSION

This study demonstrates that short-term microglia depletion between days 3 and 7 after sensory cortical stroke in mice accelerates fine motor recovery and modulates functional connectivity within bilateral sensorimotor networks. Microglial depletion led to significant early improvement in skilled forelimb use, which persisted throughout microglial repopulation. The study suggests that microglial activity during the subacute phase critically shapes the trajectory of post-stroke recovery. The findings highlight the potential of targeted suppression of activated microglia during a critical subacute window after ischaemic injury to facilitate synaptic plasticity and network reorganization, enhancing functional recovery. The study also indicates a significant decrease in connectivity within a contralesional sensorimotor network, which may reflect compensatory recruitment of contralateral networks or altered forelimb usage. However, the study has limitations, including the exclusive use of male animals and reliance on a single behavioral assay, and future studies are needed to delineate the underlying pathways and investigate the influence of cortical spreading depolarizations on function connectivity.
