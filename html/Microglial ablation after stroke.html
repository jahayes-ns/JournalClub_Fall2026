Final Summary

This study, published in Brain Communications, investigates the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase following ischemic stroke on functional recovery and network dynamics in mice. The researchers used PLX5622, a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor inhibitor, to deplete microglia for five days. They found that this treatment significantly improved fine motor skills by day 7, as demonstrated by better performance in a sensorimotor test (SPR task).

The study also revealed changes in sensorimotor network dynamics, with an increase in lower beta (10-20 Hz) connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex during active periods, and a decrease in gamma (30-60 Hz) connectivity in the contralesional sensorimotor system. The findings suggest that PLX treatment may influence microglia and contribute to cortical reorganization following a stroke.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

In this study, published in Brain Communications, the authors investigated the impact of short-term microglia depletion during the subacute phase following ischemic stroke on functional recovery and network dynamics. Using a C57BL/6 wild-type stroke model, they found that microglia depletion with PLX5622, a colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor inhibitor, near-completely restored fine motor function by day 7. This was accompanied by changes in functional connectivity in bilateral sensorimotor networks, including increased beta-band connectivity in the ipsilesional motor cortex, which correlated with contralateral fine motor improvement. Following repopulation, microglial cells showed altered morphology and gene expression profiles. The findings suggest that transient microglial modulation during the subacute phase after stroke promotes cortical network reorganization and motor recovery.

INTRODUCTION

This study investigates the effects of short-term treatment with PLX5622 on microglia and their impact on cortical reorganization following a stroke in mice. The experiment consisted of several stages, including random assignment of mice to control, stroke, vehicle, or PLX groups; induction of stroke in PLX and vehicle groups; daily intraperitoneal injections of PLX5622 for 5 days for the PLX group; assessment of microglial depletion through flow cytometry and immunohistochemistry; behavioral tests to evaluate fine motor skills using a SPR task; single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) analysis to identify changes in gene expression patterns related to microglia and cortical reorganization.

Results showed that short-term PLX5622 treatment led to microglial depletion followed by repopulation, a significant reduction in stroke volume, improved fine motor skills in the PLX group compared to vehicle, and changes in gene expression profiles of homeostatic and reactive microglia subsets.

The study also included an additional experiment that evaluated locomotor activity, Electroencephalography (ECoG) data recording, connectivity analysis, statistical analysis using Prism and R, and presentation of data as mean ± SEM. The primary focus of this additional study is on understanding how microglia affect cortical reorganization after stroke in mice.

RESULTS

In this study, a drug named PLX5622 was used to temporarily deplete microglia in a mouse model of stroke. The findings indicate that mice treated with PLX exhibited improved motor recovery as early as three days after stroke onset, as demonstrated by better performance in a sensorimotor test (SPR task).

The research also investigated changes in sensorimotor network dynamics following the treatment. On day 7, PLX-treated mice showed an increase in lower beta (10-20 Hz) connectivity within the ipsilesional motor cortex during active periods, as well as a decrease in gamma (30-60 Hz) connectivity in the contralesional sensorimotor system.

The improvement in motor function recovery and changes in network dynamics suggest that PLX treatment may influence microglia and contribute to cortical reorganization following a stroke. The group sizes ranged from 7 to 14 mice, and statistical analysis was performed using a mixed-effects model with Tukey’s multiple comparisons test.

DISCUSSION

The study shows that short-term microglia depletion between days 3 and 7 after sensory cortical stroke in mice accelerates fine motor recovery and modulates functional connectivity within bilateral sensorimotor networks. Microglia, which are rapidly activated in response to cellular injury, play a dual role in post-stroke recovery, promoting tissue repair while also releasing pro-inflammatory mediators that can contribute to secondary neuronal damage. The study demonstrates that timed microglia depletion during the subacute phase (days 3-7) improves performance in a skilled forelimb use task and leads to enduring recovery, with these effects persisting through microglial repopulation. The findings suggest that targeted suppression of activated microglia during this critical period may facilitate synaptic plasticity and network reorganization, enhancing functional recovery.

The study also found an increase in low beta connectivity (<20 Hz) within the ipsilesional motor cortex, which may reflect the formation of expanded motor networks involving secondary motor areas and could represent a compensatory mechanism facilitating recovery. However, the study has limitations, including uncertainties about whether repopulated microglia fully recapitulate the functional properties of their predecessors, and the need for further studies to delineate the underlying pathways and mechanisms.
