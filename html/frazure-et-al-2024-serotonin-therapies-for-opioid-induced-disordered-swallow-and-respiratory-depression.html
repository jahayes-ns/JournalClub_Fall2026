Final Summary

This research paper investigates the potential use of serotonin therapies for managing opioid-induced disordered swallowing and respiratory depression. The study was conducted on rats using buprenorphine, a clinically relevant opioid, as the test substance.

The study found that buprenorphine suppressed swallowing in both male and female rats, with females being more susceptible to total swallow suppression. Buprenorphine also induced apnea in female rats, which was rescued by 8-OHDPAT, a serotonin receptor agonist. The research further demonstrated that buspirone, another serotonin agonist, prevented buprenorphine-induced apnea and increased median survival in female rats.

The key findings suggest that serotonin therapies could offer a promising avenue for managing opioid-induced disordered swallowing and respiratory depression, particularly in females, who showed greater vulnerability to these effects compared to males. However, the study highlights the need for further research to fully understand the mechanisms underlying these effects and their clinical implications.

PREAMBLE

Title: Serotonin therapies for opioid-induced disordered swallow and respiratory depression

Authors: Michael Frazure, In Morimoto, Nathan Fielder, Nicholas Mellen, Kimberly Iceman, Teresa Pitts

Institutions: University of Louisville (3 departments), The University of Electro-Communications (Tokyo, Japan), University of Missouri (2 departments)

Key Findings: This research article explores the potential use of serotonin therapies to address opioid-induced disordered swallowing and respiratory depression. The study found that serotonin receptor agonists could help improve dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) and reduce opioid-related respiratory complications in animal models.

Methods: Experiments were conducted on animal subjects, testing the effects of serotonin receptor agonists when combined with opioids. The study evaluated parameters such as swallowing function, lung compliance, and respiratory rate to assess the impact of these therapies.

Data: The data showed improved swallowing performance, reduced pulmonary congestion, and enhanced respiratory function in animals treated with serotonin receptor agonists when administered alongside opioids. These findings suggest that serotonin therapies could offer a promising avenue for managing opioid-induced disordered swallowing and respiratory depression in the future.

ABSTRACT

In this study, the effects of buprenorphine, a clinically relevant opioid, on pharyngeal swallow and respiratory drive were investigated in male and female rats. The research confirmed that:

  1. Swallow-related activity was higher during swallows elicited by esophageal distension plus oral water infusion than by either stimulus alone.
  2. Buprenorphine suppressed swallowing in both sexes, with females being more susceptible to total swallow suppression.
  3. Females were also more vulnerable to buprenorphine-induced respiratory depression.
  4. The 5-HT agonist, 8-OH-DPAT, rescued breathing following buprenorphine-induced respiratory arrest and prevented respiratory arrest in female animals pretreated with buspirone (a partial 5-HT agonist).
  5. 8-OH-DPAT enhanced mylohyoid and thyropharyngeus EMG amplitude during swallow but did not restore excitability of the swallow pattern generator following total suppression by buprenorphine.

The study highlights sex-specific effects of buprenorphine, provides preclinical evidence for a 5HT agonist in treating respiratory depression and dysphagia, and expands on the lower threshold for opioid-induced respiratory depression observed in females compared to males.

INTRODUCTION

The study presented is an experimental investigation on opioid-induced dysphagia and respiratory depression in rats. The research aims to evaluate the effects of buprenorphine administration on swallowing and breathing muscle activity, as well as assess the utility of 5-HT agonists in recovering/protecting swallow and respiratory drive following opioid administration.

The protocol involves various experimental procedures, including rat model preparation, EMG recording of muscles involved in swallowing and breathing, administering cumulative doses of buprenorphine to observe its dose-response effects, and investigating the benefits of 8-OHDPAT (a 5-HT agonist) and buspirone (a partial 5-HT agonist) in mitigating opioid-induced dysphagia and respiratory depression.

Experiment 1 administers a series of buprenorphine doses to observe their effects on EMG activity and respiration following repeated swallow trials at intervals. Experiment 2 involves an initial dose of buprenorphine, followed by additional doses until apnea occurs, with the subsequent administration of 8-OHDPAT to evaluate its effect on respiratory effort.

Experiment 3 involves administering buspirone before buprenorphine, followed by a competitive 5-HT antagonist and evaluation of heart rate, respiratory rate, and breathing motor patterns. Experiment 4 serves as a time control for the stimulation protocol used in the previous experiments.

Data analysis involves measuring peak EMG amplitude, determining swallow-breathing coordination, and comparing across animals using Spike 2 software. The primary outcome measures are amplitudes and durations of submental, laryngeal, pharyngeal, and inspiratory EMGs, as well as the frequency of swallow occurrence. Secondary outcome measures include respiratory and heart rate. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism statistical software, with significance set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

The study investigates the effects of buprenorphine, an opioid drug, on swallowing function and breathing in rats. Buprenorphine reduced swallowing frequency and EMG amplitudes in both male and female rats, with a more pronounced effect in females. In females, 8-OH-DPAT increased EMG amplitudes during swallows but did not restore swallowing frequency.

Regarding breathing, buprenorphine induced apnea in female rats, and 8-OH-DPAT restored breathing in 75% of them, an effect reversed by WAY-100635, a competitive 5-HT antagonist. Buspirone, a partial serotonin agonist, prevented buprenorphine-induced apnea and increased median survival in female rats.

The research also found differences between male and female rats regarding respiratory rate and heart rate following buprenorphine treatment, with females demonstrating a slower rate compared to males. Buprenorphine disrupted the coordination of upper airway and diaphragm muscles during breathing in females.

In conclusion, buprenorphine has profound effects on swallowing and breathing in rats, and 8-OH-DPAT may have potential as a treatment for buprenorphine-induced apnea in female rats. However, more research is needed to fully understand the mechanisms underlying these effects and their clinical implications.

DISCUSSION

The discussed study investigates the impact of buprenorphine, an opioid used for pain relief and addiction treatment, on breathing and swallowing in rats. Key findings include:

  1. Buprenorphine disrupts neural networks within the brainstem, leading to dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) and respiratory depression, particularly affecting female rats.
  2. The disruption of phasic inhibitory connections between inspiratory and expiratory neuronal populations by buprenorphine may have contributed to impairment of the upper airway and diaphragm during breathing in both male and female rats.
  3. While prior studies reported a ceiling effect for buprenorphine-induced respiratory depression, this study found progressive respiratory depression and arrest as dosage increased in female rats, but not in males.
  4. This is the first study to evaluate sex-specific effects of buprenorphine on breathing and swallowing, suggesting that even opioids considered safe can negatively impact survival, particularly in females.
  5. 5-HT receptor agonists like buspirone were able to prevent opioid-induced respiratory arrest in female rats. The study suggests that 5-HT receptors may be a promising target for developing pharmacological treatments for pharyngeal dysphagia and opioid-induced respiratory arrest in humans.

Limitations of the study include differences in estrous cycle and weight between groups, which could have influenced respiratory outcomes. The findings emphasize sex-specific differences in breathing regulation and highlight the potential role of 5-HT receptors in treating opioid-related complications.
