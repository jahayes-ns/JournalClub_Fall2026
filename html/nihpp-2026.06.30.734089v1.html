An optimized 'hypoxia in a pill' regimen reverses neurodegenerative disease phenotypes in multiple preclinical models

Wang1,2,3, Marutani4, Zazzeron4, Menard4, Volpicelli-Daley5, Ichinose4, Mootha1,2,3

Final Summary

The study aims to optimize a novel "hypoxia-in-a-pill" regimen combining GBT601 and PT2399 for therapeutic applications in neurodegenerative diseases, including Leigh syndrome, Friedreich’s ataxia, and Parkinson's disease. The combination halts neurodegeneration and reverses neurological symptoms, demonstrating comparable effectiveness to continuous 11% oxygen breathing. Remarkably, it significantly extended the median lifespan of the Leigh syndrome model upon initiation after disease onset without inducing weight loss or pulmonary hypertension. The findings encourage further pre-clinical and potential clinical studies for safety and efficacy evaluation. While preprint, the research provides promising results and is funded by esteemed organizations like the National Institutes of Health.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study presents an optimized "hypoxia-in-a-pill" regimen that combines GBT601, a second-generation hemoglobin affinity enhancer, with PT2399, a HIF-2α inhibitor. This regimen was found to induce therapeutic hypoxia and demonstrate strong efficacy in three different mouse models of neurodegenerative diseases: Leigh syndrome, Friedreich’s ataxia, and Parkinson’s disease. The GBT601/PT2399 combination effectively halted neurodegeneration and reversed neurological symptoms, showing comparable effectiveness to continuous breathing of 11% FIO2. Notably, this regimen led to a significant extension in the median lifespan of the Leigh syndrome model, from approximately 62 days to 158 days, even when initiated after disease onset. The combination did not induce weight loss or pulmonary hypertension, and its findings motivate further pre-clinical and potential clinical studies for safety and efficacy evaluation.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the therapeutic benefits of chronic moderate hypoxia (CMH), as suggested by various epidemiological studies, anecdotal evidence, and preclinical research. Living at high altitude has been linked to lower mortality rates from cardiovascular diseases, stroke, cancer, Alzheimer’s disease, and Parkinson's disease. Preclinical mouse studies since 2016 have demonstrated that continuous exposure to 7-11% oxygen provides protective effects across various models of neurological and mitochondrial diseases.

However, capturing these benefits requires continuous rather than intermittent regimens, as daily hypoxic bouts are ineffective or even detrimental. Long-term exposure to chronic, continuous hypoxia is associated with potential side effects such as pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular hypertrophy. To address these challenges, the authors propose a small molecule cocktail for inducing therapeutic hypoxia, combining hemoglobin affinity enhancers (such as GBT440 and its second-generation equivalent GBT601) with HIF-2α inhibitors to achieve effective hypoxia without adverse cardiac side effects. The paper seeks to optimize the regimen using GBT601/PT2399 combination in preclinical mouse models of mitochondrial and neurodegenerative diseases.

RESULTS

The text you provided appears to be a series of research abstracts related to studies on the effects of certain drug combinations (GBT601/PT2399) on various neurological diseases, including Leigh syndrome, Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy (LHON), Leigh-like syndrome, mitochondrial complex I deficiency, Leigh syndrome caused by SURF1 mutation, Leigh syndrome caused by MTPTE mutation, LHON caused by the G11778A mutation in the MT-ND6 gene, and Parkinson's disease (PD).

In each study, the drug combination was shown to improve symptoms or slow the progression of the respective diseases. For example, in Leigh syndrome, the drug combination improved motor function, reduced brain tissue hyperoxia, and decreased levels of malondialdehyde (MDA), a marker of oxidative stress. In PD, the drug combination reversed motor dysfunction, improved balance, and reduced brain tissue hyperoxia and MDA levels.

It's important to note that these are preprint studies and have not yet been peer-reviewed or published in a scientific journal. While they provide promising results, further research is needed to confirm the efficacy and safety of GBT601/PT2399 for treating these neurological diseases.

The study focuses on the combined effects of PT2399 and GBT601 on oxygen saturation, motor function, and heart structure in animal models.
