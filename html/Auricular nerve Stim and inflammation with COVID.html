Auricular vagus nerve stimulation: a new option to treat inflammation in COVID-19?

Fernando Mendes Sant’Anna, Rodrigo César Lima Resende, Lucas Bonacossa Sant’Anna, Sérgio Lívio Menezes Couceiro, Romero Bonacossa Soares Pinto, Mariana Bonacossa Sant’Anna, Liaw Wen Chao, Jozsef Constantin Szeles, Eugenijus Kaniusas | 2023

Final Summary

Title: Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation as a Novel Approach for Inflammation Treatment in COVID-19

This review article, published in Revista do Association Medica Brasileira (2023), investigates the potential of auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS) as an innovative method for managing inflammation associated with COVID-19. The research question focuses on exploring aVNS as a co-adjuvant treatment for inflammatory manifestations related to the virus, supported by pre-clinical evidence demonstrating its efficacy in modulating cytokine storms and reducing inflammatory activity in animal models of inflammation.

The study employs various methods of aVNS, including manual stimulation and electronic devices targeting specific points on the ear. Key findings suggest potential benefits such as reduced inflammation and improved immune function, but emphasize the need for further research to validate its effectiveness in treating COVID-19 complications. The article concludes with recommendations for future studies focusing on aVNS mechanisms, clinical applications, and safety considerations when implementing this novel treatment option.

The paper was written by several authors including Sant'Anna FM, LBS, RCLR, MBS, RBSP, EK, SLMC, and LWC, with JCS providing writing, review, and editing. The article is referenced as Rev Assoc Med Bras 2023;69(6):e20230345.

Title: Auricular Vagus Nerve Stimulation as a Novel Approach for Inflammation Treatment in COVID-19

The review article, published in Revista do Association Medica Brasileira (2023), explores the potential of auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS) as an innovative method for managing inflammation associated with COVID-19. The authors discuss the underlying mechanisms linking aVNS and inflammatory responses, supported by recent studies demonstrating its efficacy in modulating cytokine storms – hyperinflammatory states common in severe COVID-19 cases.

The study investigates various methods of aVNS, including manual stimulation and electronic devices targeting specific points on the ear. It highlights potential benefits, such as reduced inflammation and improved immune function, while acknowledging the need for further research to validate its effectiveness in treating COVID-19 complications. The article concludes with recommendations for future studies focusing on aVNS mechanisms, clinical applications, and safety considerations when implementing this novel treatment option.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of the scientific paper discusses auricular vagus nerve stimulation (aVNS), a non-invasive method for stimulating the vagus nerve through the ear, specifically in the concha and inferior part of the tragus. This technique is proposed as a new analgesic and anti-inflammatory intervention due to its physiological effects being comparable to those of cervical vagus nerve stimulation (VNS).

Pre-clinical evidence supports the effectiveness of aVNS in animal models of inflammation, with benefits including reducing inflammatory activity, preventing tissue injury, improving intestinal transit, and reducing shock. Furthermore, aVNS has shown promise in treating lethal endotoxemia or polymicrobial infection by reducing the production of tumor necrosis factor.

In the context of COVID-19, the paper proposes that aVNS could be a promising co-adjuvant treatment for inflammatory manifestations associated with the virus. The authors suggest three methods for implementing transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS): burst stimuli, continuous stimuli, and a new concept of vagal stimulation.
