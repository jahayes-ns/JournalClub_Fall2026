Review: Cell-type-specific APOE4 cascade across the Alzheimer’s disease continuum

Shostak, Liang, Huang | 2026

Final Summary

The scientific paper under review explores Apolipoprotein E4 (APOE4) as a leading genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's disease (AD), highlighting its impact on different cell types across the AD continuum (preclinical, prodromal, and dementia stages). The study proposes a temporal model demonstrating how age-related stress escalates neuronal APOE4 expression, initiating early neuronal impairments in preclinical AD. This damage instigates a harmful glial cell response that contributes to neurodegeneration and expedites the onset of prodromal and dementia AD.

Key findings indicate that APOE4 triggers hyperexcitability, oxidative stress, and endo-lysosomal impairments in neurons, exacerbating early cellular stress and toxicity. It also impairs mitochondrial health independently of other pathologies during preclinical AD, amplifies preclinical amyloid burden, and initiates immune dysregulation through increased inflammatory biomarkers, leading to microglial activation and neuroinflammation in preclinical and prodromal stages.

Three potential therapeutic approaches are suggested: pre-emptive mitigation of neuronal APOE4 toxicity, targeting the excitatory/inhibitory network imbalance, and thwarting gliosis. The paper underscores the importance of comprehending the temporal and cell-type-specific effects of APOE4 throughout the AD continuum to develop effective therapies for approximately two-thirds of AD patients.

The research was supported by grants from the National Institutes of Health, with Y.H. being a co-founder and board chair of GABAeron, Inc. Better longitudinal biomarkers are vital for defining therapeutic windows, patient stratification, and tracking treatment responses.
