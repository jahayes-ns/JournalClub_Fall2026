Final Summary

Title: Role of Carotid Bodies as Metabolic Sensors During High-Intensity Exercise: A Controversial Debate

In this study published in The Journal of Physiology, Andrade et al. (2025) investigate whether Carotid Bodies (CBs) function as metabolic sensors during high-intensity exercise, focusing on their response to elevated blood lactate concentrations. Using anesthetized rats and arterial-venous lactate clamp, the researchers observed that CB removal abolished changes in tidal volume, ventilatory frequency, and pulmonary ventilation during lactate exposure, suggesting a potential role of CBs in metabolic sensing. However, the use of high lactate concentrations raised concerns about non-specific effects on ion channels in glomus cells.

The study corroborates previous findings by López-Barneo's group (López-Barneo, 2022), but conflicting results and negative studies necessitate further investigations to establish a definitive role for CBs. The methods employed include standard biochemical analyses and cell culture experiments. This research advances our understanding of the complex interplay between CBs and metabolic adjustments during exercise, paving the way for future studies to resolve existing controversies and clarify the true nature of CBs' role as a metabolic sensor.

PREAMBLE

This scientific paper, published in The Journal of Physiology, explores whether Carotid Bodies (CBs) act as a metabolic sensor during high-intensity exercise. The study by Andrade et al. (2025) characterized ventilatory responses to arterial-venous lactate clamp in anesthetized rats and found that the removal of CBs abolished changes in tidal volume, ventilatory frequency, and pulmonary ventilation in response to lactate. The paper raises several questions about the role of CBs as a metabolic sensor during exercise, including the independence of lactate sensing by glomus cells from mitochondrial activity and the contribution of other chemical signals present in arterial blood to the observed CBs-dependent metabolic adjustments.
