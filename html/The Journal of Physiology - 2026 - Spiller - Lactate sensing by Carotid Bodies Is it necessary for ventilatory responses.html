Lactate sensing by Carotid Bodies: Is it necessary for ventilatory responses to exercise?

Spiller, Moraes | 2026

Final Summary

Title: Carotid Bodies as Metabolic Sensors during High-Intensity Exercise: A Controversial Role

In the journal The Journal of Physiology, Andrade et al. (2025) investigate whether Carotid Bodies (CBs) function as metabolic sensors during high-intensity exercise by examining ventilatory responses to arterial–venous lactate clamp in anesthetized rats. The research question revolves around the potential role of CBs in sensing elevated blood lactate concentrations during exercise.

The study's methods involved characterizing ventilatory responses to lactate infusion and demonstrating that bilateral removal of CBs abolished changes in tidal volume, ventilatory frequency, and pulmonary ventilation. Glomus cell inhibition also significantly reduced ventilatory responses to lactate without affecting O2 consumption.

Key findings include the support for the mechanistic findings presented by López-Barneo's group (López-Barneo, 2022). However, due to conflicting points and negative studies previously reported, a definitive conclusion about CBs' role in this regard should be made with caution.

While the study provides valuable insights, it leaves questions unanswered regarding unmeasured lactate concentration in vessels closer to the carotid vessels, potential non-specific modulation of ion channels by high concentrations of lactate, lack of control for off-targeting transfection, and uncontrolled stress levels during exercise.

In summary, this study supports a potential role for CBs in detecting elevated blood lactate concentrations but highlights the need for further research to definitively establish their function as metabolic sensors during high-intensity exercise.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

This paper, published in The Journal of Physiology, explores whether Carotid Bodies (CBs) act as a metabolic sensor during high-intensity exercise. Andrade et al. (2025) characterized the ventilatory responses to an arterial–venous lactate clamp in anesthetized rats, demonstrating that lactate infusion generated a concentration of approximately 16 mm lactate in the femoral artery and that bilateral removal of CBs abolished changes in tidal volume, ventilatory frequency, and pulmonary ventilation in response to lactate. The study also found that glomus cell inhibition significantly reduced ventilatory responses to intravenous infusion of 1.2 m lactate without affecting O2 consumption. However, there are considerations such as unmeasured lactate concentration in vessels closer to the carotid vessels, potential non-specific modulation of ion channels by high concentrations of lactate, lack of control for off-targeting transfection, and uncontrolled stress levels during exercise that leave questions about the role of lactate sensing by glomus cells in ventilatory responses to exercise unanswered.

RESULTS

The study by Andrade et al. (2025) supports the mechanistic findings presented by López-Barneo's group (López-Barneo, 2022) regarding the potential role of CBs in detecting elevated blood lactate concentrations. However, due to conflicting points and negative studies previously reported, a definitive conclusion about CBs' role in this regard should be made with caution. The methods used involve thorough experimental analysis and data collection.
