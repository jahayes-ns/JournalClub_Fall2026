PREAMBLE

The combined preamble of the two scientific papers focuses on research exploring methods to improve motor and sensory functions for individuals with spinal cord injuries.

  1. The first study uses transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation (tSCS) through a custom-made electrode patch to target specific areas of the cervical spinal cord, combined with activity-based training. After 15 weeks, there was a significant improvement in volitional force output during isometric elbow flexion and extension, translating into functional improvements such as bringing both hands up to the face. However, tSCS did not show promising results for muscles of the hand and triceps or tactile sensation deficits in the distal arm.

  2. The second study involves a brain-computer interface (BCI) system that interprets neural activity to control an orthosis for motor function restoration, using cortical mirroring (CM), deep reinforcement learning agent (DQN RL agent), and transcranial stimulation (tSCS). The BCI improved object recognition tasks success rates and resulted in high precision in grasp and lift of objects. Additionally, the tSCS+CM intervention led to significant improvement in tactile sensitivity on the radial aspect of the right wrist, with participants perceiving sensations as low as 10g of force and exhibiting higher accuracy in localizing the sensation compared to other hand locations. Neural activity in the primary somatosensory cortex (S1) during a 'press and hold' task also showed increased modulation depth for physical stimuli after the tSCS+CM intervention, suggesting short-term plasticity effects that may contribute to improved wrist sensitivity.

In summary, both studies show promise in using various therapeutic modalities to help individuals with spinal cord injuries regain motor and sensory functions, but they also highlight the need for further research to understand their limitations and potential applications.

DISCUSSION

The 'discussion' section of the paper presents a hybrid Dual Neuromodulation (DNB) system designed to restore motor and sensory functions in individuals with spinal cord injuries (SCIs). The DNB consists of an intracortical brain-computer interface (iBCI) for motor control and a dual neuromodulation approach using transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation (tDNS) and peripheral stimuli.

The study tested the DNB on one participant with a sensory complete SCI, demonstrating significant improvements in motor control and sensory recovery. The system's modularity and adaptability make it suitable for home use with two sessions per week, potentially making it more accessible than other tSCS studies.

The authors conclude that the DNB system has promising potential in complete SCI, but challenges remain, such as optimizing stimulation parameters, adapting to interindividual variability, and streamlining identification of effective stimulation patterns. The study showed that the hybrid DNB system can engage brain and spinal pathways for functional grasping and sensorimotor recovery in complete SCI, providing continued benefits even when not actively using the system. METHODS

The scientific paper describes a neuroscience study that involves implanting microelectrode arrays in the motor and somatosensory cortex of a participant. The aim is to decode neural activity related to hand movements and tactile stimuli using a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network.

The LSTM model predicts rest, open, close, and reaching movement states every 100ms after training over 20 sessions and testing on 2 experimental types. Data collection is divided into two phases: offline data recording without feedback for the first nine blocks, followed by real-time decoding feedback for the subsequent 11 blocks.

To evaluate individual electrode tuning to specific cued movements, a Kruskal–Wallis test is performed on neural activity from up to 30 trials of each movement. The LSTM-decoded hand movements are used to wirelessly stimulate muscles for tasks like elbow flexion and extension, and hand opening and closing with varying stimulation parameters.

Linear mixed modeling is employed to analyze the effect of cue-driven electrode stimulation on modulation depth. Data analysis and figure production are done using custom code in MATLAB, Python, and SAS. De-identified data requests can be directed to the corresponding author.
