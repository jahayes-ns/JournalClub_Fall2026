High proportions of respiratory and non-respiratory modulated Pre-Bötzinger complex neurons receive monosynaptic inputs from the pontine respiratory group in rabbit

Yin, Feng, Dillard, Matthew L., Callison, Jennifer J., Zuperku, Edward J., Stucke | 2021

Keywords: Pontine respiratory group, Pre-Bötzinger complex, Respiratory rhythm generation, Post-stimulus time-histogram, In vivo decerebrate rabbit model

Final Summary

The study by Y. Feng et al. (2026) investigates the functional connectivity between the parabrachial nucleus and pre-Botzinger Complex (pre-BötC), key areas involved in respiratory rhythm generation. Using electrical stimulation and PSTH analysis, they found that during pontine stimulation, certain neuronal discharge activities increased more in specific excitatory pontine input types. However, there was no significant difference in Fn change between CO2 levels or among different PSTH types. A regression analysis revealed a strong overlap regarding the change in neuronal discharge frequency vs. Fn before stimulation, with lower initial Fn corresponding to a greater increase in Fn. Higher Fn before stimulation was associated with a larger PSTH amplitude. The data suggests that the ponto-medullary network connectivity is a fundamental feature controlling respiratory phase transitions and modulating the respiratory motor pattern during orofacial behavior such as swallowing. Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The scientific paper by Y. Feng et al. (2026) investigates the functional connectivity between the parabrachial nucleus and pre-Botzinger Complex (pre-BötC), key areas in the brainstem involved in respiratory rhythm generation. The study used electrical stimulation of the parabrachial nucleus and recorded the activity of neurons in the pre-BötC using PSTH analysis at different levels of CO2.

Key findings include: 1. During pontine stimulation, neuronal discharge activity increased more in neurons receiving excitatory pontine inputs, specifically "peak only" and "peak and trough" PSTH types. 2. There was no significant difference in Fn change between CO2 levels within each neuron type or among different PSTH types (p < 0.05, Friedman RN ANOVA on ranks and Kruskal-Wallis ANOVA on ranks). 3. A regression analysis revealed a strong overlap between individual PSTH types regarding the change in neuronal discharge frequency vs. Fn before stimulation. Lower initial Fn corresponded to a greater increase in Fn. 4. Higher Fn before stimulation was associated with a larger PSTH amplitude (p < 0.05). 5. The correlation between Δ Fn and PSTH amplitude, pooled for all PSTH types, exhibited a nearly flat regression curve (slope: 0.027), indicating minimal association.

The study suggests that the high degree of ponto-medullary network connectivity is a hard-wired core feature that controls inspiratory-expiratory phase transitions and modulates and adapts the respiratory motor pattern during orofacial behavior such as swallowing. The data was published in Respiratory Physiology & Neurobiology 343 (2026) with the article number 104591.
