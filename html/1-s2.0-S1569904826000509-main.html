Final Summary

The study by Y. Feng et al. (2026) in Respiratory Physiology & Neurobiology explores the functional connectivity between the parabrachial nucleus (PRG) and the pre-Bötzinger complex (pre-BötC), key nodes of the mammalian respiratory central pattern generator (r-CPG), in regulating breathing patterns in response to CO2 levels. The research question focused on determining monosynaptic connections between these regions.

Methods involved stimulating the PRG and recording from the pre-BötC, analyzing changes in firing probability of pre-BötC neurons relative to PRG stimuli. Key findings include: over 70% of recorded pre-BötC neurons receiving monosynaptic inputs from the PRG; minimal impact of varying CO2 levels on PSTH latency and pre-BötC neuronal discharge frequency; a negative correlation between initial neuronal discharge frequency and increase during stimulation, as well as a positive correlation between initial frequency and PSTH amplitude; and a nearly flat regression curve for the relationship between change in neuronal discharge frequency and PSTH amplitude.

The study's conclusions suggest that descending functional connectivity between the PRG and pre-BötC within the r-CPG may be underestimated in previous experimental studies, highlighting potential species differences or missed connections. PREAMBLE

The study by Y. Feng et al. (Respiratory Physiology & Neurobiology, 2026) investigates the functional connectivity between the parabrachial nucleus (PRG) and the pre-Bötzinger complex (pre-BötC) in regulating breathing patterns in response to CO2 levels. The experiment involves stimulating the PRG and recording from the pre-BötC, observing changes in firing probability of pre-BötC neurons relative to PRG stimuli to determine monosynaptic connections.

Key findings include: 1. Over 70% of recorded pre-BötC neurons receive monosynaptic inputs from the PRG, a higher rate than reported in previous studies, potentially due to species differences or missed connections. 2. Varying CO2 levels had little impact on PSTH latency and only occasionally affected pre-BötC neuronal discharge frequency. 3. Regression analysis shows that lower initial neuronal discharge frequency correlates with a greater increase during stimulation, while higher initial frequency is associated with larger PSTH amplitude. 4. The relationship between the change in neuronal discharge frequency and PSTH amplitude showed minimal variation (nearly flat regression curve). 5. Graphs illustrate mean values, along with 95% confidence intervals, for these analyses.

The study provides evidence for substantial descending functional connectivity between key pontine and medullary network nodes of the mammalian respiratory central pattern generator (r-CPG), which was previously underestimated in experimental studies.
