Title: Heart-Lung Interactions in Health and Disease: Oxygen Supplementation Enhances Oxygen Uptake Kinetics and Exercise Performance in PAH and CTEPH Patients

Authors: Shir Kadosh, Yael Baidats, Andrew M. Jones, Daryl Wilkerson, Ariela Velner, Ronen Reuveny, Michael J. Segel

Affiliations: Tel Aviv University, Sheba Tel HaShomer Medical Center, University of Exeter, University of Haifa, Reichman University, and Tel Aviv University

Abstract: This research article, published in Comprehensive Physiology in 2026, investigates the impact of oxygen supplementation on oxygen uptake kinetics (V̇O2 kinetics) and exercise performance in patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH). The study was conducted by a team of researchers from Tel Aviv University, Sheba Tel HaShomer Medical Center, the University of Exeter, the University of Haifa, Reichman University, and Tel Aviv University.

The research focused on the effects of oxygen supplementation during cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) in PAH and CTEPH patients. The findings suggest that oxygen supplementation significantly improves V̇O2 kinetics and exercise performance in both patient groups. The study used a randomized, crossover design to compare the effects of normoxia and hyperoxia during CPET.

Keywords: CPET, exercise physiology, O2 supplementation, pulmonary vascular disease, V̇O2 kinetics.

ABSTRACT

The study, published as a scientific research paper, investigates the effects of 30% oxygen supplementation during exercise on individuals with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH).

The study found that 30% oxygen supplementation significantly improved peak work rate, peak oxygen consumption, peak V̇O2/HR, and ΔV̇O2/∆WR in non-hypoxemic PAH/CTEPH patients. The mechanism for this improvement involves both an increase in arterial blood oxygen content and a pulmonary vasodilator effect that reduces right ventricular afterload, improves cardiac output, and enhances oxygen delivery to muscle tissue.

The pulmonary vasodilation may be mediated by an increase in alveolar O2 triggering a signaling cascade through endothelial cells, leading to vasodilation of pulmonary smooth muscle. This mechanism could explain the pulmonary vasodilatory effect of oxygen even in non-hypoxemic PAH/CTEPH patients.

The study has limitations, such as a small sample size and single-blinded design, but suggests that further research is needed to explore the potential role of O2 supplementation during exercise training in PAH/CTEPH patients.
