Final Summary

Title: Cellular Receptor TMEM154/Galvanin as an Electrical Gradient Sensor for Cell Migration

In the study published in Cell, Belliveau et al. aimed to identify a cellular receptor responsible for sensing electrical gradients and guiding cell migration towards the cathode. The researchers conducted the first genome-wide loss-of-function electrotaxis screen using human granulocytes and discovered that TMEM154/Galvanin is essential for this process, as Galvanin-deficient cells lost their ability to migrate in an electrical gradient while maintaining chemotaxis.

The team demonstrated that Galvanin quickly repositions itself at the anodal pole of the cell under electric fields and plays a significant role in inducing leading-edge protrusion and trailing-edge retraction. This suggests that Galvanin either inhibits actin cytoskeleton protrusion or induces actomyosin contractility.

The study offers robust evidence for a specifically evolved receptor involved in electrotaxis, opening up possibilities for future research to uncover its biological functions.
