Final Summary

The scientific review 'Magnetogenetics: Tools for Noninvasive Neuromodulation with Cellular Resolution' explores the use of magnetogenetics as a novel approach to control neuronal activity, addressing the limitations of chemogenetics and optogenetics. This technique employs magnetic nanoparticles or protein ferritin as transducers to convert magnetic fields into thermal, mechanical, or biochemical signals that activate ion channels sensitive to these stimuli.

The research question focuses on current approaches, development, limitations, applications, and future directions of magnetogenetics across various research fields, particularly neuroscience. The key methods discussed include SPION-based and ferritin-based magnetogenetics. For the former, SPIONs are used to activate temperature- or mechanosensitive ion channels like TRPV, TMEM16A, and Piezo channels for potential applications in Parkinson's disease models. In the latter, ion channels are coupled with ferritin using various methods such as Magneto2.0, T4F, antibody-based strategies, and FeRIC, although results from independent groups show conflicting outcomes due to varying factors.

Key findings indicate debates over the magnetic field (MF) transduction mechanisms for both MNPs and ferritin, with questions about whether heat generated by RF-AMFs is confined to SPION surfaces or if it dissipates, causing a bulk temperature increase in the surrounding environment. The paper emphasizes the need for consistent reporting guidelines, accurate MF measurements, an open-source database, and improved reproducibility across research groups.

In terms of conclusions, while magnetogenetics offers potential noninvasive, wireless, and depth-unrestricted methods for neuromodulation, ongoing challenges include understanding the underlying mechanisms, improving reproducibility, and developing standardized protocols. Despite these challenges, magnetogenetics has shown promise in various experimental models and may have significant impact on neuroscience and clinical settings.
