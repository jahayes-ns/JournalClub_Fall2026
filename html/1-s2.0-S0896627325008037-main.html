Final Summary

The study by Zhou et al. investigates the role of the microglial C5a-C5aR1 axis in neuroinflammation and cerebral edema caused by traumatic brain injury (TBI) and ischemic stroke (ICH). The research identified a pro-inflammatory microglial subtype, characterized by C5aR1 expression, which is expanded in the injured human brain following TBI or ICH. This C5aR1+ microglia amplify neuroinflammation, promote neurotoxic astrocyte polarization, and enhance neutrophils recruitment, worsening cerebral edema and neurological deficits.

The study found that pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 in preclinical TBI and ICH models ameliorated edema, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target. The FDA-approved drug avacopan, a C5aR1 antagonist, was found to mitigate these harmful effects by suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, restoring microglial morphology, inhibiting AKT and ERK phosphorylation, reducing astrocyte polarization, decreasing infiltrated neutrophils, alleviating cerebral edema, and accelerating functional recovery after TBI and ICH.

The study provides potential therapeutic targets for TBI and ICH by identifying the microglial C5a-C5aR1 axis as a key contributor to neuroinflammation and cerebral edema in these debilitating conditions, with avacopan showing promising results in preclinical models. Further research is needed to confirm these findings and explore the full therapeutic potential of avacopan in these conditions.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Zhou et al. identifies a pro-inflammatory C5aR1+ microglial subtype enriched in human cerebral edema tissues after traumatic brain injury (TBI) and intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). This subtype amplifies neuroinflammation, promotes neurotoxic astrocyte polarization, and recruits neutrophils, leading to increased cerebral edema. Pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 reduces cerebral edema in both TBI and ICH, suggesting a potential therapeutic target for these conditions. The research was conducted by multiple authors from several departments and institutions in China.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the significant role of acute cerebral edema, a common consequence of stroke and traumatic brain injury (TBI), in mortality, disability, and neurodegeneration. Current therapies mainly focus on patient stabilization and symptom relief, but they have limited effectiveness in reducing cerebral edema or improving outcomes. The underlying mechanisms are incompletely understood, and there are no specific pharmacological interventions available. Pro-inflammatory responses, particularly from microglia, are believed to contribute to cerebral edema progression and neurological deterioration after acute brain injury. However, systemic anti-inflammatory strategies have proven ineffective, and targeting reactive microglia has not significantly improved patient outcomes. This study aims to address these gaps by performing single-cell RNA sequencing on CNS cells from human donors with TBI, intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), and controls. The research identifies a pro-inflammatory microglial subtype (C5a receptor 1+) associated with poorer clinical outcomes in acute brain injury. In mouse models, genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of this subtype reduced cerebral edema after TBI and ICH. The study suggests that C5a receptor 1 could be a potential therapeutic target in TBI and ICH.

RESULTS

This scientific paper investigates the role of the microglial C5a-C5aR1 axis in neuroinflammation and cerebral edema caused by traumatic brain injury (TBI) and ischemic stroke (ICH).

Key findings include: - A subset of microglia expressing high levels of C5aR1, which is increased after TBI and ICH and correlates with disease severity. - Elevated plasma C5a levels in both TBI and ICH patients. - Local production of C5a in TBI, primarily from microglia and endothelial cells. - In mice with an inducible knockout of C5ar1 in microglia/macrophages, there is reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, less cerebral edema, improved neurological function, and lower lactate levels (a marker of neuronal injury) after TBI. - C5a-C5aR1 signaling in microglia activates nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), AKT, and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) pathways, leading to increased production of pro-inflammatory factors.

The FDA-approved drug avacopan, a C5aR1 antagonist, was found to mitigate these harmful effects by: - Suppressing the expression of iNOS, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6. - Restoring microglial morphology. - Inhibiting AKT and ERK phosphorylation. - Reducing C3+ astrocytes. - Decreasing infiltrated neutrophils. - Alleviating cerebral edema and accelerating functional recovery after TBI and ICH.

The study suggests a neuroprotective role for Avacopan in treating TBI and ICH by blocking the C5aR1-associated signaling, with potential therapeutic benefits. Further research is needed to confirm these findings and explore the full therapeutic potential of avacopan in these conditions.

In summary, the study identifies the microglial C5a-C5aR1 axis as a key contributor to neuroinflammation and cerebral edema in TBI and ICH, providing potential therapeutic targets for these debilitating conditions.

DISCUSSION

In this study, researchers identified a pro-inflammatory microglial subtype characterized by C5aR1 expression that is expanded in the injured human brain following Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) or Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH). These C5aR1+ microglia amplify neuroinflammatory signaling, promote neurotoxic astrocyte polarization, and enhance neutrophils recruitment, worsening cerebral edema and neurological deficits. Pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 in preclinical TBI and ICH models ameliorated edema, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target.

The study profiled over 200,000 cells from human cortex and edematous tissue, identifying five major microglial subtypes. Among them, an inflammatory subtype with strong pro-inflammatory signatures was markedly increased in TBI and ICH. Activation of C5aR1 in microglia promotes pro-inflammatory signaling via NF-κB, ERK, and AKT pathways, exacerbating cerebral edema and functional deficits in a C5a-dependent manner.

C5aR1+ microglia induced A1 polarization of astrocytes not only through cytokines TNF-α, IL-1α, but also via the LGALS9-CD44 pathway. In hemorrhagic injury models, this pathway exacerbated pathology, suggesting etiology-specific roles for this pathway in astrocyte responses. Neutrophils recruitment was enhanced by C5aR1+ microglia via CCL3-CCR1 and CCL5-CCR1 axes.

The study demonstrates that deletion of C5ar1 in microglia reduced neutrophils infiltration, suggesting the potential of C5aR1 as a therapeutic target in acute cerebral edema. Future studies are needed to define the context-specific roles of C5aR1 across central and peripheral immune compartments and assess the translational potential in clinical trials.
