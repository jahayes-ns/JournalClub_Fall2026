C5aR1+ microglia exacerbate neuroinflammation and cerebral edema in acute brain injury

Zhou, Ma, Feng, Tian, Li, Guo, Shi, Cui, Dong, Hao, He, Xiao, Chen, Wu, Gao, Hu, Zhang, Wang, Zhou, Feng, Xue, Lin, Wang, Gao, Heng, Zhao, Luo, Tian, Zhang, Guo, Li, Liu, Ge, Liu, Qu | 2025

Final Summary

The study by Zhou et al. published in Neuron investigates the role of a pro-inflammatory C5a receptor 1 (C5aR1)+ microglial subtype in acute cerebral edema following traumatic brain injury (TBI) and intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). The research team, composed of multiple Chinese authors, utilized single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on human donor samples to identify this microglial subtype as a significant contributor to poorer clinical outcomes in acute brain injury.

In both human patients and mice, the C5aR1+ microglia subtype is abundant following TBI and IS, with higher levels correlating with worse long-term outcomes. The ligand for C5aR1, C5a, was found to be elevated in the plasma of TBI and IS patients, as well as in cerebral edema tissue in mice, with hepatocytes identified as a major source and local complement playing a role in regulating injury-site immunity.

Conditional knockout (KO) mice exhibited reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and cerebral edema following TBI and IS when C5aR1 was selectively deleted in CX3CR1-positive cells (microglia/macrophages). Furthermore, inhibiting the C5a-C5aR1 signaling axis with avacopan, an FDA-approved drug for certain autoimmune diseases, significantly reduced neuroinflammation, restored microglial morphology, and improved functional recovery after TBI and IS. Avacopan decreased cerebral edema in TBI and ICH mice, enhanced BBB integrity, increased tight-junction proteins expression, and improved neurological outcomes, suggesting a neuroprotective role for Avacopan in TBI and ICH.

In conclusion, the study identifies a pro-inflammatory microglial subtype, characterized by C5aR1 expression, as significantly expanded in the injured human brain, exacerbating neuroinflammation, promoting neurotoxic astrocyte polarization, enhancing neutrophils recruitment, and contributing to cerebral edema and neurological deficits. Pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 in preclinical TBI and ICH models ameliorated edema, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target for these conditions. The study's limitations include small sample sizes, sex bias, and unclear mechanisms driving C5aR1 upregulation after TBI and ICH.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The study by Zhou et al., published in Neuron, identifies a pro-inflammatory C5aR1+ microglial subtype enriched in human cerebral edema tissues following traumatic brain injury (TBI) and intra-cerebral hemorrhage (ICH). This subtype exacerbates neuroinflammation, promotes neurotoxic astrocyte polarization via LGALS9-CD44, and recruits neutrophils via CCL3/CCL5. Pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 reduces cerebral edema, suggesting a potential therapeutic target for these conditions. The research was conducted by multiple authors from various departments in China and was co-correspondented by H.L., S.G., Q.L., and Y.Q.

INTRODUCTION

The paper focuses on acute cerebral edema, a significant contributor to mortality, long-term disability, and neurodegeneration following stroke and traumatic brain injury (TBI). Current treatments primarily focus on patient stabilization and symptom relief, but their efficacy in reducing cerebral edema is limited. The study suggests that pro-inflammatory responses are key contributors to the progression of cerebral edema and neurodegeneration. However, systemic anti-inflammatory therapy has failed to reduce cerebral edema and has increased mortality.

The paper performs single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) on 213,484 viable central nervous system cells from 29 human donors, including TBI, intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), and control subjects. The analysis identifies a pro-inflammatory C5a receptor 1 (C5aR1)+ microglial subtype that is associated with poorer clinical outcomes in acute brain injury. In mouse models, genetic deletion of C5ar1 or pharmacological inhibition reduces cerebral edema after TBI and ICH. The study suggests that C5aR1 could be a potential therapeutic target in TBI and ICH.

RESULTS

The research paper focuses on the role of microglial C5a-C5aR1 signaling in neuroinflammation and cerebral edema following Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI) and Ischemic Stroke (IS).

Key findings include: 1. A distinct subset of microglia, characterized by upregulation of C5a receptor 1 (C5aR1), is abundant in both human patients and mice following TBI and IS, with higher levels correlating with worse long-term outcomes. 2. The ligand for C5aR1, C5a, was found to be elevated in the plasma of TBI and IS patients, as well as in cerebral edema tissue in mice. Hepatocytes were identified as a major source of C5a, while local complement also plays a role in regulating injury-site immunity. 3. Conditional knockout (KO) mice exhibited reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and cerebral edema following TBI and IS when C5aR1 was selectively deleted in CX3CR1-positive cells (microglia/macrophages). 4. C5a-C5aR1 signaling promotes pro-inflammatory factor production via NF-κB, AKT, and ERK pathways in microglia.

The study also investigated the potential therapeutic effects of targeting C5a-C5aR1 signaling, demonstrating that inhibiting this axis with avacopan, an FDA-approved drug for certain autoimmune diseases, significantly reduces neuroinflammation, restores microglial morphology, and improves functional recovery after TBI and IS. Avacopan decreased cerebral edema in TBI and ICH mice, enhanced BBB integrity, increased tight-junction proteins expression, and improved neurological outcomes, suggesting a neuroprotective role for Avacopan in TBI and ICH.

DISCUSSION

In this study, a pro-inflammatory microglial subtype, characterized by C5aR1 expression, was identified as significantly expanded in the injured human brain. These C5aR1+ microglia exacerbate neuroinflammation, promote neurotoxic astrocyte polarization, and enhance neutrophils recruitment, contributing to cerebral edema and neurological deficits. Pharmacological inhibition of C5aR1 in preclinical TBI and ICH models ameliorated edema, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target.

The study profiled over 200,000 cells from human cortex and edematous tissue, identifying five major microglial subtypes. Among them, an inflammatory subtype with strong pro-inflammatory signatures was markedly increased in TBI and ICH. The authors found that C5aR1 activation in microglia promotes pro-inflammatory signaling via NF-κB, ERK, and AKT pathways, amplifying cerebral edema and functional deficits in a C5a-dependent manner.

Activated microglia promote neurotoxic A1 astrocyte polarization via TNF-α, IL-1α, and C1q. In this study, C5aR1+ microglia induced A1 polarization not only through these cytokines but also via the LGALS9-CD44 pathway. Neutrophils rapidly mobilize after brain injury and are shown to infiltrate due to CCL3-CCR1 and CCL5-CCR1 axes mediated by microglial C5aR1.

The findings suggest a glial-immune crosstalk axis centered on microglial C5aR1 that mediates inflammatory cascades, leading to cerebral edema in TBI and ICH. Given the existence of clinically approved C5aR1 antagonists for other diseases (e.g., avacopan), these findings are consistent with the potential of C5aR1 as a therapeutic target in acute cerebral edema. The study has limitations, including small sample sizes, sex bias, and unclear mechanisms driving C5aR1 upregulation after TBI and ICH.
