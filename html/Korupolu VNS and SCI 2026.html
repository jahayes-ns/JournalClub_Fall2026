Final Summary

This scientific paper titled "Safety and feasibility of paired vagus nerve stimulation with rehabilitation for improving upper extremity function in people with cervical spinal cord injury: a pilot randomized controlled trial" aimed to evaluate the safety, feasibility, and therapeutic potential of pairing invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI). The study was a single-site, triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot trial that enrolled six adults with traumatic incomplete cervical SCI.

Participants underwent implantation of the Vivistim® System and were randomized to receive either active VNS or sham VNS paired with rehabilitation. The study demonstrated that the intervention was safe, well-tolerated, and feasible in SCI patients. At immediate post-intervention assessment, all participants receiving active VNS showed improvement in upper extremity motor function compared with baseline, whereas none of the participants in the control group showed improvement. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously due to the small sample size and exploratory nature of the analysis.

The study also found that transportation and access barriers affected recruitment and limited completion of in-clinic sessions, highlighting the need for more accessible and flexible rehabilitation delivery models. The primary contribution of this study remains the demonstration of safety, tolerability, and feasibility, with exploratory clinical outcomes suggesting potential benefits warranting further investigation. Larger trials with refined inclusion criteria, home-based therapy support, extended or intensive rehabilitation, quantitative movement assessments, and hybrid in-clinic/tele-rehabilitation models are essential to determine efficacy, durability, and optimal implementation of VNS paired with rehabilitation in spinal cord injury.

Section Summaries

PREAMBLE

The paper titled "Safety and feasibility of paired vagus nerve stimulation with rehabilitation for improving upper extremity function in people with cervical spinal cord injury: a pilot randomized controlled trial" was published in Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences. The study, led by Radha Korupolu and Nuray Yozbatiran, aimed to evaluate the safety and feasibility of pairing vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury.

The single-site, triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot trial enrolled adults with chronic traumatic incomplete cervical SCI and had them undergo implantation of a VNS device. Participants were then randomized 1:1 to receive active VNS paired with rehabilitation or sham VNS paired with rehabilitation. A total of six participants completed the study through 90-day follow-up.

The results indicated that no serious adverse events, unanticipated adverse device effects, or unresolved surgery-related complications were observed. In-clinic therapy adherence was high, supporting the feasibility of the intervention. At the immediate post-intervention, all participants receiving active VNS demonstrated mild-to-moderate improvements in upper extremity motor function, whereas no improvements were observed in the sham group.

While clinical improvements were exploratory, positive participant-reported experiences and observed patterns of improvement support further investigation in larger trials designed to rigorously assess effectiveness, durability, and optimal dosing parameters. The trial registration number is NCT05601661.

INTRODUCTION

The introduction of this scientific paper discusses the global incidence of traumatic spinal cord injuries (SCIs), with cervical SCI accounting for over half of new cases, leading to tetraplegia and profound disability. Regaining arm/hand function is a top priority for individuals with tetra-plegia as it significantly improves their independence and quality of life. Various approaches are used to improve upper extremity function after SCI, but recovery is often challenging.

The paper focuses on vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) paired with rehabilitation as a promising intervention for promoting neuroplasticity, which is critical for functional recovery following neurological injury. VNS facilitates the release of neuromodulators that promote cortical plasticity and motor recovery. The effects of VNS paired with rehabilitation have been extensively studied in animal models, demonstrating enhanced cortical plasticity and improved upper extremity recovery compared to identical rehabilitation without VNS.

The study described aims to evaluate the safety, feasibility, and therapeutic potential of fully implanted invasive VNS (Vivistim®) paired with rehabilitation in individuals with cervical SCI. Although VNS is FDA-approved for stroke rehabilitation, evidence in the SCI population remains limited, making this study critical in addressing an unmet need. The study conducts a two-arm sham-controlled randomized controlled trial (RCT) to assess recruitment and retention feasibility, evaluate safety, and explore the preliminary effects of pairing active invasive VNS with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with cervical SCI.

METHODS

This study is a single-site, triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot trial evaluating the feasibility, safety, and preliminary efficacy of invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) paired with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI). Six adults with traumatic incomplete cervical SCI were enrolled and implanted with the Vivistim® System. Participants were randomized to receive either active VNS or sham VNS paired with rehabilitation.

The study involved recruitment, screening, baseline assessments, device implantation, randomization, blinding, intervention, control group crossover, and outcome measures. Participants underwent in-clinic upper-extremity rehabilitation sessions three times a week for 90 minutes each over 6-8 weeks, followed by a 90-day home-based rehabilitation program.

The primary objectives were to assess feasibility and safety, with preliminary efficacy assessed using validated measures of upper extremity motor function, activities of daily living, and quality of life. The primary clinical outcome was the Graded Redefined Assessment of Strength, Sensibility, and Prehension (GRASSP). Analyses were primarily descriptive due to the pilot nature of the study.

RESULTS

The study involved six participants with chronic traumatic incomplete cervical SCI who were implanted with the Vivistim® System. All participants completed the study procedures through a 90-day follow-up, and no serious adverse events or unanticipated complications were observed during the study period.

Four out of six participants completed all 18 in-clinic rehabilitation sessions, while adherence to the 90-day home exercise program varied across participants, ranging from 25% to 95%.

At immediate post-intervention assessment, all three participants randomized to the active VNS group demonstrated improvement in total GRASSP scores compared with baseline, whereas none of the participants in the control group showed improvement. At the 90-day follow-up, two out of three participants in the active VNS group sustained improvements in GRASSP scores relative to baseline, while one participant showed attenuation of initial gains.

All participants reported positive satisfaction with their assigned study treatment and protocol, with overall treatment satisfaction being high. Perceived changes in overall health and well-being were favorable, and tolerance of treatment-related side effects was also rated highly.

VNS therapy-related adverse events were mild and transient, with four participants reporting a brief cough during stimulation. No episodes of autonomic dysreflexia, persistent pain, or worsening spasticity attributable to VNS were observed. One participant in the control group reported bladder spasms during therapy, which were attributed to ongoing urologic issues unrelated to stimulation and resolved following suprapubic catheter exchange.

DISCUSSION

This triple-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot study aimed to evaluate the safety and feasibility of pairing invasive Vagus Nerve Stimulation (VNS) with upper extremity rehabilitation in individuals with chronic cervical Spinal Cord Injury (SCI). The primary objectives were to establish procedural safety and feasibility, with exploratory clinical outcomes to inform future trials.

The study demonstrated that implantation of the FDA-approved Vivistim® VNS system and its integration with intensive rehabilitation is safe, well-tolerated, and feasible in SCI patients. No serious adverse events or unresolved surgery-related complications were observed through 90 days of follow-up. Both the implantation procedure and subsequent in-clinic and home-based therapy phases were tolerated without significant autonomic, respiratory, or laryngeal complications.

The study found that all participants receiving active VNS demonstrated mild-to-moderate improvements in Graded Redefined Assessment of Strength, Sensibility and Prehension (GRASSP) scores immediately after completing in-clinic therapy, whereas no participants in the sham group showed post-intervention improvement. At the 90-day follow-up, two of the three participants in the active group achieved changes consistent with clinically meaningful improvement in GRASSP scores compared to none in controls. However, these findings should be interpreted cautiously due to the small sample size and exploratory nature of the analysis.

Transportation and access barriers emerged as significant challenges, affecting recruitment and limiting completion of in-clinic sessions. These observations underscore the need for more accessible and flexible rehabilitation delivery models. The primary contribution of this study remains the demonstration of safety, tolerability, and feasibility, with exploratory clinical outcomes suggesting a pattern of improvement in the active VNS group.

CONCLUSION

In a pilot randomized controlled trial, combining invasive vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) with upper extremity rehabilitation for individuals with chronic cervical spinal cord injury proved safe, feasible, and well-tolerated. The intervention demonstrated high adherence to in-clinic therapy and no serious or unanticipated adverse events, making it practical for a clinical rehabilitation setting. Although improvements were mild to moderate in a small participant group, consistent post-intervention gains in the active VNS group suggest potential benefits warranting further investigation. The findings extend the safety profile of invasive VNS to the SCI population and provide foundational human data for future studies. Larger trials with refined inclusion criteria, home-based therapy support, extended or intensive rehabilitation, quantitative movement assessments, and hybrid in-clinic/tele-rehabilitation models are essential to determine efficacy, durability, and optimal implementation of VNS paired with rehabilitation in spinal cord injury.
